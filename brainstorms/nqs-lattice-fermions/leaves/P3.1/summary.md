* **The question in plain terms**: A variational state $\psi$ can have an excellent energy while its long-range pair, spin or charge correlation $a_\psi=\langle\hat A\rangle_\psi$ is wrong. The leaf asks whether $\delta=a_0-a_\psi$ can be estimated, or honestly refused, from cheap information at cost polynomial in $V$. On the $4\times4$ instance, converged width-6 projected NNBF states have relative tail errors of 31% (pair), 15% (spin), 22% (charge) at squared overlap 0.94 (451837).
* **Why the obvious tools fail**: With $\psi=\alpha\psi_0+\sum_nc_n\lvert n\rangle$, $\delta\approx-2\alpha\sum_nc_nA_{n0}$ depends on the signs of the $c_n$, every energy moment only on $\lvert c_n\rvert^2$ (S1), and a degree-$K$ filter changes a component by at most $2K^2\omega_n/W$ (S2). On the real state 83–141% of $\delta$ sits below $1t$, against 31% of the energy error (452272). This explains the kills of variance extrapolation (R1.1), moment regressors (R3.1, R3.7, R5.3, R6.3), shared-bias ensembles (R2.3, R6.4, R7.7, R9.4) and tangent-space estimates, which vanish at a converged minimum (R3.4, R3.6, R7.4, R7.5, R8.2). S1 and S2 stand.
