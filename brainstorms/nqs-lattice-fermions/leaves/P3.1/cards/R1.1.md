# R1.1 — Lanczos-step variance extrapolation of the correlation function (Route 1) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Apply zero, one and two statistical Lanczos steps to $\psi$ and extrapolate $C(r)$ linearly in the energy variance to zero variance; the extrapolated value minus $a_\psi$ is the error bar. It should work because an exact eigenstate has zero variance.
* **Lineage**: initial batch (Round 1). Its mechanism recurs in R2.3, R3.7 and R6.1.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-10)
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0 (is it known, and is its adoption barrier fixed?) and Gate 3 (does it survive the Hard Core of near-degenerate competing orders?).
* **Evidence**: It is the published Sorella–Becca scheme (arXiv:cond-mat/0009149; arXiv:cond-mat/0201388). Derivation: in the two-level model $\psi=\cos\varphi\,\psi_0+\sin\varphi\,\psi_1$ the Krylov vector $(\hat H-E)\psi$ has weight $\propto\Delta\sin\varphi$ in the near-degenerate plane. Later measured on the real $4\times4$ state (Round 5): one and two steps cut $\Delta E$ from 0.052 to 0.023 and 0.015 while the pair error moved from $2.9$ to $2.6$ and $2.8\times10^{-5}$.
* **Why this kills it**: The route proposes nothing against the known barriers (cost $N_{\mathrm{conn}}^p$ for $p$ steps, blindness to low-energy admixtures). Variance falls while the observable stays, so the extrapolation certifies the current value.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: High-degree filters with $K\gtrsim\sqrt{W/\Omega}$ (Proposition S2) and rational (shift-inverted) filters, which later routes (R10.1d, R10.4) use.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: On states whose error is mostly high-energy junk (barely trained states) it works; the Round 3 smoke showed L1 tracking there. Answered: converged projected states carry low-energy error (Round 7: $F_\delta(1t)=0.83$–1.41 against $F_E(1t)=0.31$).
* **Checked by**: per-leaf critic, Round 2 (2026-11-11); Saturation Diagnostic S1 and S2 (Round 6).
* **Lesson**: L3 (`library.md`)
