* **Question**: Can a translation-equivariant fermionic NQS trained at $L$ initialize (or directly evaluate) $2L$ with an energy error per site that is already close to converged, so that finite-size scaling needs a fraction of the per-size retraining cost?
* **Answer**: No, for the size-independent networks we built, going from 8 to 16 sites with open boundaries. Trained directly on the 16-site cluster, such a network stalls at about 5.7% energy error, and this is not an optimiser artefact; a network with separate parameters for each site reaches 0.56%. So copying from 8 sites could at best speed the approach to a 5.7% plateau. On a periodic 16-site cluster, where such a network reached about 2% when trained from scratch, size transfer itself was not tried: that test was judged predictable.
* **What we showed**: Every size-independent network we built, whose parameters can be copied unchanged to a larger cluster, stalled at about 5.7–6.0% energy error when trained directly on the 16-site open cluster, while a network with separate parameters per site reached 0.56%, so a copy could at best speed the approach to that plateau. Better optimisation did not move the plateau: one exact step of the strongest local optimiser gained at most 5% of the remaining error on 16 sites, and on 12 sites a careful polish of the best variant removed 2.8% of its error. A copy from 8 sites also started worse than an untrained start on a periodic 16-site cluster. None of the size-independent ingredients we added closed the gap.
* **What it would take**: A size-independent design that reaches about 1% or better when trained on the 16-site open cluster itself; none is known. On periodic clusters, where such a network reaches about 2%, the direct transfer test (about 10 CPU-hours) would settle it but was predicted to fail. Sampled training at larger sizes was not attempted.
* **Conclusion**: On open-boundary $t$–$t'$–$J$ clusters at exact-diagonalisation size, no size-independent fermionic NQS family we built gets below about 5.7% relative energy error on the $4\times4$ target, and no optimiser artefact was found: one exact linear-method step gains at most 5% at the loosest cut tested ($10^{-10}$), with the gain still rising with the cut, not converged (L29) on $4\times4$, and on $4\times3$ a cutoff-ladder polish of the best arm stays at 1.028%, 0.008 percentage points above the 1.02% line (one arm, marginal). A copy of such a family cannot beat the family trained at the target, so transfer could at best save time on the way to a 5.7% plateau, while a site-indexed network trained at the target reaches 0.56%. On a $4\times4$ torus a smaller size-independent family reached at most 1.96–2.13% from scratch, but the torus transfer test was refused by the compute gate as predictable, so the periodic case is closed at this budget, not answered.

**The question.** A fermionic neural quantum state (NQS) needs an antisymmetric layer (determinant or pair-function "geminal") whose matrices change shape with size, so finite-size scaling means retraining at every size. P4.2 asks whether a network trained on a small cluster can be copied to one twice as large and start near its converged energy. The comparison is the Minimal Contrast Foil: Jastrow times projected BCS, refitted at the target.

**The mechanism.** MFNG-O (momentum-field neural geminal, open-boundary version, 426 parameters): its pair function $\kappa(\mathbf r_i-\mathbf r_j)$ is generated by a small network on a fixed Brillouin-zone grid, dressed by backflow and Jastrow depending only on local hole counts; every parameter is size independent, so transfer is an exact copy; the state is irrep-projected and exactly $S=0$.

**Chain of evidence.**
1. *A copied fit is arbitrary.* 106–112 of 122 directions are unconstrained by 8-site sources; zero-shot on the $4\times4$ torus the copy had 0.663 t/site error against 0.285 untrained, and stayed behind scratch after 20 and 50 stochastic-reconfiguration (SR) steps (R3.1; 410960, 451914).
2. *Capacity decides.* MFNG-O from scratch at the $4\times4$ open target plateaus at 5.97% (426 parameters, 452196) and 5.71% (818, 604157); site-indexed projected NNBF reaches 0.56% (410988). Above 3% the transfer array was not run (R5.1, R5.3).
3. *One LM step barely moves it.* The linear method (LM) gains at most 0.17% of the remaining error at the pre-registered cut and at most 5% at $10^{-10}$, still rising (659969, cutoff-unconverged; L29). Effective tangent rank 34 of 426 and 48 of 818.
4. *No size-independent ingredient is the lever* on $4\times3$ (≤1.10×, R5.4); a free site-resolved pair matrix gives 2.41× but has no size map, and its stand-ins fail (R5.5, R9.1, R9.2).
5. *Longer optimisation does not reach the 2× lever*: 3000-step SR runs reached 1.294% (base) and 1.057% (enlarged), above the 1.02% confirm line (704844).
6. *Polishing does not either.* The best arm (c8h32), LM-polished (731382), was frozen at a fixed relative cutoff; a post-hoc ladder (16 steps vs the registered 8) lowered it to 1.028%: HOLDS, marginally (CHECK rule UNCLEAR). The other two arms need about a 20% drop to reach AMBIGUOUS; not run (G2).

Comparisons used the same instance, start, exact sums and pre-registered thresholds; transfer was charged its source cost.

**Why the alternatives fail.** Orbital transfer, gluing, twist folding and multi-size pretraining are prior art or carry nothing new (R2, R3, R4, R3.3); seed probing and null filtering fail (R2.3, R5.b); static head starts give a speed-up of about 1 (R5.2, R9.4, R9.6); global views are published or vanish per site (R5.6, R9.3).
