* **Question**: Can the total-spin sector $S$ be imposed on a determinant- or Pfaffian-based NQS exactly, at cost below the $\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{grid}})$ spin-projection quadrature, without sacrificing backflow expressivity?
* **Answer**: Partly. The cost half is settled: exact total spin is cheap when the backflow reads only where the charges are, and has no known cheap method for size-consistent networks reading the spin pattern (low-order spin dressings project cheaply but do not scale with system size). Whether that restriction costs expressivity is not settled: it fitted a 12-site ground state about 2 times worse at equal effort (unconverged, one start), yet gave half the energy error at equal computer time (at 3 CPU-h per run, one core) on 24 sites.
* **What we showed**: Cost: a richer charge-only network with a pairing term costs 1.3 to 3.6 times the simplest exactly projected one at 12 to 48 sites; both avoid the sum over spin arrangements that the spin-reading network needs. Practice: on a 24-site strip (20 electrons, two seeds, one optimiser tuned on the charge-only network, at 3 CPU-h per run, one core), the charge-only network's energy error was 0.49–0.56 of the spin-reading network's, with or without spin-flip symmetry imposed, and the spin-reading network's total spin squared stayed 0.6–1.2 instead of 0. Capacity: fitted to the exact 12-site ground state from one random start for 2,000 exact-gradient steps, the charge-only network ended 2.2 times further from it than the exactly projected spin-reading one (one minus squared overlap $2.9\times10^{-4}$ against $1.3\times10^{-4}$; 1.6 times at 500 steps); the charge-only arm met the plateau rule (9.0% last-quarter drop); the spin-reading network was still improving. Bases built to have exact total spin give it at polynomial cost but were 4 to 53 times less accurate.
* **What it would take**: Whether the 12-site loss persists at 24 sites is untested, and each affordable test was refused because its outcome was predictable. Fitting both networks to a near-exact 24-site state (about 26 CPU-hours after a 7.7 CPU-hour 12-site rehearsal) cannot show the loss: there the spin-reading network cannot be projected, and unprojected it loses mostly on spin contamination (40–70% of its error) early in fitting (100–400 of a planned 4,000 steps), while projected it fitted about 1.8 times better (read at 100 steps only); a pass needed the unprojected network to go from 5.6 times worse than the charge-only one to 1.65 times better, and failure and refusal both close the question. A converged 12-site comparison of both networks exactly projected (11 CPU-hours) would not carry to larger sizes; two smaller refused steps (5.4 and about 2.5–3 CPU-hours, the latter a post-hoc estimate) were predicted to change nothing. Settling it needs a cheap way to give the spin-reading network exact spin at 24 sites (or a spin penalty shown to match projection), a sampled optimiser that converges at 24 sites, or a converged 24-site comparison, whose cost is unestimated because no sampled optimiser converges there (in the companion optimisation study the samples needed grew like the number of occupied configurations from 10 to 12 sites).
* **Conclusion**: For networks whose spin dependence enters as factors diagonal in the up/down occupation basis, exact $S$ is cheap only when every spin-breaking ingredient sits inside a Gaussian per charge configuration (commutant lemma plus Lemma B). Within that class `che` beats unprojected `nnbf` ($\rho$ 0.51–0.56) and its spin-flip-even form ($\rho'$ 0.49–0.55) at equal compute on 24 sites. No cheap exact-$S$ method is known for size-consistent spin-reading networks; low-order spin dressings (R2.1, R5.2) project cheaply but are not size-consistent. At 12 sites the restriction costs fit accuracy at equal effort, with a gap that widens with budget ($\kappa=(1-F)_{\rm che}/(1-F)_{\rm nnbf}$ 1.64 → 2.23 from 500 to 2000 evaluations; R45, seed 0; `che` met the plateau rule, 9.0% last-quarter drop, `nnbf` still improving). The 24-site capacity question is open: its one-sided test (R47) lacks power (L68); a converged 24-site comparison is uncosted (no converging sampled optimiser; P5.1's R51.8).

**The question in plain terms.** Neural quantum states (NQS) for the doped Hubbard model build a determinant or Pfaffian whose orbitals depend on the configuration through a neural backflow (NNBF). Exact total spin $S$ helps accuracy and is needed for sector-resolved physics. Quadrature projection over $N_{\mathrm{grid}}$ rotations is cheap only if every correlator reads charges alone: can exact $S$ be had at polynomial cost without giving up spin information?

**Mechanism.** Commutant lemma: a factor diagonal in the up/down basis commutes with spin rotations only if it reads the charge configuration $c$ alone, so projecting a spin-reading network sums $\binom{N_s}{N_s/2}$ spin arrangements of $c$ ($N_s$ singly occupied sites); no lower bound over all algorithms is claimed. Lemma B: $K$ spin-conserving Gaussians per $c$ project at $\mathcal O(KN^3)$ (Che, arXiv:2608.22925).
