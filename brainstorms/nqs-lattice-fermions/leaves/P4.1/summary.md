* **Question**: Can the total-spin sector $S$ be imposed on a determinant- or Pfaffian-based NQS exactly, at cost below the $\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{grid}})$ spin-projection quadrature, without sacrificing backflow expressivity?
* **Answer**: Partly. Exact total spin is cheap if the backflow network reads only where the charges are, and exponential, with no cheaper method known, if a network built from factors that each read a configuration's up/down spin pattern does; in one test (a 24-site strip at one filling, two seeds, the same optimiser, tuned on the charge-only network, at 3 CPU-h per run, one core), the charge-only network ended with about half the energy error (0.49 to 0.56 of it) of the same network reading spins, with or without spin-flip symmetry imposed; even the symmetrised version still had total spin squared 0.6 to 0.7 where the ground state's is expected to be 0. On 12 sites, from one random start and at equal fitting effort (one seed; its spread is unmeasured), the charge-only network ended 2.2 times further from the exact ground state than the exactly projected spin-reading network, and the gap widened with effort (1.6 to 2.2 between 500 and 2000 steps); neither had converged.
* **What we showed**: Cost: the charge-only form is 1.3 to 3.6 times the simplest exactly projected version at 12 to 48 sites. Practice: on a 24-site strip (20 electrons, two seeds, equal CPU time), the charge-only network's energy error was 0.49–0.56 of the spin-reading network's, with or without spin-flip symmetry, whose total spin squared stayed 0.6–1.2 instead of 0. Bases built to have exact total spin give it at polynomial cost but were 4 to 53 times less accurate. Capacity, 12 sites, one random start, 2,000 exact-gradient fitting steps each: one minus the squared overlap with the exact ground state was $2.9\times10^{-4}$ for the charge-only network against $1.3\times10^{-4}$ for the exactly projected spin-reading one.
* **What it would take**: Whether the 12-site loss persists at 24 sites is untested, and each affordable test was refused because its outcome was predictable: fitting both networks to a near-exact 24-site state (about 26 CPU-hours after a 7.7 CPU-hour 12-site rehearsal) cannot show the loss, because there the spin-reading network cannot be projected and, unprojected, it loses on spin contamination alone (at 12 sites it fitted 1.4 to 5.6 times worse than the charge-only one, while its projected version fitted 1.8 times better); a converged 12-site comparison (11 CPU-hours) would not carry to larger sizes. Settling it needs a cheap way to give the spin-reading network exact spin at 24 sites (or a spin penalty shown to match projection), a sampled optimiser that converges at 24 sites, or a budget for a converged 24-site comparison.
* **Conclusion**: For networks whose spin dependence enters as factors diagonal in the up/down occupation basis, exact $S$ is cheap only when every spin-breaking ingredient sits inside a Gaussian per charge configuration (commutant lemma plus Lemma B). Within that class `che` beats unprojected `nnbf` ($\rho$ 0.51–0.56) and its spin-flip-even form ($\rho'$ 0.49–0.55) at equal compute on 24 sites. **The question in plain terms.** Neural quantum states (NQS) for the doped Hubbard model build a determinant or Pfaffian whose orbitals depend on the configuration through a neural backflow (NNBF). Exact total spin $S$ helps accuracy and is needed for sector-resolved physics. Quadrature projection over $N_{\mathrm{grid}}$ rotations is cheap only if every correlator reads charges alone: can exact $S$ be had at polynomial cost without giving up spin information?

**Mechanism.** Commutant lemma: a factor diagonal in the up/down basis commutes with spin rotations only if it reads the charge configuration $c$ alone, so projecting a spin-reading network sums $\binom{N_s}{N_s/2}$ spin arrangements of $c$ ($N_s$ singly occupied sites); no lower bound over all algorithms is claimed. Lemma B: $K$ spin-conserving Gaussians per $c$ project at $\mathcal O(KN^3)$ (Che, arXiv:2608.22925).
