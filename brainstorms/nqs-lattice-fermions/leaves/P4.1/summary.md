* **Question**: Can the total-spin sector $S$ be imposed on a determinant- or Pfaffian-based NQS exactly, at cost below the $\mathcal{O}(N_{\mathrm{grid}})$ spin-projection quadrature, without sacrificing backflow expressivity?
* **Answer**: Partly. Exact total spin stays cheap (1.3 to 3.6 times the cost of the simplest exactly projected version at 12 to 48 sites) if the backflow network reads only where the charges are, and we proved that letting it read the spins makes exact projection exponentially costly in the worst case. Whether the charge-only network gives up accuracy is not known: its best size-independent form leads by too little, on one small cluster, to rule out an optimiser effect.
* **What we showed**: On a 12-site cluster a sum of 8 charge-only components beat an exactly projected network that reads spins by 35%, but a fixed number of components can only gain a bounded total energy, so that gain must fade per site. The size-independent form, a single pairing wave function whose orbitals follow the charges, was about 24% more accurate than the best comparable charge-only form after extra exact optimisation of both, but one more optimisation step showed that neither had finished optimising. Cutting its pairing down to an ordinary up-times-down form made it more than five times worse, so the trained state does use pairing.
* **What it would take**: Fully optimising both 12-site states and a matched version without pairing (running now, about 3 CPU-hours), then the same comparison at 24 sites with an optimiser that works on samples, which does not yet exist; a candidate is being prepared.
* **Conclusion**: Exact $S$ is cheap only when every spin-breaking ingredient sits inside a Gaussian per charge configuration (commutant lemma plus Lemma B). Within that class no route has yet shown a gain that is not confounded by the optimiser: SR-trained references sat about half their error above what their parameters reach, and the fixed-cutoff linear-method polish that exposed this was itself frozen (R19). **The question in plain terms.** Neural quantum states (NQS) for the doped Hubbard model build a determinant or Pfaffian whose orbitals depend on the configuration through a neural backflow (NNBF). The ground state has a definite total spin $S$; imposing it exactly helps accuracy and is needed for sector-resolved physics. The standard tool, spin projection by quadrature over $N_{\mathrm{grid}}$ rotations, is cheap only when every correlator reads charges alone. Can exact $S$ be had at polynomial cost without giving up the spin information that makes spin-resolved networks accurate?

**Mechanism and boundary.** A commutant lemma: a diagonal factor commutes with spin rotations only if it depends on the charge configuration $c$ alone, so projecting a spin-reading network sums over all $\binom{N_s}{N_s/2}$ spin arrangements of $c$ ($N_s$ singly occupied sites), #P-hard in the worst case. Lemma B: if the state at each $c$ is a sum of $K$ spin-conserving Gaussians, exact projection costs $K$ closed-form evaluations, $\mathcal O(KN^3)$ (Che, arXiv:2608.22925). Ten routes bringing spin information in otherwise died at Gates 0–1 (R0.2, R2.4, R3.2, R4.3, R5.1–R5.3, R5.7, R10, R13). R22 escapes the lemma differently: a balanced spin-coupling-tree basis is exact by construction, and its Hamiltonian stays sparse ($\mathcal O(L^{2.1})$ connected states measured up to $L=1024$).
