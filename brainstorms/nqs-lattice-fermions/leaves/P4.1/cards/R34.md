# R34 — importance-reweighted sample reuse across steps (`reuse`) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Raise the samples per step without drawing new ones: keep the last $K$ steps' configurations, reweight them by $\lvert\psi_{\mathrm{new}}/\psi_{\mathrm{old}}\rvert^2$ (correlated sampling), and accumulate both $S$ and $f$ over them. One-step evidence says the force needs more samples, and reuse supplies them if drawing a configuration is a large part of its cost.
* **Lineage**: Round 22 sub-question B, coordinator's list (correlated sampling and sample reuse); T1 bound PASS in round 22 (plain 4N, i.e. perfect zero-staleness reuse of 4 steps, Rbar 0.095 ± 0.012 against 0.016 at N); reopening-ledger row 1; killed in round 24.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G4 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: written in round 22 before the measurement (card and ledger): reuse saves only the Markov-chain cost per configuration, so its speed-up per step is at most (sampling cost + row cost)/(row cost); "below 2 the route dies at G4".
* **Evidence**: 24-site open $8\times3$ strip, $N_e=20$, 1024 chains, per SR step (seconds, sampling / local energy / log-derivatives). Production job 699824 (4 threads, median over 34–61 logged steps): `che` 0.27 / 3.12 / 0.31, ratio 1.08; `pcg` 0.79 / 9.42 / 0.66, ratio 1.08. Fresh 1-thread timing this round (`retime.py`, 3 steps, 2.0 CPU-min): `che` 0.45 / 6.61 / 0.79, ratio 1.06; `pcg` 0.97 / 17.56 / 1.09, ratio 1.05. Even counting only the log-derivatives as the row (ignoring the local energy) gives 1.57–2.20, and the sampling time also contains the amplitude recomputation that reweighting itself needs.
* **Why this kills it**: a reused configuration still needs its local energy and log-derivatives at the new parameters (the force is $\mathrm{cov}(O,E_{\mathrm{loc}})$, both functions of $\theta$), and the local energy, one amplitude per Hamiltonian-connected configuration (tens per configuration) against 10 Metropolis proposals, is 85–90% of the step. Reuse therefore buys at most 5–8% more samples per CPU-second, far below the factor 2 required, while the round-22 bound needed about 4 times the samples to matter.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: samplers with long autocorrelation (many more sweeps per step), where drawing dominates; reuse of only the metric rows (already killed as R35); a cheaper local energy (e.g. a fast-update ratio scheme), which would raise the ratio and could reopen the route.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: the ladder's 0.5 sweeps per step may under-decorrelate, so an honest sampler costs more and reuse would save more; unresolved, but more sweeps would raise the cost of fresh samples without making reuse any cheaper per row, so the 4-fold sample increase would still cost about 4 times the row work.
* **Checked by**: Round 24 worker (2026-11-17); per-leaf critic sign-off owed.
* **Lesson**: route-specific: the dominance of the local energy over drawing holds for this code's short Metropolis runs (0.5 sweeps per step), not for samplers with long autocorrelation.
