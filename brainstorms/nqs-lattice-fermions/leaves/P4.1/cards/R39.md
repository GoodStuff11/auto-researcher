# R39 — James–Stein (empirical-Bayes) shrinkage of the sampled SR force (`js-force`) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: The sampled force $f=\mathrm{cov}(O,E_{\mathrm{loc}})$ ($O=\partial_\theta\ln\psi$, $E_{\mathrm{loc}}$ the local energy), not the metric $S$, limits a sampled stochastic-reconfiguration (SR) step: the exact force lifts the one-step gain from 0.016 to 0.171 of the gap (round 22). Estimate $f$ better from the same samples: in the sampled-$S$ eigenbasis, shrink each force component toward zero by its estimated signal-to-noise ratio (mode-wise positive-part James–Stein, J1), or per group of 82 modes (grouped James–Stein, J2). Extra cost: one $N\times P^2$ product.
* **Lineage**: translation repair of R36 (round 24, ledger row 3): R36's covariance shrinkage of the metric died because the noise sits in the force; the repair shrinks the noisy mean vector itself. T0 gates: G0 narrow PASS (SNR cut-offs of force modes exist in time-dependent VMC, Schmitt & Heyl, PRL 125, 100503, 2020; adopted as an optimiser, as R21, R38); G1, G3 PASS (estimator only); G2 not predictable (biased at finite $N$, so L19 does not apply); G4 PASS ($\mathcal O(NP^2)$, or $\mathcal O(N^2P)$ when $P>N$). Whole-vector shrinkage toward zero killed at T0: it only rescales the step, which the fixed-length scoring removes.
* **Source analogy**: Stein's paradox: shrinking a noisy mean vector in three or more dimensions toward a target beats the sample mean in squared error (James & Stein 1961; Efron & Morris, JASA 68, 117, 1973). Mean → $f$; its covariance → $\mathrm{Var}(f)$ from per-sample terms; loss → the step's energy change in $S$-whitened coordinates. Gap (found by the screen): shrinkage pays when the signal is concentrated or near the target; here most force modes have SNR below one.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T1 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: `tests/P4.1_cheap_su2_r25/RULE_E.md`, written before the run: RULE_B protocol (one SR step from `che`'s SR-trained 12-site start, 16,384 i.i.d. draws, 3 seeds, exact-metric lengths 0.01–0.03, scored by the exact energy as gap fraction R, best length per seed, Rbar = mean); PASS-T1 if the better of J1, J2 $\ge$ Rbar(base) + 0.05; KILL if $\le$ Rbar(base) + 0.02 (covers "less than twice the base").
* **Evidence**: open $4\times3$, $5+5$, $U=8$, $t'=-0.25$, round-22 draws (base reproduced): base 0.016 ± 0.022; J1 0.005 ± 0.015; J2 −0.008 ± 0.013; exact force 0.171 ± 0.015. J1 kept a third of the modes, mean factor 0.16. Reported only, on guided draws (R38): base 0.086 ± 0.011, J1 0.062 ± 0.008, J2 0.056 ± 0.012. 4.7 CPU-min (`logs/shrink_screen.txt`).
* **Why this kills it**: both estimators are at or below the plain sampled force, below base + 0.02, so KILL fires. The components the exact step needs are individually buried in noise, so re-weighting them cannot recover the headroom; only more independent information can.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: shrinkage toward an informative non-zero target (the previous force is momentum, killed as SPRING, R15); other starts and sizes ($P>N$ at 24 sites).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: one start, one size, and the shrinkage factors are themselves noisy; unresolved, but J2 pools 82 modes per factor and did no better.
* **Checked by**: Round 24 worker (2026-11-17); per-leaf critic sign-off owed.
* **Lesson**: proposed library entry: shrinking a noisy SR force cannot replace samples when most force modes have SNR below one; with R36 the shrinkage analogy fails for both metric and force.
