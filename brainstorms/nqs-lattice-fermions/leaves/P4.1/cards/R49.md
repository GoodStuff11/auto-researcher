# R49 — angle-dependent Gaussians inside the spin-projection integral (angle-gcm) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Put spin structure into the projection itself: replace $P_S\Phi_c=\int d\Omega\,D^{S*}(\Omega)R(\Omega)\Phi_c$ by $\int d\Omega\,D^{S*}(\Omega)R(\Omega)\Phi_{c,\Omega}$, with a Gaussian whose orbitals depend on $c$ and on the rotation angle, so each quadrature node carries its own correlated reference at the same $\mathcal O(N_{\rm grid}N^3)$ cost.
* **Lineage**: round-30 batch, item (c); cross-field translation from nuclear physics (generator-coordinate mixing, here in the projection angle).
* **Source analogy**: generator-coordinate methods mix projected HFB states along a collective coordinate (Sheikh et al., arXiv:1901.06992); here the coordinate is the rotation angle. Gap: in the source each mixed state is already projected.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G1 (T0, 2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 1: is $\hat S^2$ still exact for every $\theta$?
* **Evidence**: derivation. $f(\Omega)=R(\Omega)\Phi_{c,\Omega}$ is a general vector-valued function on SU(2); $\int D^{S*}f$ lies in spin $S$ only when $f(\Omega)=R(\Omega)\chi$ for one fixed $\chi$. Otherwise the $\Omega$-dependence shifts weight between irreps and the result has components in every $S$. A general angle-dependent weight $a(\Omega)$ is not exact either: it shifts weight between irreps just as $\Phi_{c,\Omega}$ does. The exact sub-cases are $\Phi_{c,\Omega}=\Phi_c$ (`che`), constant weights on a few fixed Gaussians (a $K$-Gaussian sum, R1, killed at G4), and weights in the span of $D^S_{MK'}(\Omega)$ ($K$-mixing), which equals $P_S$ applied to one fixed vector.
* **Why this kills it**: exactness is the leaf's requirement, and the exact sub-cases are existing routes.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: mixing several separately projected Gaussians (R1, RG1: finite-size gain only).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: $K$-mixing (weights $\sum_{K'}g_{K'}D^S_{MK'}(\Omega)$) is exact and angle-dependent. Answered: it reduces to projecting one fixed vector $\sum_{K'}g_{K'}|\Phi_c;K'\rangle$, which is `che` (or an R1 sum) with a different reference; no new class.
* **Checked by**: per-leaf critic, 2026-11-17: upheld; exact sub-case corrected (objection 4).
* **Lesson**: route-specific (a restatement of the projector's definition).
