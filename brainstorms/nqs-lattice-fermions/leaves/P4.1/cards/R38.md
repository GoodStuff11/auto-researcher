# R38 — guided (flattened) sampling distribution for the SR step (`guided`) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: The one-step screen showed that the force $f=\mathrm{cov}(O,E_{\mathrm{loc}})$, not the metric, limits sampled SR here. Near the nodes of a determinant state $O=\partial\ln\psi$ diverges, so under $\lvert\psi\rvert^2$ sampling the force has heavy tails. Draw the step's samples from a flatter mixture, $q=0.8\,p+0.2\,p_{\mathrm{flat}}$ ($p\propto\lvert\psi\rvert^2$, $p_{\mathrm{flat}}\propto\lvert\psi\rvert$), with self-normalised weights $p/q$: same cost per sample, bounded weights, more samples where the force estimator is noisy.
* **Lineage**: Round 22 Operation B (active probing: change where the estimator samples, not how it solves), after the class bounds killed every metric-side route (R32, R35, R36) and the force control variate (R37). Prior art: guiding functions for infinite-variance forces in real-space QMC (Attaccalite & Sorella, PRL 100, 114501, 2008; Pathak & Wagner, AIP Adv. 10, 085213, 2020) and overdispersed $\lvert\psi\rvert^\alpha$ sampling for NQS (arXiv:2507.05352, with NetKet code). G0: known and recently published, so it is adopted as the leaf's optimiser, not claimed as a new method (as R21); G1, G3 PASS; G2 tested below; G4: same per-sample cost at any size; whether the heavy-tail gain persists at 24 sites is the sharpest gap.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T1 (T1b, job 752885, 2026-11-17) as a route to the polished 12-site baseline; kept as a step-efficiency tool.
* **Kill rule**: `tests/P4.1_cheap_su2_r25/RULE_D.md`, written before the run: three 60-step replicas of R21's held-out-step protocol with guided draws (16,384 samples per step) from `che`'s SR-trained start; R = fraction of the gap to the exact energy removed, scored exactly. REACHES-POLISHED if median R(60) ≥ 0.7475 (the exactly polished `che`) with no step raising the exact energy by more than 0.02 of the gap; CLOSE if 0.60 ≤ median < 0.7475; FAILS if median R(60) < 0.60 ("guided SR plateaus above the polished state; R38 stays a step-efficiency tool only").
* **Evidence**: T1 one-step screen (3 seeds): Rbar 0.086 ± 0.011 guided against 0.016 ± 0.022 unguided, close to plain 4 times the samples (0.095 ± 0.012). Multi-step, one seed: R(15) 0.364 against R21's 0.195. T1b: R(60) = 0.536, 0.512, 0.508 (median 0.512 < 0.60); last steps gain about zero; 1.92 CPU-h. 
* **Why this kills it**: the median replica stops at about half the gap, well short of what exact steps reach from the same family, and the flat last steps show a noise floor rather than slow progress, so more steps of the same estimator would not reach the polished state; FAILS fires.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: tuned mixtures (weight 0.2 and $p_{\mathrm{flat}}\propto|\psi|$ were fixed before the screen); more samples per step, or guided sampling combined with sample reuse (R34) or a force-shrinkage estimator; 24 sites, where the heavy-tail gain could differ; a decaying step length to push under the floor.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: a plateau at a noise floor is expected for any fixed-sample SR, so the test may only show that 16,384 samples (about 6 per parameter) are too few, not that guiding fails; answered in part: the rule asked whether this optimiser at this budget reaches the baseline, which is what the leaf needs, and the cheaper fixes are listed above as untested.

* **Lesson**: proposed library entry (guided sampling doubles sampled-SR step efficiency but plateaus at about half the gap).
