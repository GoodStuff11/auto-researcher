# R33 — minSR / kernel-trick SR with many samples (`minsr`) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Solve the SR step in sample space, $d=-O^{\mathsf T}(OO^{\mathsf T}+\lambda)^{-1}\bar e$ with $N_s\times N_s$ kernel $T=OO^{\mathsf T}$ (Chen & Heyl, Nat. Phys. 20, 1476, 2024; Rende et al. kernel trick), so the solve costs $\mathcal O(N_s^2P)$ and many more samples fit, to bring the sampled step to the exact one.
* **Lineage**: Round 22 sub-question B (scalable sampled optimiser), from the coordinator's list.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G2 (T0, 2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 2: is the route distinguishable from the plain SR baseline it replaces?
* **Evidence**: by the push-through identity $O^{\mathsf T}(OO^{\mathsf T}+\lambda)^{-1}=(O^{\mathsf T}O+\lambda)^{-1}O^{\mathsf T}$, minSR with shift $\lambda$ returns exactly the parameter-space SR step from the same samples. It changes the solve cost, $\mathcal O(N_s^2P)$ against $\mathcal O(NP^2+P^3)$, which matters only when $P\gg N_s$. Here $P\approx2700$ and $N_s=16{,}384$, so $N_s>P$ and minSR is the costlier form of the same estimator; the sample count is limited by the cost of the log-derivative rows, which minSR does not reduce. At 24 sites $P$ grows to about $10^4$ for the `pcg` width used so far, still within the regime where the parameter-space solve is cheaper per sample.
* **Why this kills it**: same samples give the same step; the bottleneck measured in R21 (10% of the exact step's gain at about 6 samples per parameter) is estimator noise, which an identical estimator cannot reduce.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: very wide networks ($P\gg10^5$) where only minSR's solve fits in memory; any change of the estimator itself (R36, R37) applied inside minSR.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: minSR is used with $\lambda$ chosen relative to the kernel's spectrum, a different regulariser; answered: that is a choice of shift, which RULE_B's V1/V6–V8 comparisons already vary.
* **Checked by**: per-leaf critic, 2026-11-17: upheld.
* **Lesson**: route-specific; the identity is textbook.
