# R48 — backflow fed SU(2)-scalar moments of its own charge-conditioned Gaussian (moment-feat) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Give the cheap exact-S network spin information without reading $x$'s spins: compute SU(2)-scalar quantities of the charge-conditioned Gaussian $\Phi_c$ (bond correlations $\langle\mathbf S_i\cdot\mathbf S_j\rangle_{\Phi_c}$, local moments, pair amplitudes) and feed them back to the backflow. They are rotation scalars, so the Gaussian stays a function of $c$ and Che's closed-form projection stays exact (Lemma B).
* **Lineage**: round-30 batch, item (c) of the brief (spin information inside the Gaussian class, L11).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G2 (T0, 2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 2: does a matched-compute foil reach the same function class?
* **Evidence**: derivation. Each feature is a deterministic function of $(c,\theta)$, since $\Phi_c$ is fixed by $c$ and the parameters. The network is therefore $\mathrm{NN}_2(c,f(c,\theta))$, a function of $c$, contained by universal approximation in `che` with a wider or deeper charge network, the matched-compute foil (L38). Among constructions whose only spin-reading ingredients are diagonal in the up/down basis, the cheap exact-S ones known here are sums of Gaussians per charge configuration (Lemma B); within that class spin enters only via the Gaussians' type and number. Outside it: non-diagonal SU(2)-scalar operators (R12, R13, R2.1, R25) and spin-adapted bases (R0.4, R22), killed on size scaling or accuracy, not on exactness or cost.
* **Why this kills it**: it adds features, not information; at best it reparametrises `che`, and a gain could not be credited to spin information.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: Among constructions whose only spin-reading ingredients are diagonal in the up/down basis, the cheap exact-S ones known here are sums of Gaussians per charge configuration (Lemma B); within that class spin enters only via the Gaussians' type and number (type: pairing, R2; non-collinear, RG3; number: R1, G4). Outside it: non-diagonal SU(2)-scalar operators (R12, R13, R2.1, R25) and spin-adapted bases (R0.4, R22), killed on size scaling or accuracy, not on exactness or cost; this kill says nothing about them. It holds only for features that are functions of (charge configuration, parameters), which leave the network a function of $c$.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: self-consistent features may be an inductive bias a plain network learns slowly. Answered: that is a learning-speed gain (L33), not expressivity.
* **Checked by**: per-leaf critic, 2026-11-17: upheld on its narrow reasoning; the "only via type and number, all tried" scope reworded (objection 1).
* **Lesson**: L11 (among spin-diagonal constructions, the cheap exact-S ones known are $c$-indexed Gaussian sums; not a lower bound over all constructions).
