# R37 — zero-mean Hamiltonian-symmetry control variate for the SR force (`cv`) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: For a real Hermitian $H$, $\langle O_iE_{\mathrm{loc}}\rangle$ and $\langle(HO_i\psi)/\psi\rangle$ have the same expectation, so their difference is a zero-mean control variate for the force. Mixing the two estimators, $f_\beta=(1-\beta)f+\beta f_{\mathrm{alt}}$, could cut the force noise that limits the sampled step.
* **Lineage**: Round 22 sub-question B, coordinator's list (SR with control variates); related to Assaraf–Caffarel zero-variance estimators.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T1 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: `tests/P4.1_cheap_su2_r25/RULE_B.md`: KILL if Rbar ≤ Rbar(base) + 0.02, and KILL at equal cost unless the force variance falls by at least $K$, the mean number of Hamiltonian connections per sample (the cost of $(HO\psi)/\psi$ needs log-derivatives on every connected configuration).
* **Evidence**: one step, 3 seeds: base 0.016 ± 0.022; $\beta=0.5$ −0.005 ± 0.010; $\beta=1$ −0.008 ± 0.011. Force error in the $(S+10^{-3})^{-1}$ norm over 16 seeds fell 3.9× ($\beta=0.5$) and 6.7× ($\beta=1$), against $K=32.6$.
* **Why this kills it**: the variance reduction is five times short of its cost, and the steps got worse even at equal samples, because replacing the force breaks the cancellation between errors of $S$ and $f$ built on the same samples.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: control variates that need no connected-configuration log-derivatives.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: an optimised per-parameter $\beta$ could do better; it cannot recover a 5× cost gap.
* **Checked by**: Round 22 worker (2026-11-17); not yet reviewed by a critic.
* **Lesson**: as R32 (force noise limits the step), plus: change $S$ and $f$ together, since their sampling errors partly cancel.
