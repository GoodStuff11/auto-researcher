# R32 — two-stage blocked SR step (Zhao–Neuscamman blocking for SR) (`bsr`) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Split the 2637 parameters into 21 blocks (P0, Jastrow, W1, b1, b2, and W2 per hidden unit), solve the shifted SR system inside each block, where samples per parameter are 20 times higher, and recombine the 21 block directions with a small sampled 21×21 solve that restores inter-block coupling, as the blocked linear method does (Zhao & Neuscamman, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 13, 2604, 2017).
* **Lineage**: Round 22 sub-question B (scalable sampled optimiser), coordinator's list; also block-diagonal QGT approximations in NQS (layer-wise, K-FAC-like; 2025).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T1 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: `tests/P4.1_cheap_su2_r25/RULE_B.md`, written before the run: class bound "if Rbar(exact S + sampled f) below Rbar(base) + 0.05, every metric-side route is KILLED"; per variant KILL if Rbar ≤ Rbar(base) + 0.02. R is the exact gain of one step from `che`'s SR-trained parameters in units of the gap, best of three exact-metric lengths, mean of 3 seeds, 16,384 samples.
* **Evidence**: base 0.016 ± 0.022; block-diagonal −0.002 ± 0.008; two-stage blocked −0.007 ± 0.005; exact S with sampled force −0.024 ± 0.011; exact force with sampled S +0.171 ± 0.015 (exact step 0.288).
* **Why this kills it**: blocking improves the metric estimate, but the metric is not what limits the sampled step: even the exact metric makes it worse, while the exact force recovers 60% of the exact step. The block step also drops cross-block curvature.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: blocking as a memory device at very large P, and blocking combined with a better force estimate.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: one start point, one size; the noise split could differ at the polished endpoints or 24 sites; unresolved.
* **Checked by**: Round 22 worker (2026-11-17); not yet reviewed by a critic.
* **Lesson**: proposed library entry (log round-22): in sampled SR at a few samples per parameter the force noise, not the metric noise, limits the step.
