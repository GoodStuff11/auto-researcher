# R28 — metric-whitened L-BFGS from exact S builds, with periodic metric refresh (`plbfgs`) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Pay for one exact metric build (1.9 CPU-min for `che`), whiten the parameters with it, $\theta=\theta_0+Mz$, $M=U(w+10^{-7}w_{\max})^{-1/2}$, and run L-BFGS in $z$ with exact gradients at 0.13 CPU-min each; the hybrid (ledger row 2) refreshes the metric every $k$ gradient steps. The metric removes the near-redundant parameter combinations that made plain L-BFGS (R27) crawl, and cheap gradient steps replace most expensive builds.
* **Lineage**: Round 22 sub-question A, Operation A on R27 (T1 AMBIGUOUS, 1.12×); the refresh hybrid became reopening-ledger row 2 at the round-23 closure attempt; killed at T0 in round 24.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T0 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: ledger row 2: the hybrid must reach $\ge 3\gamma_{\mathrm{LM}}$ ($\gamma_{\mathrm{LM}}=7.27\times10^{-6}$ relative energy per CPU-min, `che`) to reopen anything; below $1.5\gamma_{\mathrm{LM}}$ KILLED (rules of `tests/P4.1_cheap_su2_r25/RULE.md`, R30's RULE).
* **Evidence**: derivation from round 22's R28 run (`results/che_plbfgs.json`; build $B=1.874$, call $c=0.133$ CPU-min). A cycle costs $B+kc$ and gains $G(k)$. Repeating the first cycle: max rate 1.31× ($k\approx16$). Best $k$-call stretch anywhere in the run: 1.35× (k=15), 1.45× with overshoot calls removed. One ladder step plus $k$ L-BFGS calls on the shared build, best stretch credited: 1.69× (1.79× without overshoots). 3× would need $B\le0.33$ CPU-min, 6 times cheaper than the exact build. Log `round-24-R28`.
* **Why this kills it**: every bound is an overestimate (best stretches from a higher eps, gains shrink as eps falls, each refresh restarts the L-BFGS memory, which cost 7 calls in round 22), yet the most generous is 1.79×, below 3×, and the bounds that keep the hybrid's own structure are below 1.5×. The best possible test outcome reopens nothing, so the test is not worth running.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: a metric 5 or more times cheaper than the exact build (sketched or gradient-updated S); unpolished starts; the `pcg` arm (build/gradient ratio 13 vs 14, no better).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: a refreshed metric could make later L-BFGS steps better than any stretch of the round-22 run, since that run's metric was stale for 50 calls. Answered: the first 7–16 calls after a fresh build are in the data, and their rate (1.08–1.31×) is the fresh-metric case; bound 3 already credits the best stretch to every cycle.
