# R2.3 — Irrep scan with fixed-node-ranked selection (round-2 Route 2.3) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Project the trained NNBF onto every real one-dimensional irreducible representation (irrep) of the lattice symmetry group, score each projection by the fixed-node energy, and keep the best. The hope was that the sign-only score picks the ground state's symmetry sector where the variational energy does not.
* **Lineage**: Operation B.1 (frame change into the irrep frame), round 2. Kept as diagnostic arm P of the round-2 test; its finding (most of the sign error is symmetry contamination) drove the round-3 pivot to projected training.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G3 (2026-11-11; Gate 0 also failed unless fixed-node ranking beat energy ranking).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 3: does it separate competitors that share a symmetry sector? Gate 0, as pre-registered in the round-2 rule: FAIL "if energy ranking is right at least as often (then it is plain projection, already standard)".
* **Evidence**: A scan separates competitors only when they sit in different irreps; P3.2 found that stripe competitors share a sector after projection on large clusters. Projection of backflow states with energy ranking is standard (Romero, Nys and Carleo, arXiv:2406.09077; Loehr and Clark, arXiv:2510.26906). On the smoke run both rankings picked the ground state's irrep (1 of 1), so the scored verdict was FAIL.
* **Why this kills it**: Where the scan works it is standard projection, and where competitors share a sector it cannot work.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: its use as a diagnostic, which it remained, and projected training (adopted in round 3).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: on small clusters the competitors do sit in different sectors, so the scan would help there (not raised by a critic). Answered: there plain energy ranking does as well.
