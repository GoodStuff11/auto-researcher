# R2.4 — Better local optimiser, MinSR or SPRING with a smaller rate (round-2 Route 2.4) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Replace sampled-metric SR by an exact-metric or minimum-step natural-gradient method (MinSR, SPRING) with a smaller learning rate, on the theory that the wrong-sign state is an artefact of noisy, large SR steps rather than a true basin.
* **Lineage**: Operation A.1 on the optimiser (round 2). Revisited in the reopening review as R7.2.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-11).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0: is it known, and is the reason it does not escape basins fixed?
* **Evidence**: MinSR (Chen and Heyl, Nat. Phys. 20, 1476, 2024) and SPRING (Goldshlager, Abrahamsen and Lin) exist. A better local optimiser converges faster into the basin it starts in. Later, R7.1 showed that even a second-order fit aimed straight at the exact signs gains only 22% on the toy.
* **Why this kills it**: Local optimisers change the speed of convergence, not the basin reached, so the route cannot address the leaf's question.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: faster convergence as a cost gain (not this leaf's target).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: if the residual error were slow convergence rather than a basin or capacity, a better optimiser would remove it (not raised by a critic). Answered by R7.1: a second-order sign-targeted fit plateaus too.
