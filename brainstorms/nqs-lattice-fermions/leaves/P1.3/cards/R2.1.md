# R2.1 — Symmetrised natural-orbital re-anchoring with fixed-node acceptance, SNO-FN (round-2 Route 2.1, re-audited as Route 3.0) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: After a warm-up, estimate the one-body density matrix of the trained NNBF, average it over the lattice symmetry group and diagonalise it, giving natural orbitals of definite parity. Candidates replace the reference orbitals with the top natural orbitals or frontier swaps, keeping the backflow weights. After $r=40$ relaxation steps each candidate is scored by the guided fixed-node energy (the ten Haaf construction with the candidate's signs), which replaces its amplitudes by the best amplitudes for that sign, so the relaxation-limited amplitude error that killed R2 drops out.
* **Lineage**: Operation A.3 from R2 after its kill at G3 (acceptance retargeted to a sign-only score; moves regenerated from the trained state). Re-audited in round 3 as Route 3.0 (card R3.0); mutated into R3.1.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-11, round-3 re-audit; it also failed G2 and G3).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0 (known, adoption barrier not fixed), Gate 2 (can a fair null match it?) and Gate 3 (does it win only on an instance without the Hard Core?).
* **Evidence**: Gate 0: at scale the acceptance is lattice fixed-node Green's-function Monte Carlo guided by the NQS, which P1.1 killed at Gate 0 (Loehr and Clark, arXiv:2510.26906: its energy depends on the guide). The test computed it as a noise-free eigenvalue, so it could not fail on noise or population-control bias, and its roughly 10 fixed-node solves per cycle were outside the matched budget. Gate 2: the null restarted from the same symmetry-broken UHF, so it had less information than the route. Gate 3: on the target instance (4×3 Hubbard torus, $4+4$ electrons, $t'=-0.25$, $U=8$) projected SR from the same UHF reaches $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}\approx5\times10^{-6}t$ at width 8, against $0.082t$ for unprojected SR at width 32 (job 407525, three seeds). Projecting a 400-step unprojected state after training already cut its sign error from $0.084t$ to $0.019t$.
* **Why this kills it**: Its novel ingredient is an already-killed estimator used without its costs, and on its instance any symmetry-restoring move beats the unprojected null by construction, so a win would say nothing about basins.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: the re-anchoring move with a sampled, budgeted acceptance against projected controls (R3.1).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: the exact fixed-node score is a fair idealisation for a T1 test, and its bias at scale might be small (not raised by a critic). Answered: P1.1's kill rests on the guide dependence, not noise, and the target vanished under projection anyway.
