# R7.7 — Direct sign-oracle basin probe on the 4×4 instance (reopening ledger item I1) (SHELVED)
* **The idea**: On the 4×4 open $t$–$t'$–$J$ instance that still has a sign error, start from the 625182 end states at widths 4, 6 and 10, fit each by SR fidelity to the sign-oracle target (its own amplitudes with the exact signs), and release the best checkpoint for the same energy steps as a paired cold continuation. This is the only basin probe on the instance where the CAPACITY verdict lives, and it fixes the round-5 flaw that the oracle arm never started below the cold level.
* **Lineage**: reopening-ledger ingredient I1, from the cross-node critic (2026-11-16); id assigned in this register.
* **Outcome**: SHELVED (GATE-REFUSED: G2 predicted outcome and G4 tier, reviewer triage 2026-11-17).
* **Why shelved**: A T3-tier job of about 250 CPU-h (9 width-seed pairs, an oracle arm of at most 600 SR steps and a 300-step cold arm at 12–15 s per step on 8 threads). Refused on two compute-gate rules (reviewer triage, 2026-11-17). G2: the predicted outcome is ORACLE-MISS or RELAX with probability at least 0.8 (from the toy LM-MISS of R7.1, the width trend, and the oracle arm W ending at the cold level at every width), and only a BASIN, at probability at most 0.2, would reopen the leaf, so the run is not worth its cost. G4: a job of 100 to 1000 CPU-h needs a passed T2 on the route, and R7.7 has none. Past spend (2,233 CPU-h, all before the gate) does not count toward the G5 budget and was not part of the refusal. The refusal was recorded from the reviewer's reading of the gate rules; no `compute_gate.py` call was made for it.
* **What would unshelve it**: a user budget of about 250 CPU-h for the probe together with an auditor approval and a waiver of the passed-T2 tier rule; or cheap evidence that overturns the prediction: a Levenberg–Marquardt (LM) sign-oracle fit that halves the error on a gapped exact-sum toy whose cold error is at least $0.005t$, or a projected cold run captured by a same-sector competitor.
