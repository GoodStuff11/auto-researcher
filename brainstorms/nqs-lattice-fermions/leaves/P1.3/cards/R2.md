# R2 — Orbital-swap basin hopping, OSBH (round-1 Route 2, re-audited as Route 2.0) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: After a plain-SR warm-up, propose discrete swaps of the determinant's reference orbitals: one of the $w$ highest occupied one-body directions is exchanged with one of the $w$ lowest empty ones, for spin up, spin down or both. The trained correlator is kept, each proposal is relaxed for $r$ SR steps, and the lowest variational energy is accepted if it beats the incumbent relaxed for the same $r$ steps. All steps count against the same budget as plain SR. A swap changes the occupied subspace, hence the total momentum and the nodal structure, in one move, which SR cannot do continuously.
* **Lineage**: initial batch (round 1); the only round-1 survivor. Made conditional by the round-1 critic, re-audited and killed in round 2 as Route 2.0 (card R2.0), and kept as the ablation arm AE of the round-2 test. Mutated into R2.1 (Operation A.3: acceptance retargeted to a sign-only score).
* **Source analogy**: basin hopping in cluster global optimisation (Wales and Doye, J. Phys. Chem. A 101, 5111, 1997) and occupied–virtual swaps of the maximum-overlap method in quantum chemistry (Gilbert, Besley and Gill, DOI:10.1021/jp801738f). There, a relaxed or mean-field energy ranks solutions without amplitude error; here the trained correlator leaves a large amplitude error after any swap (Analogy Map B1).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G3 (2026-11-11; it also failed G0 and G2).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 3: does the route work only by removing the Hard Core? Concretely, can an energy compared after $r$ relaxation steps rank basins at the energy scale of competing orders? Gates 0 and 2 asked whether the move set is known and whether a null can match the metric.
* **Evidence**: The amplitude error left after a short relaxation is of order $0.1t$ to $1t$: P1.1 measured an amplitude share $\Delta E_{\mathrm{amp}}=0.023t$ even after 1500 steps at width 128, and the smoke run had re-anchored candidates about $2t$ above the incumbent before relaxation. The quantities to be ranked are smaller: the in-sector gap at the 4×3 avoided crossing is $0.273t$ ($0.023t$ per site), and the Hard-Core scale is $10^{-3}t$ per site. Gate 0: the move set is SCF metadynamics, the maximum-overlap method and shell-occupation scans (Thom and Head-Gordon, PRL 101, 193001, 2008). Gate 2: the round-1 test had no matched-restart null, so its gap and escape fraction were order statistics of 3 to 12 runs, and its "NQS" arm had 378 parameters (effectively Jastrow–Slater). The 6-site smoke run accepted no swap and gave an escape fraction of $-0.24$.
* **Why this kills it**: An acceptance score whose error exceeds the differences it must rank chooses by noise, so the route cannot find the better basin at the Hard Core. Without a null, any gain could be extra restarts, and the move set itself is known.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: discrete re-anchoring with an acceptance that is blind to amplitude error (R2.1, R3.1), or with relaxations run to convergence; instances whose basin gaps exceed the relaxation error.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: with a long enough relaxation the energy does rank correctly (not raised by a critic). Answered: a relaxation that long costs as much as a restart, which is the multi-start baseline the route had to beat.
