* **Question**: Can VMC optimization be made to change the sign structure of a fermionic NQS cheaply, rather than staying in the basin set by the initial mean-field state?
* **Answer**: Not known yet, but on the clusters we could test there was no wrong-sign trap. Networks trained without the ground state's symmetries kept the symmetry breaking of their mean-field start. With symmetries built in, the sign error on a 16-site cluster fell about 3-fold as the network doubled in size. On a 12-site cluster even a fit aimed straight at the exact signs got only about 20% below ordinary training, so network size, not the optimiser, sets the limit. The direct 16-site trap test (about 250 CPU-hours) was judged predictable and not run.
* **Conclusion**: At exact-diagonalization size, no wrong-sign basin was found for a symmetry-projected neural-network backflow state. The residual sign error on the 4×4 open $t$–$t'$–$J$ instance falls as the network widens, and on a 12-site toy even a second-order fit aimed straight at the exact signs gets only 22% below the cold start: the lever is expressivity, not the optimiser. The one direct basin probe on the 4×4 instance (about 250 CPU-h) was refused by the compute gate as predictable, so that instance's basin question is closed at this budget, not answered.
* **Scope**: projected NNBF (width 3 to 10) with exact-force stochastic reconfiguration; the sign half on one 4×4 OBC $t$–$t'$–$J$ instance ($6+6$, $t'=-0.25$, $J=0.4$) and on a 4×3 open $t$–$J$ toy ($5+5$); the competitor half on 4×3 tori at in-sector gaps of $0.234t$ and $0.273t$ and on the toy at $0.12t$. Exact sums or exact forces throughout; nothing beyond 16 sites.
* **Compute spent**: 2,233 CPU-h in five Slurm jobs (410199, 451976, 507178, 507179, 625182; master Compute Ledger), plus 0.3 CPU-h of single-thread T1 runs (593 CPU-s in round 6, 489 CPU-s in the reopening review). All of it predates the compute gate.

**The question.** A neural quantum state (NQS) is a neural network that returns the amplitude $\psi_\theta(\mathbf n)$ of a many-body wave function for each occupation configuration $\mathbf n$. For fermions the sign of $\psi_\theta$ carries the antisymmetry, and on doped two-dimensional lattices the signs are the hard part. The ansatz is neural-network backflow (NNBF), a Slater determinant whose orbitals depend on the whole configuration through a small network, trained by stochastic reconfiguration (SR), a natural-gradient method that acts like a small imaginary-time step and never flips a sign directly. A network started from unrestricted Hartree–Fock (UHF) might therefore stay in a *wrong-sign basin*: signs worse than the best the network could hold, which SR never leaves. If such basins exist, a cheap optimiser fix beats a bigger network. Sign quality is $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}=E_{\mathrm{sign}}-E_0$, where $E_0$ is the exact ground energy from exact diagonalization (ED) and $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ the lowest energy reachable with the trained signs fixed and the best non-negative amplitudes; it vanishes only for the exact signs. "Projected" means the output is summed over the lattice symmetry group $G$, $\psi_P=P_\chi\psi_\theta$, so the state carries the ground state's symmetry labels $\chi$.

**The chain of evidence.** First, the early basins were symmetry contamination. On a 4×3 Hubbard torus an unprojected width-32 NNBF had $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}=0.082t$; training with projection from the same UHF start reached $5\times10^{-6}t$ at width 8 (job 407525). After projection only a same-sector competitor can be a basin, and on the smallest-gap torus found ($\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}=0.234t$) all nine projected cold runs reached $\psi_0$ (410199). Second, on the 4×4 OBC cluster (dimension 1,681,680) a projected width-6 NNBF keeps $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}=0.0125t$ (451706). Three cold arms from different references and an oracle arm W, fidelity-trained toward $\psi_0$ and released, got equal energy steps under a pre-registered rule. The best cold error fell 0.0192, 0.0117 and 0.0070 at widths 4, 6 and 10 (1284, 1734, 2634 parameters), and W ended at 0.0189, 0.0115 and 0.0070, within margins of 0.003 to 0.005 (507179, 625182). Every width stayed formally unconverged, so the pre-registered width-trend tie-break returned CAPACITY. Third, because W never started below the cold level, two toy probes placed a better-signed state directly: the target keeps the cold state's amplitudes and inserts the exact signs. On a projected 4×3 $t$–$J$ toy (675 parameters), first-order SR reached 0.0092 against a cold 0.0109 and Levenberg–Marquardt (LM, second order) 0.0085, both far from the threshold 0.0055 (R7.1). Released, the states returned to the cold level at slightly higher energy. At $\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}=0.12t$ no cold run was captured by the competitor (R7.3).

**Why the alternatives fail.** Energy-accepted orbital swaps cannot rank basins below the relaxation's amplitude error (R2); fixed-node acceptance reuses a killed estimator (R2.1); capped deflation and orthogonal probes are SCF metadynamics (R2.2, R3.2); sampler changes cannot move SR's fixed points (R3); continuation keeps the metastable branch (R1, R4, R3.3); better local optimisers converge into the same basin (R2.4, R7.2); tempering needs a basin that is absent (R7.5); projection-cost ideas cannot be judged with exact sums (R3.4, R7.4); and the one surviving escape route lost its premise (R3.1).
