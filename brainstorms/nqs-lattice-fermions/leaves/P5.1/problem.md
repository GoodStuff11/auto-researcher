# Problem: P5.1 Efficient optimisation of fermionic NQS to their representable minimum

### 1. Mathematical Abstraction & Barriers
* **Formulation**: An ansatz $\psi_\theta$, $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^P$ (determinant or Pfaffian layer with backflow), energy $E(\theta)=\langle\psi_\theta|\hat H|\psi_\theta\rangle/\langle\psi_\theta|\psi_\theta\rangle$. The **representable minimum** is $E^\star_\theta=\min_\theta E(\theta)$ over the ansatz's own parameters (in practice the lowest value any optimiser reaches; computable to useful accuracy only by exact sums over the sector on small clusters, here the 12-site `che` state, open $4\times3$ Hubbard, $5+5$, $U=8$, $t'=-0.25$, $P=2637$, 157,216 orbit representatives). Progress is scored as $R=(E_{\rm start}-E)/(E_{\rm start}-E_0)$, the fraction of the gap to the exact ground energy $E_0$ removed; the exactly ladder-polished state has $R_{\rm pol}=0.7475$; its energy is an upper bound on $E^\star_\theta$, so in $R$ terms $R_{\rm pol}$ is a lower bound on $R^\star$ (shortfalls measured against it mix the optimiser's deterministic shortfall with noise; R51.8 measures against each schedule's own noise-free run). A **sampled step** draws $N$ configurations, costs $N\,(c_{\rm loc}+c_{O})$ for local energies ($\mathcal O(\text{connections})$ amplitudes each) and log-derivative rows, plus a metric solve ($\mathcal O(NP^2+P^3)$ for SR, $\mathcal O(N^2P+N^3)$ for MinSR). **Total samples to accuracy** $N_{\rm tot}(\delta)=\sum_t N_t$ until $E(\theta_t)-E^\star_\theta\le\delta\,(E_{\rm start}-E^\star_\theta)$. **Solved** means: an optimiser whose per-step cost is $\mathrm{poly}(V)$ reaches $\delta\lesssim0.1$ with $N_{\rm tot}$ below that of SR and MinSR at the same $\delta$, shown on exact clusters and at one size where exact sums are impossible (the 24-site strip, with a variance-extrapolated or DMRG reference).
* **The Node Barrier**: where existing optimisers halt (this run's evidence):
  - **B1 Ill-conditioning at polished endpoints.** The metric $S$ spans more than $10^7$ in eigenvalue and the remaining gain lies in its small-eigenvalue directions; exact linear-method ladders converge only linearly (gain ratio 0.76–0.98 per step), and L-BFGS, truncated Newton and Anderson were not faster per CPU-minute (L29, L60).
  - **B2 Force noise at few samples per parameter.** Near the nodes $O=\partial\ln\psi$ diverges, so the force $f=\mathrm{cov}(O,E_{\rm loc})$ is heavy-tailed; at 6 samples per parameter the exact force recovers 60% of the exact step, the exact metric none, and improving $f$ alone breaks the $S$–$f$ error cancellation (L59). Guided draws halve the noise (L61); R38 with them was stopped at 51% of the gap after 60 steps, still descending (R51.1).
  - **B3 Step-selection noise.** A step length scored on held-out samples needs a resolution finer than one step's gain; in R38's late steps the held-out error was 2 times the typical selected drop (R51.6). This did not slow the optimiser: at equal samples, fixed-step and held-out-step SR progress at the same rate through 60 steps; the fixed step simply keeps going (R51.1, T1a; the earlier reading that selection caused R38's plateau was withdrawn by the per-leaf critic, 2026-11-17). B3 is therefore a cost of noisy acceptance tests (R51.6), not a demonstrated cause of stalling.
  - **B4 Cost of the metric.** The exact metric build is 1.9 CPU-min against 0.13 for an exact gradient; its cost is dominated by the per-configuration Jacobian rows (1.14 CPU-min) rather than the Gram product (0.23) or the eigensolve (0.04) (round 1, T1). Sampled linear-method steps diverge or keep about 30% of the exact gain (L55).
* **Anti-Solution Space**: no claim of fast global convergence for arbitrary instances (QMA-hardness, Invariants §5); nothing that needs exact sums at the target size (L39); no single-proposal importance estimator of a non-stoquastic sum (L12); no metric-only estimator improvement in the force-noise regime (L59); no result scored after a fixed short number of steps or at an unpolished foil (L33).
* **Novelty audit (what is known and why it is not a solution)**:
  - SR / natural gradient (Sorella 1998; Amari) and its kernel form MinSR (Chen & Heyl, Nat. Phys. 20, 1476, 2024) make the step affordable at $P\gg N$, but say nothing about how many samples reach $E^\star_\theta$; MinSR's step at $N<P$ is the most noise-exposed (R15 regime).
  - SPRING (Goldshlager, Abrahamsen & Lin, J. Comput. Phys. 2024, arXiv:2401.10190) beat MinSR and KFAC on small molecules; here it was 2.67 times worse at equal wall time at 0.1 samples per parameter (P4.1 R15). Its convergence for $\mu<1$ has an $\mathcal O(1/N_s)$ sampling floor (Wang & Liu, arXiv:2604.18357, 2026), which states the floor but not how to remove it.
  - KFAC and layer-block QGT (FermiNet/DeepQMC; Shokry et al., arXiv:2510.08430) cut the metric's cost; they do not address force noise, and blocking failed here (P4.1 R32). Rende et al. (arXiv:2310.05715) scale SR in parameters, not in samples.
  - Linear method for NQS (Frank & Kastoryano, arXiv:2104.11011; blocked LM, Zhao & Neuscamman, JCTC 13, 2604, 2017): sampled LM loses the zero-variance property at few samples per parameter (L55).
  - Overdispersed sampling for NQS (arXiv:2507.05352) and guiding functions for infinite-variance QMC forces (Attaccalite & Sorella, PRL 100, 114501, 2008; Pathak & Wagner, AIP Adv. 10, 085213, 2020): adopted here (R38, R51.1), halve the noise.
  - SR's shift as a statistical filter at $P>N$ (Hur, NeurIPS 2026); Nyström-preconditioned natural gradient for PINNs (arXiv:2505.11638) and for neural Gross–Pitaevskii VMC (arXiv:2512.11339): matrix-free metric solves, which save the Gram and eigensolve but not the Jacobian rows that dominate here (B4).
  - **Which SR the Question means, and what is not new.** "SR" and "MinSR" in the Question are taken in their papers' sense: Born-sampled ($|\psi|^2$) fixed-step SR (Sorella; NetKet; Chen & Heyl). Guided (overdispersed) sampling is a published sampler (arXiv:2507.05352), not standard SR, so a Yes resting on guided or $|\psi|$ draws (R51.11's $q\propto|\psi|$ is the $\alpha=1$ member of the same family) credits a published sampler, measured here against the representable minimum. The comparison with Born-sampled SR at equal total samples rests on R51.8's 10-site Born arms (all at $N\ge P$ but one 1k rung) and R51.16's 12-site Born arms ($N\ge P$). MinSR differs from SR only at $N<P$; that half of the comparison is untested and has no route.
  - Searches found no NQS work using leverage-score (spectral) row sampling for the metric, SVRG-type variance reduction in SR, or a measurement of total samples to the representable minimum (searches 2026-11-17, log round-01).

### 1b. Analogy Map
* **B1 restated**: minimise a smooth function whose curvature spans many decades, where the remaining decrease lies along nearly flat directions and each curvature estimate is expensive.
  - *Quantum chemistry SCF* (DIIS, trust-region Newton; Pulay 1980): extrapolation works because the SCF map is smooth and near-linear close to convergence. Gap: the ladder map is not smooth (cutoff jumps). Tried: P4.1 R29, R30 (killed, L60).
  - *Inverse problems* (truncated SVD, Tikhonov, discrepancy principle): the incumbent cutoff ladder (L29). Gap: none; it is the foil.
  - *Deep-learning second order* (K-FAC, Shampoo): Kronecker factors are cheap for dense layers with large minibatches. Gap: few samples per parameter, small eigen-directions matter. Tried: P4.1 R32 (killed).
  - *Sketched numerical linear algebra* (randomised Nyström preconditioning, Frangella, Tropp & Udell, SIMAX 44, 718, 2023): works when matvecs are cheap and the spectrum decays fast. Gap: here the Jacobian rows, not the products, cost most, and the needed rank is over 1200 of 2637 (L29). Tried: R51.2 (killed T1).
* **B2 restated**: estimate a regression of a heavy-tailed response on heavy-tailed regressors from a few samples per coefficient, where the estimates of the design matrix and of the cross-covariance share errors that partly cancel.
  - *Stochastic optimisation variance reduction* (SVRG, Johnson & Zhang 2013; SAGA): works because the per-sample gradient at an anchor is recomputable cheaply on the same data. Gap: in VMC a sample is tied to $|\psi_\theta|^2$, so anchor corrections need reweighting whose variance grows with size; reuse was killed for cost (P4.1 R34). Tried: R51.13 (direction recursion, killed T1 at $a$=0.1 only), R51.18 (recursion on $S$ and $f$, killed T1 at $a$=0.1 with the fresh-metric length rule).
  - *Optimal subsampling for regression* (Wang, Zhu & Ma, JASA 113, 829, 2018): sample rows $\propto$ residual × leverage to minimise estimator variance. Gap: only amplitude-based distributions are free in VMC. Tried: R51.11 (LIVE).
  - *Random reshuffling* (Gürbüzbalaban, Ozdaglar & Parrilo, Math. Prog. 186, 49, 2021): without-replacement epochs beat i.i.d. sampling on a finite sum. Gap: the VMC objective is a population mean. Tried: R51.17 (killed T0).
  - *Real-space QMC infinite-variance forces* (guiding functions, $\epsilon$-regularisation): worked by bounding the near-node weights. Tried: R38 (tool), force shrinkage R39 (killed).
  - *Randomised NLA row sampling* (leverage scores, Drineas, Mahoney & Muthukrishnan 2006; subsampled Newton, Roosta-Khorasani & Mahoney, Math. Prog. 174, 293, 2019): sampling rows by leverage gives a relative spectral approximation in every direction with $\mathcal O(P\log P)$ rows. Gap: leverage needs the metric itself (stale scores), and Born sampling is fixed by VMC. Tried: R51.3 (T1 PASS); sketched leverage on sampled rows, R51.12 (killed T0).
  - *Robust statistics* (median-of-means, Lugosi & Mendelson 2019): works for i.i.d. heavy tails at many samples per block. Gap: blocks here have about 1.5–2 samples per parameter. Tried: R51.5 (killed T0 in round 1, reopened to LIVE 2026-11-17; near-node forces have infinite variance, Trail, PRE 77, 016703, 2008, so heavy tails persist at block level).
  - *Statistical learning theory* (random-design ridge regression, Hsu, Kakade & Zhang, Found. Comput. Math. 14, 569, 2014; kernel ridge rates, Caponnetto & De Vito 2007): the excess risk is effective dimension × residual variance / $N$, so the sample need of a ridge step is a closed-form constant $\kappa$ of one state. Correspondence: features → $O$, response → $E_{\rm loc}$, ridge → SR shift. Gap: heavy-tailed $O$ at nodes and an iterated step. Tried: R51.10 (T1, scoring on R51.8).
* **B3 restated**: choose a step size when the objective can be evaluated only with noise larger than one step's improvement.
  - *Stochastic trust region / line search* (STORM, Chen, Menickelly & Scheinberg, Math. Prog. 169, 447, 2018): accept only probabilistically accurate decreases. Gap: needs accuracy that scales with the step's gain. Tried: R51.6 (killed T1).
  - *Stochastic approximation* (Robbins–Monro step decay; Polyak–Ruppert averaging, SIAM J. Control Optim. 30, 838, 1992): needs no function values; averaging cancels the noise ball. Gap: non-convex, redundant parameters; averaging off the noise ball. Tried: R51.7 (SHELVED until a fixed-step run levels off; its T2 kill was outside its premise).
  - *VMC correlated-sampling line search* (Umrigar et al. 2007): works at many samples per parameter. Gap: overfits at few (L52).
* **B5 (round 4) restated**: how the number of noisy measurements per update needed to keep an iterative estimator on its noise-free path grows with problem size.
  - *Ridge / kernel regression sample complexity* (as B2): grows with the effective dimension, polynomial for light tails. Tried: R51.10.
  - *Monte Carlo coverage of a distribution's support* (effective sample size, $e^{H}$ as the number of typical configurations): estimators whose noise tracks the support need samples growing like $e^{sV}$; also why aggregating duplicate draws stops saving once $e^{H}\gg N$. Used as the exponential reference in R51.8.
  - *Stochastic-gradient scaling laws in deep learning* (critical batch size grows with the gradient noise scale, McCandlish et al., arXiv:1812.06162, 2018): batch size beyond the noise scale buys little per sample, matching R51.1's equal-total-sample result (smaller $N$ per step wins). Gap: the noise scale is measured, not predicted, and its growth with model size is empirical. Tried: R51.15 (LIVE).
  - *Dynamic sample-size methods* (Byrd, Chin, Nocedal & Wu, Math. Prog. 134, 127, 2012, norm test): enlarge the batch when the gradient's sampling error exceeds a fraction of its norm. Gap: heavy-tailed rows make the sampled error estimate noisy. Tried: R51.15 (LIVE).
* **B4 restated**: form a Gram matrix of $10^5$ expensive rows when only its action in a few thousand directions matters.
  - *Semistochastic projector QMC* (Petruzielo et al., PRL 109, 230201, 2012): exact in a deterministic subspace, sampled outside. Gap: the deterministic share vanishes with size. Tried: R51.4 (killed G4).
  - *Sketching* (as B1): R51.2. *Subsampled Newton* (as B2): R51.3.
