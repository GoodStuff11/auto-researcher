* **Question**: Can a determinant- or Pfaffian-based fermionic NQS be optimised to within a small fraction of the lowest energy its own parameters can represent, at a sampled cost per step that grows polynomially with system size and with fewer total samples than stochastic reconfiguration or MinSR need for the same accuracy?
* **Answer**: Not known. Stochastic reconfiguration (SR, the standard natural-gradient optimiser) with flattened sampling came within about 2% of exact optimisation on one 12-site state and needed at least 3 times fewer samples than standard SR, but over the one size step measured (10 to 12 ladder sites) its samples per step grew 3.8 times, as fast as the number of configurations the state occupies; the third size needed to tell that from a steep power of the size was not bought. Against MinSR (the same step solved in sample space, different only with fewer samples than parameters) there is no comparison.
* **What we showed**: On a 12-site doped Hubbard network state, SR with flattened sampling and a fixed step fell short of exact noise-free optimisation by 23%, 9% and 2% of its gain at 16,000, 66,000 and 262,000 samples per step, so noise slows the path. At equal total samples it reached 90% of the noise-free gain with 3.3 to 9.4 times fewer samples than SR drawing from $|\psi|^2$ on three clusters of 10 and 12 sites. To come within 10% of its noise-free run it needs 31,280 samples per step on a 10-site two-leg ladder and 118,600 on the 12-site one: 3.8 times more, against 1.7 times for the cube of the size and 3.5 times for the occupied configurations. Generic noise-reduction tricks for the gradient (control variates built from the Hamiltonian, averaging over each move's neighbours, symmetry-paired samples, estimates across sizes) gave no net gain at equal cost on 10 and 12 sites; only reshaping the sampling distribution helped.

### The question and the argument
A fermionic neural quantum state (NQS) is trained by variational Monte Carlo: each step draws $N$ configurations, estimates the energy gradient (force $f$) and the metric $S$ (covariance of the log-derivatives), and moves along $S^{-1}f$ (SR; MinSR solves the same step in sample space, identical when $N\ge P$, the parameter count). On the 12-site state exact-sum optimisation reaches 75% of the gap to the exact energy (L61). "Guided" means a flattened distribution, a published sampler (arXiv:2507.05352); the Question's SR draws from $|\psi|^2$.

*Fixed step, sampling-limited (R51.1).* With the step fixed, the 120-step shortfall fell as $N^{-0.87}$ and barely responded to halving the step, so noise slows the path.

*The size question (R51.8, R51.10; R51.9 killed).* Polynomial cost is decided by how the needed $N$ grows with size against each size's noise-free run; one-step proxies missed it by 2–4× (R51.9). The statistic was re-registered (rev. 2) for the fixed step's bounce before any 12-site data; the 10-site need is 31,280 samples per step and the 12-site need 118,600: ×3.79, above the occupied-support growth ×3.52 and far above $V^3$ (×1.73), so this step is support-like (one seed; margin 1.08×). The 14-site third size (about 160 CPU-h) was rejected by the compute auditor: its likeliest result (AMBIGUOUS, p 0.49) changes nothing, and a support-like one would need a second seed beyond the budget (R51.8 SHELVED). Ridge-regression theory ($\kappa$, R51.10) predicted about 22k and is killed as a predictor.

*Alternatives.* Sampling $\propto|\psi|$ has 4.7× lower $\kappa$ than guided (R51.11) and at 12 sites needed 0.43× guided's total samples (Q-FEWER), but its reweighting variance grows with size (effective samples per draw 45% → 31% → 22% at 10, 12, 14 sites; shape unresolved). A growing samples-per-step schedule (R51.15) was killed (its gain was censoring); recursion on $S$ and $f$ (R51.18) failed because the recursed metric is indefinite. Direction recursion (R51.13), reuse, reshuffling and sketched leverage (R51.14, R51.17, R51.12) lost. Median-of-means (R51.5) was killed (block steps too often uphill); step decay (R51.7) is shelved, its trigger met at 10 sites. Force variance reduction (round 12, 10 and 12 sites): zero-variance control variates (R51.19) and discrete Stein controls (R51.25) keep ≥ 94% of the variance, because the SR force $O(E_{\rm loc}-E)$ is already zero-variance at an eigenstate; Rao–Blackwell averaging (R51.23) cuts variance 7–9× but costs one amplitude per neighbour (23 → 30), a net loss; antithetic pairs (R51.22) and multilevel estimates (R51.24) died at T0, gradient-optimal sampling (R51.20) is dominated by R51.11, and stratification (R51.21) merged into it.

*Strongest objections.* (1) Born-SR comparisons have one Born seed per arm; MinSR's $N<P$ regime is untested. (2) 10–14 sites can exclude support-like growth, not prove a polynomial; conversely the 10 → 12 step is one seed over a size factor 1.2, where ×3.8 is also $V^{7.3}$: far above $V^3$, not proven exponential.

*Limits.* Exact i.i.d. draws, one seed per arm, one network family, nothing at 24 sites.
