### The question and the argument
A fermionic neural quantum state (NQS) is trained by variational Monte Carlo: each step draws $N$ configurations, estimates the energy gradient (the force $f$) and the metric $S$ (the covariance of the log-derivatives of the wave function), and moves along the natural gradient $S^{-1}f$ (SR; MinSR is the same step solved in sample space, identical when $N\ge P$, the parameter count). On the 12-site test state, exact-sum optimisation reaches 75% of the gap to the exact energy (L61); "guided" means a flattened sampling distribution. Fixed-step SR with overdispersed sampling (arXiv:2507.05352) is standard practice.

*Fixed step, sampling-limited (R51.1).* Fixed-step and held-out-step SR progress equally at equal samples through 60 steps. With the step fixed, the 120-step shortfall fell as $N^{-0.87}$ and barely responded to halving the step, so noise slows the path. At equal total samples fewer per step won within 120 steps, yet only $4N$ and $16N$ reached 90% of the exact-sum value.

*The size question (R51.8, R51.10; R51.9 killed).* Polynomial cost is decided by how the needed $N$ grows with size against each size's noise-free run; one-step proxies missed it by 2–4× (R51.9). At 10 sites the guided need for a 5% shortfall was 94,150 per step, but the fixed step bounces near the minimum, so that number depends on the averaging window; the statistic was re-registered (10% on a 60-step mean, with a resolvability check) before any 12-site ladder data. Ridge-regression theory predicts the trend from one state ($\kappa$); its first step (10 → 12 sites, $\times0.70$) is on the polynomial side.

*Alternatives.* Round 5 added sampling $\propto|\psi|$, with 4.7× lower $\kappa$ than guided (R51.11), and a growing samples-per-step schedule (R51.15); variance-reduced recursions and reuse of old draws lost to fresh draws (R51.13, R51.18 at T1; R51.12, R51.14, R51.17 at T0). Median-of-means over sample blocks (R51.5) was reopened: its kill used a one-step probe, and near-node forces are infinite-variance (Trail, PRE 77, 016703, 2008). Step decay with averaging (R51.7) is shelved; leverage-sampled metric rows (R51.3) cut exact-sum cost, not samples.

*Strongest objections.* (1) The only equal-total-sample comparison with plain SR is one 10-site seed at $N\ge P$ (guided 9.4× fewer); MinSR's regime ($N<P$) reached 90% in no arm; R51.16 tests it at 12 sites. (2) The size test spans 10 to 14 sites, with the 10-site instance easier; it can exclude support-like growth, not prove a polynomial.

*Limits.* Exact i.i.d. draws (no Markov chains), one seed per arm, one network family, no 24-site evidence.
