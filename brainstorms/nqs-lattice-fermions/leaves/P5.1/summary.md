* **Question**: Can a determinant- or Pfaffian-based fermionic NQS be optimised to within a small fraction of the lowest energy its own parameters can represent, at a sampled cost per step that grows polynomially with system size and with fewer total samples than stochastic reconfiguration or MinSR need for the same accuracy?
* **Answer**: Not known. With flattened sampling and a fixed step, stochastic reconfiguration (SR, the standard natural-gradient optimiser) got within about 2% of exact optimisation on one 12-site state, but how its sample need grows with size is unmeasured. Against standard SR (samples drawn from $|\psi|^2$) at equal total samples, flattened sampling needed at least 3 times fewer samples at 10 and 12 sites (one seed per standard arm); against MinSR (the same step solved in sample space, different only with fewer samples than parameters) there is no comparison.
* **What we showed**: On a 12-site doped Hubbard network state, SR with flattened sampling and a fixed step fell short of exact noise-free optimisation (by a different method) by 23%, 9% and 2% of its gain at 16,000, 66,000 and 262,000 samples per step; halving the step removed only 14% of the shortfall, so noise slows the path. On the same 12-site state, at equal total samples, flattened SR reached a sustained 90% of the exact optimiser's gain with 4.5M samples where standard SR (two step lengths) needed 14.7M or never got there, and sampling $\propto|\psi|$ (reweighted) needed 1.9M. On a 10-site ladder, flattened sampling first reached 90% of the noise-free gain with 9.3–9.4 times fewer total samples than standard sampling (two flattened seeds, one standard seed, 120 steps), and needs 31,280 samples per step to come within 10% of the noise-free run's late-time gain. Near the minimum the fixed step bounces between two energies, so the last few percent are set by the step, not the samples.

### The question and the argument
A fermionic neural quantum state (NQS) is trained by variational Monte Carlo: each step draws $N$ configurations, estimates the energy gradient (force $f$) and the metric $S$ (covariance of the log-derivatives), and moves along $S^{-1}f$ (SR; MinSR solves the same step in sample space, identical when $N\ge P$, the parameter count). On the 12-site state exact-sum optimisation reaches 75% of the gap to the exact energy (L61). "Guided" means a flattened distribution, a published sampler (arXiv:2507.05352); the Question's SR draws from $|\psi|^2$.

*Fixed step, sampling-limited (R51.1).* With the step fixed, the 120-step shortfall fell as $N^{-0.87}$ and barely responded to halving the step, so noise slows the path; at equal total samples fewer per step won within 120 steps, yet only $4N$ and $16N$ reached 90%.

*The size question (R51.8, R51.10; R51.9 killed).* Polynomial cost is decided by how the needed $N$ grows with size against each size's noise-free run; one-step proxies missed it by 2–4× (R51.9). The statistic was re-registered (rev. 2) for the fixed step's bounce before any 12-site data; the 10-site need is 31,280 samples per step, the 12-site ladder is running. Ridge-regression theory ($\kappa$) puts 10 → 12 → 14 sites ($\times0.70$, $\times1.84$) on the polynomial side, a prediction recorded before the ladders.

*Alternatives.* Sampling $\propto|\psi|$ has 4.7× lower $\kappa$ than guided (R51.11) and at 12 sites needed 0.43× guided's total samples (Q-FEWER), but its reweighting variance grows with size (effective samples per draw 45% → 31% from 10 to 12 sites, Gate 4 FAIL at T0 on two volumes); a growing samples-per-step schedule (R51.15); recursion on $S$ and $f$ (R51.18) failed with a length that was correct in the exact metric: the recursed metric is indefinite and the solved direction raises the energy. Direction recursion at $a$=0.1 (R51.13), reuse and reshuffling (R51.14, R51.17) and sketched leverage (R51.12) lost. Median-of-means (R51.5) reopened, then killed: 4096-sample block steps raised the energy on 83 of 240 steps (line 80; 0.35 against 0.03 for 16,384 at matched progress); step decay (R51.7) shelved, its trigger now met at 10 sites.

*Strongest objections.* (1) Born-SR comparisons have one Born seed per arm (10 sites: guided 9.3–9.4× fewer; 12 sites: ≥ 3.3×); MinSR's $N<P$ regime is untested. (2) 10–14 sites can exclude support-like growth, not prove a polynomial.

*Limits.* Exact i.i.d. draws, one seed per arm, one network family, no 24-site evidence.
