# R51.17 — without-replacement pool reshuffling across steps (reshuffle) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Draw one pool of $KN$ configurations, then take $K$ consecutive steps, each on a disjoint mini-batch of $N$ from the pool (reweighted to the current parameters). In finite-sum SGD, random reshuffling beats with-replacement sampling because the mini-batch errors of one epoch sum to zero; here it would give $K$ small steps (the regime R51.1 found best at equal samples) whose accumulated noise is that of one $KN$ batch.
* **Lineage**: round 5 Operation B (forced orthogonal direction 3, cross-field translation; a new Analogy-Map entry under B2/B5).
* **Source analogy**: random reshuffling / without-replacement SGD (Gürbüzbalaban, Ozdaglar & Parrilo, Math. Prog. 186, 49, 2021; Mishchenko, Khaled & Richtárik, NeurIPS 2020): faster convergence to the minimiser of a finite sum, because each sample is used exactly once per epoch. Correspondence: epoch → $K$ SR steps; finite sum → the pool. Gap: the VMC objective is a population expectation, not the pool's average.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T0 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: T0: the route must reduce the accumulated step error over $K$ steps below that of $K$ independent batches of $N$ fresh draws at equal compute.
* **Evidence**: derivation. Let each step's error be the mini-batch mean of per-sample terms with variance $\sigma^2$. Fresh draws: $K$ independent errors, accumulated variance $K\sigma^2/N$. Reshuffled pool: the $K$ mini-batch errors sum to $K$ times the pool's error, whose variance is $\sigma^2/(KN)$, so the accumulated variance is $K^2\sigma^2/(KN)=K\sigma^2/N$, the same; the cancellation is relative to the pool mean, which itself carries the full sampling error. Reweighting adds variance (R51.14: $\chi^2$ 0.074 five steps apart at 10 sites), and compute is equal because every reused sample needs a fresh local energy and row (P4.1 R34).
* **Why this kills it**: to first order the accumulated error equals that of fresh draws, and the weights make it worse; there is no finite-sum structure for reshuffling to exploit.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: curvature-order effects (the order of mini-batches along a curved path), which are second order in the step; problems where the pool is the target (supervised fits to a fixed sample set).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: higher-order terms could favour reshuffling at large step; unresolved, but they are $\mathcal O(\tau^2)$ at $\tau=0.005$ and cannot offset the weight variance.
* **Checked by**: round 5 worker (2026-11-17); per-leaf critic owed.
* **Lesson**: proposed library entry: without-replacement reuse and reshuffling gain only relative to a finite pool; for a population objective with equal per-sample cost they match fresh draws at first order.
