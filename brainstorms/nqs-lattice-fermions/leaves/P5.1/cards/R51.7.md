# R51.7 — Robbins-Monro step decay with Polyak-Ruppert averaging (rmavg) (LIVE)
* **The idea**: Replace energy-based step-length selection, which round 1 showed is noisier than one step's gain at the plateau, by a schedule that needs no energies: a fixed metric length decaying as $\tau_t=\tau_0(t_0/(t_0+t))^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha\in(1/2,1)$, plus a running average of the parameters over the late steps. A constant step leaves the iterate in a noise ball whose size scales with the step; decaying it shrinks the ball, and averaging cancels the ball's spread without extra samples.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, cross-field translation from stochastic approximation (barrier B3); its T1 is gated on R51.1's half-length arm (STEP-NOISE).
* **Source analogy**: Robbins–Monro stochastic approximation and Polyak–Ruppert iterate averaging (Polyak & Juditsky, SIAM J. Control Optim. 30, 838, 1992). Source: stochastic gradients of a locally strongly convex objective; averaging attains the optimal asymptotic variance because iterates oscillate symmetrically around the minimum. Correspondence: SGD step → fixed-length SR step; noise ball → R38's plateau. Gap: the NQS landscape is non-convex with redundant (near-flat) parameter directions, along which the average can drift; and the decay slows the deterministic progress, which at polished points is already linear (L60). Prior use in VMC: parameter averaging over the last iterations is standard in Jastrow–Slater VMC (Becca & Sorella, *Quantum Monte Carlo Approaches for Correlated Systems*, CUP 2017), and learning-rate schedules are common in NQS; the novelty is only its use against a measured floor, so G0 counts it as an adopted tool, not a new method.
* **Outcome**: LIVE (T0; blocked on R51.1 unit 2).
* **T0 evidence so far**: the average of R38's three final parameter vectors had $R=0.548$ against 0.536 for the best replica (round 1 T1a, A2 WEAK): averaging helps a little and does not leave the manifold, but a 3-point average at R38's plateau is not a noise-ball measurement.
* **Pending kill rule**: promoted to T1 only if R51.1 returns STEP-NOISE (halving the length lowers the deficit by at least 25%); killed at T0 if R51.1 returns NOT-NOISE (the floor does not scale with noise, so shrinking the noise ball cannot remove it).
* **Sharpest Gap**: whether the decay can be fast enough to shrink the ball yet slow enough not to stall the linear deterministic progress (L60) within a few hundred steps.
* **Gate 4 claim**: no extra cost per step (one parameter vector of memory); whether the floor's dependence on step length persists at 24 sites is untested.
