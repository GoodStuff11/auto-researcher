# R51.7 — Robbins-Monro step decay with Polyak-Ruppert averaging (rmavg) (SHELVED)
* **The idea**: Replace energy-based step-length selection (whose held-out error exceeds one step's gain in R38's late steps, R51.6) by a schedule that needs no energies: a fixed metric length decaying as $\tau_t=\tau_0(t_0/(t_0+t))^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha\in(1/2,1)$, plus a running average of the parameters over the late steps. A constant step leaves the iterate in a noise ball whose size scales with the step; decaying it shrinks the ball, and averaging cancels the ball's spread without extra samples.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, cross-field translation from stochastic approximation (barrier B3); gated on R51.1's half-length arm D; killed at T2 in round 4, changed to SHELVED by the per-leaf critic (2026-11-17).
* **Source analogy**: Robbins–Monro stochastic approximation and Polyak–Ruppert averaging (Polyak & Juditsky, SIAM J. Control Optim. 30, 838, 1992): for stochastic gradients of a locally strongly convex objective the iterates oscillate in a ball of size proportional to the step, and averaging attains the optimal variance. Correspondence: SGD step → fixed-length SR step; noise ball → the shortfall left once a run levels off. Gap: non-convex landscape with near-flat directions; decay slows deterministic progress. Parameter averaging is standard in Jastrow–Slater VMC (Becca & Sorella, CUP 2017).
* **Outcome**: SHELVED (2026-11-17; untested in its premise). Job 773528 (3.4 CPU-h): UNRESOLVABLE, DECAY-HELPS excluded; 12 sites need 30,100 vs 31,800 fixed (ρ 0.95); 10 sites ρ ∈ [0.43, 1.71]; stays SHELVED (Addendum 1).
* **What was measured**: 12-site `che`, guided draws, 16,384 samples per step, exact scoring (jobs 759974, 761860). Halving the length and doubling the steps (arm D, $\tau=0.0025$, 240 steps) left a shortfall to the polished state of 0.147 of the gap, against 0.171 for the full length (arm A, 120 steps): ratio 0.858 against the 0.75 that RULE_B's STEP-NOISE clause needed to promote R51.7. At equal total samples D matched the 4-times-samples arm (3.9M draws: 0.606 vs 0.608).
* **Why shelved**: that clause tested the route outside its premise. Step decay with averaging targets the noise ball once a run has levelled off; no arm had (tail slopes 0.0005–0.0012 per step), and a fixed-horizon ratio kill stands only with a converged or equal-wall-time arm (L33), while D cost 2× A. The proper test (decay from a levelled-off fixed-step run) has no starting point yet. 
* **What would unshelve it**: a fixed-step run with last-20 slope $<2\times10^{-4}$ (per step, in $R$); then decay plus averaging from that state against the same run continued at fixed length, at equal samples. **Trigger met at 10 sites** (per-leaf critic, 2026-11-17): R51.8's 5x2 exact (noise-free) arm is a fixed-step limit cycle, last-40 slope $\approx-1.7\times10^{-4}$ in $R$.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: if the noise ball scales with the step, D's 14% drop implies the ball is about 28% of A's shortfall ($\approx0.05$ of the gap), not negligible; and at equal samples D matched the 4× arm.
* **Translation repair**: decay triggered by a levelled-off detector instead of a fixed schedule; dismissed at T0 as a separate route because it is the unshelve test itself.
