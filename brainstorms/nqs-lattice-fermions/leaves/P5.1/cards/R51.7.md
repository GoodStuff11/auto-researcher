# R51.7 — Robbins-Monro step decay with Polyak-Ruppert averaging (rmavg) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Replace energy-based step-length selection, which round 1 showed is noisier than one step's gain at the plateau, by a schedule that needs no energies: a fixed metric length decaying as $\tau_t=\tau_0(t_0/(t_0+t))^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha\in(1/2,1)$, plus a running average of the parameters over the late steps. A constant step leaves the iterate in a noise ball whose size scales with the step; decaying it shrinks the ball, and averaging cancels the ball's spread without extra samples.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, cross-field translation from stochastic approximation (barrier B3); gated on R51.1's half-length arm D.
* **Source analogy**: Robbins–Monro stochastic approximation and Polyak–Ruppert averaging (Polyak & Juditsky, SIAM J. Control Optim. 30, 838, 1992): for stochastic gradients of a locally strongly convex objective the iterates oscillate in a ball of size proportional to the step, and averaging attains the optimal variance. Correspondence: SGD step → fixed-length SR step; noise ball → R38's plateau. Gap: non-convex landscape with near-flat directions; decay slows deterministic progress. Parameter averaging is standard in Jastrow–Slater VMC (Becca & Sorella, CUP 2017).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T2 (2026-11-17), by R51.1's pre-registered STEP-NOISE clause (arm D).
* **Kill rule**: `tests/P5.1_noise_floor_r01/RULE_B.md`: "STEP-NOISE if Delta(D) ≤ 0.75 Delta(A) while D's tail slope is ≥ 0 ... R7 (decaying step length) is promoted to a T1 run"; `voi_unit2.md`: otherwise R51.7 is killed.
* **Evidence**: 12-site `che` (open $4\times3$ Hubbard, 5+5, $U=8$, $t'=-0.25$), guided draws, 16,384 samples per step, exact scoring (jobs 759974, 761860). Halving the length and doubling the steps (arm D, $\tau=0.0025$, 240 steps, equal path length) left a shortfall to the polished state of 0.147 of the gap, against 0.171 for the full length (arm A, 120 steps): ratio 0.858 against the 0.75 needed. At equal total samples D matched the 4-times-samples arm (3.9M draws: D 0.606, B 0.608), and the shortfall fell as samples per step$^{-0.87}$.
* **Why this kills it**: R51.7 assumes the shortfall is a noise ball whose size scales with the step. Halving the step removed only 14% of it, and the shortfall's dependence on samples (a stationary-ball model predicts 0.042 at 16 times the samples, 0.0155 was observed) shows noise slowing the path rather than a ball around a reached minimum; a decay schedule shrinks a ball that is not the main loss, and costs deterministic progress.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: decay starting late, once a run has truly levelled off (no arm here had: tail slopes 0.0005–0.0012 per step); averaging as a free final polish (R38's 3-replica average gained 0.012); other sizes.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: D's margin to the threshold (0.018 in $R$) is about one replica spread, and the rule's unit 3 would add a second seed; answered: a flip needs the second seed 1.9 spreads below the first (P about 0.03), so the compute gate refused unit 3 (G2) and the prediction stands.
