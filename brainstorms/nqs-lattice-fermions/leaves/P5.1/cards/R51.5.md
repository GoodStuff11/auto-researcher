# R51.5 — median-of-means SR step over sample blocks (mom) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Split the $N$ samples of an SR step into $K$ blocks, solve the SR system $S_b\,\delta_b=f_b$ in each block (metric and force from the same block, so their error cancellation is kept), and take the geometric median of the $K$ block steps. A median is robust to the rare near-node configurations that make the force heavy-tailed (L59), so the step should be less noisy than the mean at the same $N$.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, cross-field translation from robust statistics (barrier B2); killed at T0 in round 1, reopened by the per-leaf critic (2026-11-17) because R51.1 superseded the kill's premise.
* **Source analogy**: median-of-means estimators for heavy-tailed means (Lugosi & Mendelson, Found. Comput. Math. 19, 1145, 2019). Source: i.i.d. heavy-tailed gradients with many samples per block, so every block mean is accurate and the median only discards outlier blocks. Correspondence: block mean → block SR step. Gap: each block must itself give a usable SR step.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T1 (2026-11-18), on the premise arm's rise clause (job 769669); T2 never released.
* **Former kill**: T0 in round 1 (one-step Born probe, L59); reopened when R51.1 showed fixed-step guided SR descends at 6.2 samples per parameter (text in `log/round-08-R51.5.md`).
* **Kill rule**: pre-registered 2026-11-17, before any data; 12-site `che`, guided draws, fixed length $\tau=0.005$, exact scoring, seed 9000: "*Premise (T1)*: the fixed-step guided arm at $N/4=4096$ samples per step, 240 steps. KILLED if its $R$ at 0.98M total draws (step 240) is below 0.247 (half of the 16,384 arm's 0.494 at the same draws) or more than a third of its steps raise the exact energy." T2 ($K=4$) was gated on it.
* **Evidence**: $R$ = fraction of the gap from the start energy to the exact ground energy closed (R51.1's gap 0.022373). Job 769669 (`tests/P5.1_r51_5/score_premise.py`, `results/score_premise.json`), 4×3 `che` ($P$=2637, so 4096 draws is about 1.55 per parameter), 240 steps, 983,040 draws: $R(240)=0.495$ against the 0.247 line (the 16,384 arm: 0.494 at equal draws), last-20 mean 0.484; energy rose on 83 of 240 steps (0.346) against the line of 80, binomial sd at $p=1/3$ about 7.3. R51.1 arm A (16,384 per step) rose on 18 of 120.
* **Why this kills it**: the rise clause is met (83 > 80), so the premise fails under the rule. The premise was that a 4096-sample block step is reliable enough to be one of four votes; a block step that raises the energy on about a third of steps means a median of 4 blocks will often sit near an uphill step (with independent blocks, at least two of four are uphill with probability about 0.43), so the median is not safely downhill.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: larger blocks ($K=2$ of 8192, or $K=4$ at $N$=65,536); other robust estimators (trimmed means, coordinate medians, clipping of near-node forces); other sizes or a decaying step.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: 83 against 80 is a margin of about 0.4 binomial sd, and $R$ progress was as good as the 16,384 arm at equal draws (0.495 vs 0.494). Also, 5×2 fixed-step SR rose on about 0.39 of steps at every $N$ (prediction log), so rises may be generic to fixed-step SR near the bounce, not a small-block defect; against that, arm A at 12 sites rose on only 0.15. Unresolved; the pre-registered line stands.
* **Checked by**: per-leaf critic owed.
* **Lesson**: route-specific: the kill rests on a threshold margin at one size and one block size, so it does not generalise.
