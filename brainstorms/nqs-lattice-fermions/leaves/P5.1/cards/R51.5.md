# R51.5 — median-of-means SR step over sample blocks (mom) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Split the $N$ samples of an SR step into $K$ blocks, solve the SR system $S_b\,\delta_b=f_b$ in each block (metric and force from the same block, so their error cancellation is kept), and take the geometric median of the $K$ block steps. A median is robust to the rare near-node configurations that make the force heavy-tailed (L59), so the step should be less noisy than the mean at the same $N$.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, cross-field translation from robust statistics (barrier B2).
* **Source analogy**: median-of-means estimators for heavy-tailed means (Lugosi & Mendelson, Found. Comput. Math. 19, 1145, 2019; robust gradient descent, Prasad et al., JRSS-B 82, 601, 2020). Source: i.i.d. heavy-tailed gradients with many samples per block, so every block mean is already accurate and the median only discards outlier blocks. Correspondence: block mean → block SR step. Gap: each block must itself give a usable SR step.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T0 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: T0: "each block must have enough samples per parameter for its own SR step to carry signal; otherwise the median of noise is noise."
* **Evidence**: at the 12-site `che` with 16,384 samples (6.2 per parameter) a full-sample SR step recovers only $\bar R=0.016\pm0.022$ of an exact 0.288 gain (L59); with $K\ge3$ blocks each block has $\le2$ samples per parameter, where steps were pure noise (P4.1 R15 at 0.1 per parameter; L55 sampled LM at 3 per parameter raised the energy at every step). A median also has a bias of order the block spread for skewed distributions, and the near-node force distribution is skewed.
* **Why this kills it**: the median can only remove outlier blocks if most blocks are accurate; here every block is noise-dominated, so the median is a noisy, biased step with $K$ times fewer samples behind it.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: median-of-means at many samples per parameter (late, large-$N$ steps), or medians applied to scalar quantities such as a step-length score; trimming of individual samples' weights (close to R38's guided sampling).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: the median could be taken over the force only, with a shared metric, keeping more samples per block; answered: replacing the force alone breaks the $S$–$f$ cancellation and lost in R37 and R39 (L59).
* **Checked by**: round 1 worker (2026-11-17); per-leaf critic owed.
* **Lesson**: route-specific (an instance of L59's samples-per-parameter regime).
