# R51.5 — median-of-means SR step over sample blocks (mom) (LIVE)
* **The idea**: Split the $N$ samples of an SR step into $K$ blocks, solve the SR system $S_b\,\delta_b=f_b$ in each block (metric and force from the same block, so their error cancellation is kept), and take the geometric median of the $K$ block steps. A median is robust to the rare near-node configurations that make the force heavy-tailed (L59), so the step should be less noisy than the mean at the same $N$.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, cross-field translation from robust statistics (barrier B2); killed at T0 in round 1, reopened by the per-leaf critic (2026-11-17) because R51.1 superseded the kill's premise.
* **Source analogy**: median-of-means estimators for heavy-tailed means (Lugosi & Mendelson, Found. Comput. Math. 19, 1145, 2019; robust gradient descent, Prasad et al., JRSS-B 82, 601, 2020). Source: i.i.d. heavy-tailed gradients with many samples per block, so every block mean is accurate and the median only discards outlier blocks. Correspondence: block mean → block SR step. Gap: each block must itself give a usable SR step.
* **Outcome**: LIVE (untested). Next: premise arm gated, unsubmitted (`tests/P5.1_r51_5/`; log round-08-R51.5).
* **Former kill (T0, round 1)** and why it no longer holds: it rested on a one-step, Born-sampled, held-out probe (L59: 6.2 samples per parameter recovered $0.016\pm0.022$ of an exact 0.288 gain), on P4.1 R15 (0.1 samples per parameter) and on L55 (a different method). R51.1 later showed fixed-step guided SR at 6.2 samples per parameter descends steadily (arm A: 18 energy rises in 120 steps), and at equal total samples smaller batches won at every budget (0.98M draws: 16,384 per step 0.494 > 65,536 0.426 > 262,144 0.290), which points to blocks of about 2 samples per parameter still carrying signal. Near-node forces have infinite variance (Trail, Phys. Rev. E 77, 016703, 2008), so heavy tails persist at block level, median-of-means' best case.
* **Kill rule (pre-registered 2026-11-17, before any data)**, 12-site `che`, guided draws, fixed length $\tau=0.005$, exact scoring, seed 9000, $R$ and $\Delta=0.7475-$(mean $R$ of last 20 steps) as in R51.1:
  - *Premise (T1)*: the fixed-step guided arm at $N/4=4096$ samples per step (the arm R51.1's equal-sample trend also needs), 240 steps. KILLED if its $R$ at 0.98M total draws (step 240) is below 0.247 (half of the 16,384 arm's 0.494 at the same draws) or more than a third of its steps raise the exact energy.
  - *Decisive (T2, only if the premise survives)*: $K=4$ blocks of 4096 per 16,384-sample step, geometric median, 120 steps, against R51.1 arm A ($\Delta_A=0.171$) at equal total samples. PASS if $\Delta_{\rm MoM}\le0.128$ ($0.75\Delta_A$), KILLED if $\Delta_{\rm MoM}\ge0.171$, AMBIGUOUS between.
* **Sharpest Gap**: a median of skewed block steps is biased by about the block spread; whether robustness beats that bias at 1.55 samples per parameter per block is the unknown.
* **Gate 4 claim**: per step the cost of $K$ SR solves on $N/K$ samples, $\mathcal O(NP^2+KP^3)$, plus a Weiszfeld median ($\mathcal O(KP)$ per iteration); the block size needed scales with the samples per parameter that carry signal, measured only at 12 sites.
* **Translation repair**: none needed while LIVE; the round-1 repair candidate (median of the force alone over a shared metric) is dismissed at T0, since replacing the force alone breaks the $S$–$f$ cancellation (R37, R39, L59).
