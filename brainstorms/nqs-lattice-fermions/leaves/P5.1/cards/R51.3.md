# R51.3 — leverage-score row sampling of the metric (levrow) (LIVE)
* **The idea**: Build the parameter metric $S=Y^{\mathsf T}Y$ of a polishing step from a small random subset of configurations (rows of the Jacobian $Y$), drawn with probability proportional to half their wave-function weight plus half their ridge leverage $\ell_r=y_r^{\mathsf T}(S+\lambda I)^{-1}y_r$, with importance weights. Leverage sampling gives a relative spectral approximation in every direction, including the small-eigenvalue directions where the polishing gain lies (L29); weight (Born) sampling misses the rare configurations that carry them. 13% of the rows would cut the metric's cost about 7-fold, the saving P4.1 R28 needs.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, cross-field translation from randomised numerical linear algebra (barriers B2 and B4), after R51.2's timing.
* **Source analogy**: leverage-score row sampling for least squares and spectral sparsification (Drineas, Mahoney & Muthukrishnan, SODA 2006; Spielman & Srivastava, SIAM J. Comput. 40, 1913, 2011) and subsampled Newton methods (Roosta-Khorasani & Mahoney, Math. Prog. 174, 293, 2019). Source: a tall matrix whose Gram is needed to relative accuracy; sampling $\mathcal O(d\log d/\epsilon^2)$ rows by leverage gives $(1\pm\epsilon)$ spectral accuracy, because leverage measures how much each row matters to some direction. Correspondence: rows → configurations, $d$ → effective dimension $\sum\ell=2163$. Gap: scores need the metric itself, and in VMC configurations are drawn by Markov chains from $|\psi|^2$, not from arbitrary $q$.
