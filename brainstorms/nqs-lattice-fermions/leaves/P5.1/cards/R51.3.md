# R51.3 — leverage-score row sampling of the metric (levrow) (LIVE)
* **The idea**: Build the parameter metric $S=Y^{\mathsf T}Y$ (the Gram matrix of per-configuration log-derivative rows $Y$) of an exact-sum polishing step from a small random subset of rows, drawn with probability proportional to half their wave-function weight plus half their ridge leverage $\ell_r=y_r^{\mathsf T}(S+\lambda I)^{-1}y_r$, with importance weights. Leverage sampling approximates the metric to relative accuracy in every direction, including the small-eigenvalue ones where the polishing gain lies (L29); weight (Born) sampling misses the rare rows that carry them. 13% of the rows would cut the metric's cost about 7-fold (the saving P4.1 R28 needs).
* **Lineage**: initial batch, cross-field translation from randomised numerical linear algebra (barriers B2, B4), after R51.2's timing; also the repair claimed for R51.4.
* **Source analogy**: leverage-score row sampling and spectral sparsification (Drineas, Mahoney & Muthukrishnan, SODA 2006; Spielman & Srivastava, SIAM J. Comput. 40, 1913, 2011), subsampled Newton (Roosta-Khorasani & Mahoney, Math. Prog. 174, 293, 2019): $\mathcal O(d\log d/\epsilon^2)$ rows sampled by leverage give $(1\pm\epsilon)$ spectral accuracy of a tall matrix's Gram. Rows → configurations, $d$ → effective dimension $\sum\ell=2163$. Gap: scores need the metric itself, and VMC draws configurations from $|\psi|^2$ by Markov chains.
* **Outcome**: LIVE (T1, T1b PASS; T1c MARGINAL 2.40×; T1d MARGINAL $\rho=2.37$ cross-machine, superseded by T1d′ PASS $\rho'=3.53$ same machine, 2026-11-17; 2.22 with the score build charged in full). Off-question: it lowers exact-sum (unscalable) polishing cost, not samples; matters only for P4.1-style 12-site comparisons.
