# R51.3 — leverage-score row sampling of the metric (levrow) (LIVE)
* **The idea**: Build the parameter metric $S=Y^{\mathsf T}Y$ of a polishing step from a small random subset of configurations (rows of the Jacobian $Y$), drawn with probability proportional to half their wave-function weight plus half their ridge leverage $\ell_r=y_r^{\mathsf T}(S+\lambda I)^{-1}y_r$, with importance weights. Leverage sampling gives a relative spectral approximation in every direction, including the small-eigenvalue directions where the polishing gain lies (L29); weight (Born) sampling misses the rare configurations that carry them. 13% of the rows would cut the metric's cost about 7-fold, the saving P4.1 R28 needs.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, cross-field translation from randomised numerical linear algebra (barriers B2 and B4), after R51.2's timing.
* **Source analogy**: leverage-score row sampling for least squares and spectral sparsification (Drineas, Mahoney & Muthukrishnan, SODA 2006; Spielman & Srivastava, SIAM J. Comput. 40, 1913, 2011) and subsampled Newton methods (Roosta-Khorasani & Mahoney, Math. Prog. 174, 293, 2019). Source: a tall matrix whose Gram is needed to relative accuracy; sampling $\mathcal O(d\log d/\epsilon^2)$ rows by leverage gives $(1\pm\epsilon)$ spectral accuracy, because leverage measures how much each row matters to some direction. Correspondence: rows → configurations, $d$ → effective dimension $\sum\ell=2163$. Gap: scores need the metric itself, and in VMC configurations are drawn by Markov chains from $|\psi|^2$, not from arbitrary $q$.
* **Outcome**: LIVE (T1 and T1b PASS, 2026-11-17).
* **T1 evidence** (`tests/P5.1_rowsample_r01/RULE.md`, polished 12-site `che`, exact force, 12-candidate step grid, $m=8P=21{,}096$ rows of 157,216, 2 draws): fraction of the exact metric's best step gain ($1.255\times10^{-4}$): leverage $\phi=1.037,0.958$; guided $0.731,0.720$; Born $0.235,0.218$. Rule PASS ($\ge0.8$ and $\ge$ Born + 0.15).
* **T1b evidence** (`RULE_stale.md`): scores from a point one polishing step away, $\phi=0.985,0.996$ (PASS); from the SR-trained start about 20 exact steps away, $\phi=0.948$ (one draw, reported); top-10% leverage sets overlap the target's by 0.994 and 0.927. The scores barely move during polishing, so they can be computed once.
* **Pending kill rule (T1c, to be pre-registered next round)**: 10 polishing steps of the cutoff-ladder metric step from 8P leverage-sampled rows (scores from the start, exact force), against the exact ladder's rate $\gamma_{\rm LM}=7.27\times10^{-6}$ relative energy per CPU-minute; PASS at $\ge3\gamma_{\rm LM}$ (P4.1 ledger row 2's bar), KILL below $1.5\gamma_{\rm LM}$.
* **Sharpest Gap**: (i) whether the per-step gain holds over many steps at about 0.35 CPU-min (Jacobian of 21k rows 0.15, Gram 0.03, eigensolve 0.04, exact gradient 0.13); (ii) for sampled VMC at large size, drawing configurations by leverage needs each proposal's log-derivative row, and whether $|\psi|^2(a+\ell)$ can be sampled at polynomial extra cost is untested.
* **Gate 4 claim**: rows needed scale with the effective dimension ($\sim P\log P$), not with the Hilbert space, so the cost per metric is $\mathcal O(P\log P)$ Jacobian rows plus $\mathcal O(P^3)$, polynomial; the gain over Born sampling is per step, not a finite-size bonus, if leverage stays concentrated on rare configurations at larger $V$ (untested beyond 12 sites).
* **Gate 0**: no NQS/VMC use found (searches 2026-11-17); guided sampling (R38) is a cruder fixed version. G1–G3 PASS.
