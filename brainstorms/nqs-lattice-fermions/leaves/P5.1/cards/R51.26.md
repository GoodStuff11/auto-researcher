# R51.26 — noise-level spectral filter of the SR step (snrcut) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Change the step's geometry, not its estimator: in the eigenbasis of the sampled metric, estimate each mode's force signal-to-noise ratio $\rho_k$ from 16 batches of the same draws and drop (soft cut $1/(1+(\rho_c/\rho_k)^6)$) the modes where noise dominates, then take the usual fixed-length step. Plain SR divides noise in small-eigenvalue modes by $\lambda_k+10^{-3}$ and spends its fixed length on it; if the noise-free path's gain sits in high-SNR modes, the need would track that signal, not the occupied support. With $N=\infty$ the filter is the identity, so R51.8's exact arm is its noise-free reference.
* **Lineage**: round 16 batch, cross-field translation from statistical inverse problems (Analogy Map B5); Wiener-shrinkage translation repair tested on the same rule.
* **Source analogy**: spectral cut-off regularisation with the cut set by the noise level (Engl, Hanke & Neubauer 1996; Bissantz et al., SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 45, 2610, 2007); in tVMC as SNR regularisation (Schmitt & Heyl, PRL 125, 100503, 2020). Operator → $S$, data → $f$, noise level → per-mode batch sd. Works there when the signal decays faster than the noise over the spectrum (source condition). Gap: iterated steps; no known source condition here.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T1 (2026-11-18).
* **Kill rule**: `tests/P5.1_r16_gen/RULE_snrcut.md` (before any arm): $g=N_{\rm eq}/N$ from R51.8's plain seed-9000 $s(N)$ curve; PASS $g\ge2$ at 4096 and 16,384, KILL $g\le1.25$ at both. Addendum (after the KILL, before the repair arm): Wiener shrinkage $\rho^2/(1+\rho^2)$ at 4096, same lines.
* **Evidence**: 5×2, guided, $\tau$ 0.005, 120 steps, seeds 9000+n, against R51.8's exact arm ($G_{\rm ref}$ 0.0016011; `results/score_r16.json`). $\rho_c$=1: $s$ 0.520 at 4096 (plain 0.445), 0.176 at 16,384 (plain 0.141) → $g$ 0.75, 0.76; median kept modes 327 and 621 of 1832. $\rho_c$=2 at 4096: $s$ 0.693, $g$ 0.44 (63 kept). Wiener at 4096: $s$ 0.485, $g$ 0.85. Plain seed spread 0.008 in $s$. Mechanism arm (addendum 2, round 17): cut $\rho_c$=1 at 4096 with plain SR's normalisation (kept modes not lengthened; step 0.84 of $\tau$): $s$ 0.503, $g$ 0.80 (`score_r17_snrP.json`). 16 CPU-min in all.
* **Why this kills it**: every filtered arm needs more samples than plain SR, and the loss grows with the filter's strength ($g$ 0.85 → 0.75 → 0.44). Deleting the low-SNR components from the plain step itself, without lengthening the rest, still loses ($g$ 0.80 < 0.95), so the loss is not a renormalisation artefact: those components carry gain.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: 6×2 and larger, where the SNR spectrum may differ (untested); SNR filtering for stability rather than sample count (its tVMC use).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: the deciding arms renormalised to length $\tau$, so lengthening the high-SNR part in an overshooting regime could explain $g<1$ (critic 16). Answered: the unlengthened cut still gives $g$ 0.80; its step is 16% shorter, a residual length difference of the size that moved R51.1 by 14%. One seed, one size.
* **Checked by**: per-leaf critic round 16 (2026-11-18): upheld at 10 sites; objection 5 tested round 17 (mechanism stands, $g$ 0.80).
* **Lesson**: L76: at 10 sites the plain SR step's components in modes with per-step force SNR below 1 carry gain, so noise-level filters (cut or Wiener) raised the need ×1.2–2.3.
