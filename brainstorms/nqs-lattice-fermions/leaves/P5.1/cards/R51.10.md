# R51.10 — ridge-regression noise-to-signal ratio as sample-need predictor (kappa) (LIVE)
* **The idea**: The SR direction $x=(S+\lambda)^{-1}f$ is a ridge regression of the local energy on the log-derivatives. Learning theory gives its sample complexity in closed form: $N$ draws from $q$ leave an expected squared error $\mathrm{tr}[A C_q A S]/N$ in the metric norm, $A=(S+\lambda)^{-1}$, $C_q$ the importance-weighted covariance of the per-sample residual terms. Its ratio to $x^{\mathsf T}Sx$, $\kappa_q$, is computable exactly from one state at each exact-sum size in minutes. If $\kappa$'s growth with size predicts the measured multi-step need's growth, $\kappa$ at 14 sites (one exact metric) and a sampled $\kappa$ at 24 sites replace multi-step ladders.
* **Lineage**: Operation B (round 4), cross-field translation from statistical learning theory (barrier B2 and the size question); scored from R51.8's arms.
* **Source analogy**: random-design ridge regression and kernel ridge regression, where the excess risk is (effective dimension $d_\lambda=\mathrm{tr}\,S(S+\lambda)^{-1}$) × (noise variance) / $N$ (Hsu, Kakade & Zhang, Found. Comput. Math. 14, 569, 2014; Caponnetto & De Vito, Found. Comput. Math. 7, 331, 2007; Bach, COLT 2013). Correspondence: features → $O$, response → $E_{\rm loc}$, design covariance → $S$, ridge → SR shift. Gap: the theory assumes light tails and i.i.d. draws; here $O$ diverges at nodes (Born $\kappa$ is dominated by rare configurations) and the step is iterated, so one-state $\kappa$ may not track a multi-step need.
