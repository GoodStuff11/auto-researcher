# R51.2 — sketched low-rank (Nystrom) metric for exact-sum polishing (sketch) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Replace the exact metric build of an exact-sum polishing step (the parameter metric $S=Y^{\mathsf T}Y$ over all 157,216 orbit representatives, 1.9 CPU-min for the 12-site `che`) by a randomised Nystrom sketch $S\Omega$ of rank $k$, or use that sketch only as a preconditioner for conjugate gradients on the stored rows, so that metric-whitened gradient steps (P4.1 R28) become 3 times faster than the cutoff-ladder linear method, which needs a metric at most 0.33 CPU-min.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, seed (b) from P4.1 R28's T0 kill ("a metric 5 or more times cheaper than the exact build"); cross-field translation from randomised numerical linear algebra.
* **Source analogy**: Randomised Nystrom approximation and preconditioning (Frangella, Tropp & Udell, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 44, 718, 2023; for natural gradient in PINNs, arXiv:2505.11638). Source: a positive semidefinite system whose matrix-vector products are cheap and whose spectrum decays fast, so a rank-$k$ sketch with $k\ll P$ captures it. Correspondence: Gram/kernel matrix → metric $S$; matvec → $Y^{\mathsf T}(Yv)$. Gap predicted: the rows of $Y$ (per-configuration log-derivatives) may cost more than the products.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T1 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: `tests/P5.1_rowsample_r01/RULE.md`, Part 1, written before the run: "KILLED at T1 if $J\ge0.33$ CPU-min, since every such method computes all Jacobian rows first."
* **Evidence**: one-thread timing at the polished `che` (log `tests/P5.1_rowsample_r01/logs/rowsample_all2.txt`, two runs agree within 3%): Jacobian rows 1.14 CPU-min, Gram product 0.23, eigensolve 0.04, one exact energy 0.045. T0 rank argument: the cutoff ladder keeps ranks 1225–1752 of 2637 (L29), so a sketch of rank $k\ge1225$ costs $2nPk\ge0.93$ of the Gram product, saving nothing even there.
* **Why this kills it**: any sketch built from the full Jacobian costs at least 1.14 CPU-min, 3.5 times the 0.33 needed, before any product is formed; and the products it could save are 12% of the build.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: methods that compute fewer Jacobian rows (row subsampling, R51.3); a cheaper Jacobian (forward-mode or fused per-sample gradients), which would change the timing premise; larger systems where $P^3$ dominates.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: the Jacobian code (per-row replicated parameters with autograd) may be far from optimal, so a compiled kernel could put J under 0.33. Unresolved; even then, the rank argument leaves the Gram saving below 1.1-fold, so only a 6-fold faster Jacobian would reopen it, and that speed-up would benefit the exact ladder equally.
* **Checked by**: round 1 worker; per-leaf critic (2026-11-17): upheld.
* **Lesson**: proposed library entry: in exact-sum polishing of NQS the metric's cost is the per-configuration Jacobian, not its products, so sketching the products cannot make it cheaper.
