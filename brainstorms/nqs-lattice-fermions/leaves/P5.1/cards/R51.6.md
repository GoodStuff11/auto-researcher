# R51.6 — stochastic-trust-region step acceptance (storm) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Keep R38's guided sampled SR step and held-out step-length ladder, but accept the chosen length only if its held-out energy drop exceeds twice its own standard error; otherwise skip. This should remove the steps that raised the exact energy in R38's late steps while keeping the real drops, as probabilistic trust-region methods do.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, cross-field translation from stochastic derivative-free optimisation (barrier B3), motivated by round 1's T1a reading that step selection caused most of R38's plateau (a reading withdrawn by the per-leaf critic, 2026-11-17: R38 progressed as fast as the fixed-step arm at equal samples and was stopped, not stalled; R51.1).
* **Source analogy**: STORM, stochastic trust region with probabilistic models (Chen, Menickelly & Scheinberg, Math. Prog. 169, 447, 2018; Paquette & Scheinberg, SIAM J. Optim. 30, 349, 2020): accept a step only when a function estimate, accurate with fixed probability, confirms sufficient decrease. It works because the estimate's accuracy is tightened in proportion to the step's predicted decrease. Correspondence: function estimate → correlated-sampling held-out energy. Gap: here the held-out accuracy is fixed by $N$ and does not tighten with the decrease.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T1 (T1a, 2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: `tests/P5.1_noise_floor_r01/RULE_A.md`, A3, written before the analysis: "KILLED at T1a if [the mean gain of the 2-sigma rule over steps 41-60] is $\le$ the mean realised gain."
* **Evidence**: stored candidates of job 752885 (3 replicas, steps 41–60, 16,384 guided samples, exact energies of every candidate): median held-out error 4.6e-4 against a median selected drop of 2.3e-4 energy units; the 2-sigma rule never fired, so its gain was 0.000 against 0.0020 of the gap per step realised (and 0.0078 for always taking the shortest length 0.005). Log `round-01`; `results/floor_diag.json`.
* **Why this kills it**: a step's true gain in R38's late steps (about 0.008 of the gap, 1.8e-4 energy units) is below the held-out error at this $N$, so a confirmation test rejects every step and the optimiser stops; making it fire needs about 25 times the held-out samples.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: acceptance tests at larger $N$; a test pooled over many steps; dropping energy-based selection altogether (R51.1 arm A, fixed length), which the same data favour.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: A3 is a one-step counterfactual at states made by the old rule; answered: the rule would also never fire at the new states unless their per-step gains were 4 times larger, which the stored candidates (largest gains about 0.01 of the gap) exclude.
* **Checked by**: round 1 worker; per-leaf critic (2026-11-17): kill upheld; Lesson objection 1 (applied: proposed lesson withdrawn).
* **Lesson**: route-specific. The proposed entry ("selection itself makes the floor") is withdrawn: held-out selection is noisier than one step's gain, but at equal samples it did not slow R38 relative to a fixed step (R51.1, T1a).
