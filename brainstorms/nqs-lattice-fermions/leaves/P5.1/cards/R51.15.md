# R51.15 — noise-scale-driven samples-per-step schedule (adaptN) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: R51.1 found that at equal total samples small batches win early, but only large batches reached the last few percent within 120 steps. So grow the samples per step during training: start small and raise $N$ when the measured noise-to-signal of the step (sampled $\kappa$, R51.10, or the gradient noise scale) exceeds $N$.
* **Lineage**: round 5 batch, cross-field translation from stochastic optimisation (Analogy Map B3/B5), Operation A of R51.7 (decay samples instead of step length).
* **Source analogy**: dynamic sample-size methods (Byrd, Chin, Nocedal & Wu, Math. Prog. 134, 127, 2012; Bollapragada, Byrd & Nocedal, SIAM J. Optim. 28, 3312, 2018) and the critical batch size (McCandlish et al., arXiv:1812.06162): enlarge the batch when the gradient's sampling error exceeds a fraction of its norm. Gradient → SR direction, norm test → $\hat\kappa/N\le\theta^2$. Gap: heavy-tailed rows make $\hat\kappa$ itself noisy.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T1 (2026-11-18, orchestrator decision), on the censoring clause; T1 had passed only as an optimistic bound; no schedule run was built.
* **Kill rule**: the rule lived on this card's Sharpest Gap and the leaf's portfolio row, not in a RULE file; both were written before job 769213 was submitted (card a2fd002, row 5380e13; submission b560aec, 2026-11-17). Row: "the long small-$N$ run reaches 95% as cheaply → the schedule's gain was censoring; otherwise build the schedule run (T2)". Card: "a small-$N$ arm run longer may reach 0.95 as cheaply. R51.16's guided 16k continuation … measures exactly this". T1's own rule (`tests/P5.1_r05_gen/RULE_adaptN.md`: PASS if the oracle gain is ≥ 1.5 at 0.90 or 0.95 $R_{\rm pol}$) stated that the envelope "can kill the route but not promote it beyond T1".
* **Evidence**: 4x3 `che`, $\tau$ 0.005, guided draws, gap 0.022373, first crossing (the envelope's statistic). T1 oracle envelope over R51.1's 120-step arms: 4.8M / 12.1M draws to 0.90 / 0.95 $R_{\rm pol}$ (best fixed 6.75M / 22.0M; gain 1.41 / 1.83). Fixed 16,384 continued 332 more steps (task 0 of 769213): 4.26M / 6.67M (sustained 4.51M / 6.93M) (`log/round-09-scoring.md`).
* **Why this kills it**: the envelope is the cheapest any schedule could do given those arms; one fixed small $N$, run longer, beat it at both targets (0.89× and 0.55× its draws). The T1 gain came from stopping the small-$N$ arms at 120 steps, not from a need for larger $N$; the row's censoring branch applies.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: schedules aimed past 0.95 $R_{\rm pol}$ (the step's period-2 bounce, not $N$, sets the last percent, R51.8); larger sizes, where a fixed small $N$ may stall (R51.8's size ladder); schedules on the step length or $N<P$ (MinSR).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: one seed of the continued arm, and first crossings favour lucky fluctuations. Answered: sustained crossings (4.51M, 6.93M) also beat the envelope, which is itself optimistic (it picks the best arm per bin); a real schedule would pay more than it.
* **Checked by**: orchestrator kill decision 2026-11-18 (git history of the criterion verified by the leaf worker); per-leaf critic sign-off pending.
* **Lesson**: proposed entry (round-9 scoring): an oracle samples-per-step envelope built on runs stopped at a fixed step count can be a censoring artefact; continue the smallest-$N$ arm before crediting a schedule.
