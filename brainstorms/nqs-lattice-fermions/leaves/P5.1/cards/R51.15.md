# R51.15 — noise-scale-driven samples-per-step schedule (adaptN) (LIVE)
* **The idea**: R51.1 found that at equal total samples small batches win early, but only large batches reach the last few percent (95% of the polished gain needed 262,144 per step). So grow the samples per step during training instead of fixing them: start small and raise $N$ when the measured noise-to-signal of the step (sampled $\kappa$, R51.10, or the gradient noise scale) exceeds $N$. This replaces R51.7's step-length decay, killed because the shortfall is not a noise ball around a reached minimum, with a schedule in samples, which R51.1 showed is what controls the shortfall.
* **Lineage**: round 5 batch, cross-field translation from stochastic optimisation (Analogy Map B3/B5, untried entry), Operation A of R51.7 (swap the decayed quantity from step length to samples).
* **Source analogy**: dynamic sample-size methods (Byrd, Chin, Nocedal & Wu, Math. Prog. 134, 127, 2012, the "norm test"; Bollapragada, Byrd & Nocedal, SIAM J. Optim. 28, 3312, 2018) and the critical batch size (McCandlish et al., arXiv:1812.06162): enlarge the batch when the gradient's sampling error exceeds a fixed fraction of its norm. Correspondence: gradient → SR direction, norm test → $\hat\kappa/N\le\theta^2$. Gap: heavy-tailed rows make the sampled $\hat\kappa$ itself noisy; VMC searches (2026-11-17) found $|\psi|^\alpha$ adaptive sampling (Misery, arXiv:2507.05352) but no adaptive samples-per-step schedule.
* **Outcome**: LIVE (T1 PASS on an optimistic bound, 2026-11-17).
* **T1** (`tests/P5.1_r05_gen/RULE_adaptN.md`, stored data of R51.1's 4x3 arms; `envelope.json`): the bin-by-bin oracle switch reaches 0.90 $R_{\rm pol}$ with 4.8M draws against 6.75M for the best fixed $N$ (gain 1.41) and 0.95 $R_{\rm pol}$ with 12.1M against 22.0M (gain 1.83) → **PASS** (rule: $\ge1.5$ at 0.90 or 0.95). Diagnostics (not scored): the bin-by-bin switch picks noise below $R\approx0.5$, so the best monotone schedule (16k to $R$ 0.58, 65k to 0.68, then 262k) is the honest bound: gains 1.43, 1.24, 1.81 at 0.80/0.90/0.95; on R51.8's finished 5x2 guided arms (targets relative to its exact arm) 1.27, 1.07, 1.94.
* **Sharpest Gap**: the gain at 0.95 comes from the small-$N$ arms being stopped at 120 steps (censored), not from a proven need for larger $N$; a small-$N$ arm run longer may reach 0.95 as cheaply. R51.16's guided 16k continuation (292 more steps, same job) measures exactly this, so R51.15's T2 is read from it before any schedule run is built.
* **Gate 4 claim**: per-step cost unchanged (SR's); the gain is a constant factor in total samples (≤ about 2) at any size if the noise-scale rule transfers; not a scaling change.
* **Gate 0**: dynamic sample sizes are standard in stochastic optimisation and practised informally in VMC (more samples at the end); no measured schedule against the representable minimum found. G1–G3 PASS.
