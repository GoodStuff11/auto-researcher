# R51.9 — one-step direction-fidelity proxy for the size scaling (phiscale) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Replace R51.8's multi-step ladders by a one-step screen: at each size's SR-trained start, draw $N$ samples, take one fixed-length guided SR step and score the fraction $\phi(N)$ of the exact step's energy gain it keeps (as round 1's T1 did for metrics); $N_\phi(V)$ = the $N$ with $\phi\ge0.9$, its growth with $V$ standing in for the growth of the multi-step need. Cost: one exact metric per size plus seconds per draw, so 14 sites would cost minutes instead of hours.
* **Lineage**: Operation B (round 4), generated as the cheaper alternative to R51.8; killed at T0 in the same round.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T0 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 2 / T0 question: can a one-step quantity at the start stand in for the samples a multi-step run needs to reach a fixed fraction of its reference?
* **Evidence**: on the 12-site `che`, the one-step counterfactual rate of the fixed length 0.005 at R38's states was $0.0078\pm0.0006$ of the gap per step (round 1, T1a), while the fixed-length optimiser at its own states of similar $R$ gained 0.0020–0.0025 (R51.1 arm A, steps 60–80): 3–4× lower. R51.1's arms also show that what limits the multi-step run is accumulated noise along the path (a stationary-ball model is off by 2.7× at $16N$), which one step from one state does not see. And from R51.1's start, step 1 kept $\phi$ = 0.45, 0.57, 0.82 of the exact step's gain (0.112 of the gap; `logs/preflight_S4x3_exact.txt`) at $N$, $4N$, $16N$ (jobs 759974, 761860), so $N_\phi(0.9)>262{,}144$, while the multi-step run reached 98% of the polished value at 262,144 and 95% at an interpolated 115,000: one step overstates the need here by more than 2×, the opposite direction from the 3–4× rate gap, so no fixed conversion exists even at one size.
* **Why this kills it**: the proxy measures fidelity where the optimiser is not limited, and the only calibration available (one size) already shows one-step quantities off by 2–4× in both directions; its size trend could therefore not be read as the sample need's trend without the multi-step data it was meant to replace.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: a one-state predictor calibrated against multi-step data at two sizes (R51.10, which uses the exact error variance rather than a one-step gain); one-step screens at states sampled along the multi-step path.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: a ratio across sizes might be right even if each value is 3–4× off; unresolved in general, which is why R51.10 is tested against R51.8's measured ratio before it replaces anything.
* **Checked by**: round 4 worker; per-leaf critic (2026-11-17): upheld.
* **Lesson**: proposed library entry (one-step counterfactual rates overstate sustained rates 3–4×; round 3).
