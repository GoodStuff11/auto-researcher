# R51.24 — multilevel Monte Carlo across cluster sizes or network widths (mlmc) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Estimate the force at the target as a cheap coarse-level force plus a correction from coupled coarse/fine samples (multilevel Monte Carlo), with levels being cluster sizes or network widths, so most samples are spent where they are cheap and the expensive level only resolves a small-variance difference.
* **Lineage**: round 12 batch, cross-field translation from stochastic-PDE numerics; replaced by R51.25 (Operation B).
* **Source analogy**: multilevel Monte Carlo (Giles, Oper. Res. 56, 607, 2008): the telescoping sum $E[P_L]=E[P_0]+\sum_l E[P_l-P_{l-1}]$ with $P_l,P_{l-1}$ computed on the same random path, so ${\rm Var}(P_l-P_{l-1})$ falls with $l$. Correspondence: mesh level → cluster size or width. Gap: a coupling (same random input at both levels) and the same estimand at each level.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T0 (2026-11-18).
* **Kill rule**: T0: there must be a coupled pair of estimators whose difference has small variance and whose coarse mean is cheaper to estimate.
* **Evidence**: (i) sizes: the $V$-site force is a vector over the $V$-site network's parameters evaluated on $V$-site configurations; the $V'$-site force lives on a different space, there is no telescoping identity and no shared random input (no NQS/VMC use found, search 2026-11-18). (ii) Widths: the narrow network's force is either over a different parameter vector or, for a nested sub-network, a subset of components, so it controls no other component; as a control variate on shared samples its mean is unknown and must be estimated, which is the cheap-surrogate debiasing of L25 (surrogate error variances 2.3–466× ${\rm Var}(E_L)$, saving ≤4%).
* **Why this kills it**: no coupling of the same estimand exists across sizes, and across widths the construction reduces to a surrogate control variate already measured to save ≤4%.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: multilevel schemes in time-step or in chain length (no such level structure exists for i.i.d. VMC); MLMC on a single observable with an exact coarse model.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: transfer across sizes as a warm start is useful, but it is not a variance reduction of one step's force (L43 scores warm starts separately).
* **Gate 4 (explicit)**: no estimator to scale.
* **Checked by**: per-leaf critic round 15 (2026-11-18): upheld.
* **Lesson**: L25 (surrogate control variates), route-specific otherwise.
