# R51.12 — sketched ridge-leverage row selection for the sampled metric (sketchlev) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Make R51.3 usable without exact sums: estimate each sampled configuration's ridge leverage $\ell_r=y_r^{\mathsf T}(\hat S+\lambda)^{-1}y_r$ from a sketch of the sampled rows themselves (fast approximate leverage via a random projection, or leverage of a cheap surrogate), then build the metric from the high-leverage subset of the step's samples with importance weights, keeping R51.3's 99.8% step fidelity at a fraction of the metric cost.
* **Lineage**: round 5 batch, cross-field translation from randomised numerical linear algebra (barrier B4), a scalable repair of R51.3's need for every configuration's row.
* **Source analogy**: fast leverage-score approximation by Johnson–Lindenstrauss sketches (Drineas, Magdon-Ismail, Mahoney & Woodruff, JMLR 13, 3475, 2012) and recursive ridge-leverage sampling (Cohen, Musco & Musco, SODA 2017). There, rows are cheap to read, the Gram is the expense, and a sketch costs $\mathcal O(NP\log P)$ instead of $\mathcal O(NP^2)$. Gap: in sampled VMC every drawn row is already computed for the force.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T0 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 4 / T0 operation count: the route must save at least a factor 2 per step at equal step fidelity.
* **Evidence**: (i) the force $f=\langle O_c\,e\rangle$ needs the row $O(x)$ of every sampled configuration, so selecting rows for $\hat S$ saves only the Gram product, never the rows (R51.3's saving at exact sums was the rows: 1.14 of 1.9 CPU-min, B4). (ii) Leverage of $N$ sampled rows needs $(\hat S+\lambda)^{-1}y_r$ for each, $\mathcal O(NP^2)$, the same as the Gram it would save; a $k$-column sketch still costs $\mathcal O(NPk)$. (iii) At 24 sites the local energy is 85–90% of the step (P4.1 R34), so the Gram's share bounds any saving to about 10%. (iv) A metric from fewer rows than the force breaks the $S$–$f$ error cancellation (P4.1 R35, R37), and the exact metric itself recovers none of the sampled step's loss (L59).
* **Why this kills it**: the saving is capped by the Gram share of a step (≤ about 10–15% here) and the leverage estimate costs about as much, so no factor 2 exists; and NOISE-FLOOR (R51.1) says the force, not the metric, limits.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: leverage as a sampling target for the force (R51.11 covers it); exact-sum polishing at small sizes (R51.3 itself); very large $P$ at small $N$, where the Gram ($NP^2$) could dominate, though MinSR's $N^2P$ form already handles that.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: with $P\gg$ the row cost per parameter, the Gram grows as $P^2$ against the row's $P$; answered: MinSR's sample-space solve removes that regime without any leverage.
* **Checked by**: round 5 worker (2026-11-17); per-leaf critic owed.
* **Lesson**: proposed library entry: row subsampling of the metric pays only where rows are computed for the metric alone; in sampled VMC every drawn row feeds the force, so the saving is bounded by the Gram share.
