# R51.4 — semistochastic metric: exact top-weight rows plus sampled tail (semistoch) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Split the configurations into a deterministic set $D$ of the $K$ largest-weight configurations, whose contributions to the metric and the force are summed exactly, and a sampled remainder drawn from the rest. The exact part removes the variance of the dominant configurations, so fewer samples should reach the same step quality than plain $|\psi|^2$ sampling.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, cross-field translation from projector quantum Monte Carlo (barrier B4 of the Analogy Map).
* **Source analogy**: semistochastic FCIQMC (Petruzielo, Holmes, Changlani, Nightingale & Umrigar, PRL 109, 230201, 2012; Blunt et al., J. Chem. Phys. 142, 184107, 2015): the projector is applied exactly inside a deterministic space and sampled outside. It worked there because molecular ground states concentrate most weight on a small set of determinants, so the deterministic space removes most variance. Gap: a lattice ground state in occupation basis spreads its weight over a number of configurations exponential in the volume.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G4 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 4: "Is there a standard argument that the gain vanishes with $N$?"
* **Evidence**: argument. For an extensive state, the largest single-configuration weight decays as $e^{-cV}$ (the weight is a product over roughly independent regions), so a deterministic set of polynomial size $K$ holds weight at most $K e^{-cV}\to0$; the sampled remainder then carries all of the variance at large $V$. Blunt et al. report the largest gains for weakly correlated molecules, consistent with this. At 12 sites the set would be a finite-size bonus: the 157,216 representatives are few enough that a top-weight set could hold a sizeable share.
* **Why this kills it**: Gate 4 fails any method whose benefit exists only on small clusters; the deterministic share vanishes exponentially with the number of sites, so the gain is a finite-size effect.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: deterministic sets chosen by leverage or by the force's heavy tail rather than by weight (that is R51.3's question); molecules with a dominant reference; a local (patch) deterministic set, which runs into L57.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: near the nodes, which drive the force noise (L59), the relevant configurations are low-weight, so a deterministic set targeted at them, not at top weight, could stay useful; answered: that set is also exponentially large at large $V$, and targeting it is R51.3.
