* **Question**: Can the amplitude ratios $\psi(\mathbf{n}')/\psi(\mathbf{n})$ needed for local energies of a backflow-type state be computed in $o(N_e^3)$ per configuration (amortized), for example by exploiting locality of backflow, without changing the variational family?
* **Answer**: Partly. For backflow networks whose extra internal rows each read only a local patch of the lattice, we derived and checked an exact method that needs about N_e² instead of N_e³ operations. Keeping one row per site it ran only about 1.8 to 3.5 times faster than the comparable dense network (16 and 24 sites, large error bars). The 5- to 10-fold gains need fewer rows than sites, which changes the family, and those reduced networks lost sign accuracy increasingly with size, ending about twice as bad at 15 sites. A test of whether that is incomplete training is running.
* **What we showed**: For backflow networks built from extra internal rows that each read only a local patch of the lattice, a hop changes only about ten rows, so each needed ratio can be computed exactly from a stored inverse in about N_e² operations; this was verified to machine precision. Unrestricted backflow cannot be sped up this way (a one-line bound). Keeping one row per site, the cost per independent sample was only 1.8 to 3.5 times lower than the dense comparison network on 16- and 24-site lattices; halving or quartering the rows gave 5 to 10 times, but every such reduced network had larger sign errors than the full one on clusters of 8 to 15 sites, by about a factor of two at 15 sites even with four times more features per row.
* **What it would take**: A way to keep fewer stored rows than sites without losing sign accuracy, shown first at 12–15 sites and then in an equal-wall-time comparison with sampled training on 16 or more sites, where no exact reference exists. The test in flight only asks whether the 15-site loss is an artefact of incomplete training.
* **Conclusion**: An exact local energy costing $\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$ instead of $\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$ or more is achievable and verified for determinant and pairing NQS with patch-local hidden rows; but at reachable sizes the all-sites version is only $1.78\pm0.52$ to $3.50\pm0.85$ times cheaper than dense HFPS ($L=16$ and 24), and the 5- to 10-fold gains need the carried matrix made smaller than one hidden row per site, and every way of doing that tested so far (fixed sublattices, hole-gated rows) lost sign quality on 12- to 15-site clusters. **The question.** Neural-network backflow (NNBF) makes each amplitude a determinant of a matrix a network computes from the whole configuration; a local energy needs all $\mathcal{O}(zN_e)$ one-hop neighbours, so it costs $\mathcal{O}(N_e^4)$, against $\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$ for the Minimal Contrast Foil (projected BCS or Slater times Jastrow). The leaf asks for an antisymmetric family with an exact local energy below $\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$ and no accuracy loss at the $10^{-3}t$ per-site scale of competing orders. A one-line bound (F1) shows this is impossible for unrestricted backflow: the family must change by low rank under a hop.

**The mechanism.** Give each site a "hidden fermion" row whose entries depend only on a small patch, coupled to the physical orbitals by a learned linear map. A hop changes only the $b\approx10$ rows whose patch it touches, so each ratio is a $b$-dimensional determinant from a carried inverse, and decoupled subsystems factorise. Verified exact to $10^{-14}$, drift $<6\times10^{-11}$ over $10^4$ moves (R6.1′, R7.1, R7.3).
