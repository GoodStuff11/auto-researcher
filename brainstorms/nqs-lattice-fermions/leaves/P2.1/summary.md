* **Question**: Can the amplitude ratios $\psi(\mathbf{n}')/\psi(\mathbf{n})$ needed for local energies of a backflow-type state be computed in $o(N_e^3)$ per configuration (amortized), for example by exploiting locality of backflow, without changing the variational family?
* **Answer**: Partly. For backflow networks whose extra internal rows each read only a local patch of the lattice, we derived and checked an exact method that needs about N_e² instead of N_e³ operations. Keeping one row per site it ran only about 1.8 to 3.5 times faster than the comparable dense network (16 and 24 sites, large error bars). The 5- to 10-fold gains need fewer rows than sites, which changes the family, and those reduced networks lost sign accuracy increasingly with size, ending about twice as bad at 15 sites. A test of whether that is incomplete training is running.
* **Conclusion**: An exact local energy costing $\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$ instead of $\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$ or more is achievable and verified for determinant and pairing NQS with patch-local hidden rows; but at reachable sizes the all-sites version is only $1.78\pm0.52$ to $3.50\pm0.85$ times cheaper than dense HFPS ($L=16$ and 24), and the 5- to 10-fold gains need the carried matrix made smaller than one hidden row per site, and every way of doing that tested so far (fixed sublattices, hole-gated rows) lost sign quality on 12- to 15-site clusters. Checkerboard rows with four times more features per row also lost: their sign-error ratio to the all-sites route rose to 1.92 at 15 sites (job 731409), and a learned per-configuration choice of active rows did worse than the fixed checkerboard (2.57 against 1.59 at 12 sites). The one live route asks whether the 15-site loss is an optimiser artefact (R15.1, a deterministic polish of both arms, job 742029 running).

**The question.** Neural-network backflow (NNBF), the accurate neural wave function for lattice fermions, makes each amplitude a determinant of a matrix that a network computes from the whole configuration. A local energy needs the amplitudes of all $\mathcal{O}(zN_e)$ configurations one hop away ($z$ neighbours, $N_e$ electrons), so it costs $\mathcal{O}(N_e^4)$, against $\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$ for the Minimal Contrast Foil (projected BCS or Slater times a Jastrow factor). The leaf asks for an antisymmetric family with an exact local energy below $\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$, a Monte Carlo sweep at the Foil's $\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$, and no loss of accuracy at the $10^{-3}t$ per-site scale of competing orders. A one-line bound (F1) shows this is impossible for unrestricted backflow: the family must change by low rank under a hop.

**The mechanism.** Give each site a "hidden fermion" (an extra row of an augmented matrix) whose entries depend only on a small patch of the configuration, and couple these rows to the physical orbitals through a learned linear map. A hop then changes only the $b\approx10$ rows whose patch it touches, so each ratio is a $b$-dimensional determinant from a carried inverse, and decoupled subsystems factorise (size consistency). This family (R6.1′ in the Slater frame, R7.1 in the pairing frame, R7.3 with a sparse hidden–hidden block) was verified exact to $10^{-14}$ with drift below $6\times10^{-11}$ over $10^4$ accepted moves.

**Why the alternatives fail.** Fixed-rank or summed global channels are not size-consistent (R6.1, R7.4, R3.1); products of normalised ratios have extensive poles (R6.2); hierarchies and deeper trunks cannot be tested at exact-sum size (R2.6, R3.4, R6.4, R7.2); cheaper engines for the all-sites wave function miss the cost bar (R13.1, R13.1′).

**Strongest objection and answer.** All cost numbers use untrained parameters with $\tau_{\mathrm{int}}\approx1$ to 1.8 sweeps, and all accuracy numbers are single-seed and unprojected on clusters of at most 15 sites. Trained states may decorrelate more slowly, which would move every cost crossover outward, never inward, and a single seed cannot exclude that `half_F16`'s 1.11 at $V=14$ is noise. The answer is scope, not refutation: no claim here extends to trained VMC at $L\ge16$.

**Limits.** No accuracy test at the deployment scale ($L\ge16$, variational Monte Carlo without an exact reference) was run; ED-size accuracy is at most 15 sites; projected comparisons were done only for the earlier routes.
