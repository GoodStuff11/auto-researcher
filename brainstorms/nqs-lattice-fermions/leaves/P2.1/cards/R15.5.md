# R15.5 — Near-field plus low-rank far-field hidden couplings (Round 15.5) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Repair R15.4 by giving the coupling a far field, as fast multipole and hierarchical matrices do: $K=K_{\mathrm{loc}}+PB$, with $K_{\mathrm{loc}}$ supported on plus-shaped domains, $P$ a cluster basis (one column per $2\times4$ cluster, so the far rank $R=V/8$ grows with $V$) and $B$ dense. The engine carries $A^{-1}$ and $Q_P=A^{-1}P[\mathrm{up},:]$ ($N\times R$) instead of $N\times V$, so long-range coupling is kept at reduced cost.
* **Lineage**: translation repair of R15.4 (replace the component: add a non-local part in place of pure locality), Round 15; also an Operation A re-gating of R13.4, whose Gate 2 kill rested on R13.1 as the foil, a foil that later failed its own cost gate. No successor.
* **Source analogy**: source problem: dense kernel matrix–vector products. Correspondence: near field = $K_{\mathrm{loc}}$; far-field low-rank blocks = $PB$. Why it worked there: far-field kernels are smooth (analytic away from the source), hence low rank at each level. Translation gap: a learned $K$ has no smoothness guarantee, and the far correlations that matter here are sign correlations, not smooth decays. Citations: Greengard and Rokhlin, J. Comput. Phys. 73, 325 (1987); Hackbusch, Computing 62, 89 (1999).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0 (known family with an unaddressed adoption barrier), with the Gate 4 operation count deciding which far rank is affordable.
* **Evidence**: operation count at $L=16$ with R13.1's measured move statistics (127 accepted moves and 843 ratios per sweep, $k=9.6$, $b=8$): per accepted move $2kN(N+R)+RbV$, per ratio $N^2+NRb+5Nb$. Against R13.1 ($1.35\times10^8$ multiply-adds per sweep) this is $8.6\times10^7$ at $R=V/8$ (1.57 times cheaper, predicted $R_D(16)\approx5$) but $1.27\times10^8$ at $R=V/4$ (1.06 times, no cost case), because the far part moves work from the update to every ratio ($NRb$). So the only affordable family has far rank $R/N\approx0.29$. A local-plus-low-rank backflow with controllable range and rank is published (tensor backflow, arXiv:2407.11779), and Liu and Clark (arXiv:2311.09450) show that low-rank corrections are less expressive than full backflow; that expressivity loss is the barrier.
* **Why this kills it**: the affordable member of the family is a range-and-rank truncated backflow whose rank sits where the known expressivity loss applies, and the route adds no mechanism against that loss; the repair restores long range only by re-introducing the published low-rank restriction.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: a far field whose rank is set per configuration (only where it is needed), which is the placement question R15.6 tested on rows rather than couplings; a far-field rank above $V/8$ on hardware where $NRb$ ratio products are cheap.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: the near field here is full rank and patch-nonlinear, which tensor backflow's truncation is not exactly, so the family is not identical; unresolved, and a kill-only accuracy test at $V=12$ and 15 (about 30 CPU-minutes) could still be gated if a critic overturns G0.
