# R15.4 — Domain-local hidden couplings (Round 15.4) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Keep one hidden row per site (the full capacity of the rows) but make each hidden fermion's coupling column $K[:,m]$ nonzero only on a plus-shaped domain around $m$, as local-correlation methods restrict excitations to orbital domains. The Schur engine then needs no $N\times V$ table $Q=A^{-1}K$, since $A^{-1}K[\mathrm{up},S]$ is formed on demand in $\mathcal{O}(5Nb)$, cutting the accepted-move cost from $2kN(N+V)$ to $2kN^2$.
* **Lineage**: cross-field translation from quantum chemistry (local correlation), Operation B, Round 15; repaired as R15.5.
* **Source analogy**: source problem: coupled-cluster correlation energy scales as a high power of system size. Correspondence: hidden coupling column $K[:,m]$ = the domain of orbitals an excitation may reach; hidden rows = local pair correlations. Why it worked there: dynamic correlation decays with distance in gapped systems, so domain truncation (Pulay and Saebø; pair natural orbitals in DLPNO) keeps 99.9% of the correlation energy at near-linear cost. Translation gap: the sign structure (the Hard Core) is a non-local nodal property, not a decaying dynamic correlation. Citations: Pulay, Chem. Phys. Lett. 100, 151 (1983); Riplinger and Neese, J. Chem. Phys. 138, 034106 (2013).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-17).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0: is it known, and does it fix the reason it is not standard practice?
* **Evidence**: with $K$ local, entry $(i,j)$ of the pair-function correction is $\sum_{m\in D(i)}K[i,m]h_m\Omega_m[:,j]$, a function of the environment near $i$ only: finite-range backflow in the pairing frame. Finite-range backflow is SCALE (arXiv:2604.25775), and tensor backflow with controllable range and rank truncation is arXiv:2407.11779; SCALE's adoption barrier is the measured accuracy gap of 3 to $4\times10^{-3}t$ per site at $16\times16$ (leaf barrier P1). The operation count would pass the cost bar easily (about 14 times fewer operations than `dense2` at $L=16$), which is exactly why the literature already uses it.
* **Why this kills it**: it re-labels R1 (finite-range backflow) in another frame, and nothing in it addresses the locality accuracy gap.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: a local coupling plus an extensive non-local part that restores the missing long-range dependence (R15.5).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: the pairing reference $F_0$ and the dense readouts $\Omega_m$ are global, which SCALE's orbitals are not; but the configuration dependence, the only part that can correct the reference's nodes, is local in one index, and P1 is about exactly that; not resolved by any measurement here.
