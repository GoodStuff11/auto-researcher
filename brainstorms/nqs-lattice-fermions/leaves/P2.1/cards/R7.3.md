# R7.3 — Sparse hidden–hidden HFDS, SH-HFDS (Route 7.3) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: R6.1′ plus a configuration-dependent sparse hidden–hidden block $I+L(\mathbf n)$, with $L$ coupling nearest-neighbour hidden rows through local features. By the Schur complement the effective backflow contains $(I+L)^{-1}=\sum_k(-L)^k$, products of local features along lattice paths, so the correction is non-local and nonlinear (string-like, as for a hole moving in a spin background) while a hop still changes only $1+b$ rows.
* **Lineage**: Operation B.3, cross-field translation from Gaussian Markov random fields (round 7).
* **Source analogy**: in Gaussian Markov random fields a sparse precision matrix yields a dense covariance while every conditional stays local (Rue and Held, Gaussian Markov Random Fields, 2005); it works because the model carries the sparse matrix, never its dense inverse. Correspondence: precision is $I+L$, covariance is the resolvent. Translation gap: the determinant needs the augmented inverse of dimension $N+V$, whose update dominates the sweep.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at CRITIC (2026-11-16, Round 11 closure critic, after a T2 PROMOTE by rule).
* **Kill rule**: as R6.1″: a cost-reduction route survives only with a per-sample cost advantage at equal accuracy against the cheapest same-frame cubic baseline; short-horizon parameter-matched ratios cannot promote.
* **Evidence**: job 699305, instance 4, seed 11, 75 steps: $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ 0.0523 (route), 0.0655 (best local-hidden-row foil), 0.0880 (NNBF), so $\rho_N=0.594$ and $\rho_F=0.798$, PROMOTE by rule; the route was 8 times slower per step than NNBF (30.4 s against 3.7 s). Round 8b cost per decorrelated sample against NNBF of width 6 at $L=16$: $R(16)=1.47\pm0.60$, DOWNGRADE (uncertain).
* **Why this kills it**: the accuracy evidence is learning speed at matched parameters, and the cost evidence shows no advantage at $L\le16$; the route carries no part of the leaf's claim.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: a compiled engine without Green's tables (the 11c lesson), which was never built for this family, and a converged comparison at $L\ge16$.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: the critic's "update bottleneck" reasoning used a cost constant that Round 11c later showed was an artefact of carrying unnecessary tables, so the cost half of this kill is weaker than stated; unresolved, and the accuracy half stands.
