# R3.1 — SK-LR, shared-reference low-rank sum, round-3 test (Route 3.1) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: $\psi=e^{J}\prod_\sigma\det A_{0\sigma}\sum_{k=1}^Kw_k\prod_\sigma\det(I_r+C_{k\sigma}(\mathbf n)Y_{k\sigma})$, with $Y=A_0^{-1}\Phi_h[\mathrm{occ}]$ a carried table and $C_k$ the output of a narrow sign trunk; a positive amplitude network $e^J$ takes the rest of the parameters. Every ratio is a small determinant from carried tables, so $C_E=\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$, and the hypothesis was that the sum keeps projected-NNBF sign quality at fixed parameter count.
* **Lineage**: R2.2 retained (round 3), restated in round 5 as a parameter-split claim after the critic showed rank alone is not binding.
* **Source analogy**: the table method of multi-determinant QMC (Clark et al., J. Chem. Phys. 135, 244105, 2011), as in R2.2; the gap that killed it is size consistency.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G4 (2026-11-16, retroactive T0 argument, upheld by the round-6 per-leaf critic).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 4 (scaling and relevance): does the gain survive growing system size, i.e. is the sign capacity size-consistent at fixed width?
* **Evidence**: T0 lemma, corrected by the round-6 critic: restrict $M$ decoupled blocks to two states of opposite block sign; the target sign is the parity of $M$ bits, which has polynomial-threshold degree $M$ (Minsky–Papert). With a block-separable or locally connected trunk, each term is a polynomial of degree at most 2 (at most 4) in block functions, and so is any sum of $K$ terms, so parity is out of reach for $M\ge5$ at every $K$; a dense trunk needs width $\Omega(N_e)$, which makes $C_E=\Omega(N_e^3)$. The finite-size measurement (job 526080, $4\times4$ OBC $t$–$t'$–$J$ instances 0 and 3, $6+6$ and $5+5$ electrons, 3 seeds, 1200 projected SR steps, $P\approx1.5$–$1.7\times10^3$) was AMBIGUOUS: $\rho_s=\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}/\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}(\text{projected NNBF})=0.97$ and 0.87, but the oracle-spin ratio 1.30 and 1.16 sat in the ambiguous band; cost check PASS (594 times faster than brute force per connection at $N_e=896$).
* **Why this kills it**: the leaf's goal is sub-cubic cost with no accuracy loss at scale; a family whose sign capacity at fixed width cannot represent independent block signs loses accuracy as $V$ grows, and fixing that costs at least the Foil's exponent.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: the measured ED-size fact (a width-6 trunk with four rank-1 terms nearly matches projected NNBF on 16 sites, 236–594 times cheaper per connection); products instead of sums (R6.2) and local hidden rows (R6.1′).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: real ground states are not parities of decoupled blocks, so the worst case may not bind; the critic answered that size consistency is the minimum requirement for any extensive claim, and that the ED gain was largely a point-group-projection finite-size bonus (projection cut NNBF's sign error from 0.091 to $0.0125t$).
