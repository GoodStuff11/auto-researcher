# R2.6 — Fast-multipole hierarchical backflow (Route 2.6) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Near-field orbital rows are full-rank local backflow, while far-field influence enters through rank-$p$ channels per level of a quadtree, so the change of the orbital matrix under a hop has rank $\mathcal{O}(r^2+p\log V)$ and ratios stay cheap while every particle feels distant ones.
* **Lineage**: Operation B.3, cross-field translation from fast multipole methods, round 2; repaired as R6.4 (Tree-HFDS).
* **Source analogy**: the fast multipole method and hierarchical matrices (Greengard and Rokhlin, J. Comput. Phys. 73, 325, 1987; Hackbusch, Computing 62, 89, 1999) compress far-field interactions to low rank per level, which works because smooth kernels have low-rank far fields. Correspondence: near field is local rows, far field is pooled coarse channels. Translation gap: the backflow correction need not be smooth, and on exact-sum clusters everything is near field.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G2 (2026-11-11).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 2 (is the far-field mechanism weaker than a known baseline?) and Gate 3 for testing (can the claim be tested within the Hard Core at affordable size?).
* **Evidence**: the far-field channels are pooled rank-$p$ channels, the structure the round-1 critic showed is a strict subfamily of the Liu–Clark correction; on every cluster that admits exact sums ($V\le16$) all sites lie in the near field.
* **Why this kills it**: its novel part is a known weak subfamily, and its claim cannot be measured where $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ is exact.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: a hierarchy with nonlinear coarse features (R6.4) tested by variational Monte Carlo at $8\times8$ or larger.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: the hierarchy's value is precisely at large size; accepted, which is why the kill is scoped to exact-sum testing.
* **Checked by**: round-2 per-leaf critic (2026-11-11).
