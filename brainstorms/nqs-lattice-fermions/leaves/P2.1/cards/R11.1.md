# R11.1 — Sublattice hidden rows, checkerboard `half` at $F=4$ (Round 11b, 11c, 12, 12b) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Keep R7.1's pairing-frame wave function but place hidden rows only on the checkerboard sublattice ($v_h=V_h/V=1/2$), so the carried augmented inverse shrinks from dimension $N+V$ to $N+V/2$. The accepted-move update, which dominates the sweep, scales as the square of that dimension, so the route becomes several times cheaper than dense HFPS while staying size-consistent.
* **Lineage**: reopening-ledger ingredient (the Round 11 Saturation Diagnostic named "fewer hidden rows than sites" as the structural falsifier); cost-tested in 11b (Python, diagnostic stands) and 11c (compiled, falsified the diagnostic); its larger-$F$ mutation is R12.3.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T1 (2026-11-16, job 704752).
* **Kill rule**: Round 12 T1 rule, unchanged in Round 12b: "KILL the variant if $\rho>1.25$ at 2 or more counted sizes", with $\rho=\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}(\text{variant})/\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}(\texttt{full})$ at fixed $F=4$, unprojected exact-force SR trained to a plateau, and a size counted only if converged with $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}(\texttt{full})\ge10^{-3}t$.
* **Evidence**: cost (11c, compiled, untrained parameters, $L=16$): $R_D=5.05\pm1.27$ times cheaper per decorrelated sample than dense HFPS. Accuracy, OBC $t$–$t'$–$J$ toys with $V=8$, 12, 14, 15, seed 11: $\rho=2.44$, 1.59, 2.07 and 1.80, all four counted.
* **Why this kills it**: removing half of the hidden rows loses sign quality by a factor of 1.6 to 2.4 at every size, so the cost gain is bought with accuracy, against the leaf's goal.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: the same placement with more features per row (R12.3), and placements that depend on the configuration (R13.2).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: at fixed $F$ the variant also has 30 to 50% fewer parameters, so the test mixes row count with parameter count; the Round 12d matched-parameter comparison (B) was designed to separate them and has not run.
