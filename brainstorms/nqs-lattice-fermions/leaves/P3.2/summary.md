* **Question**: Can a deliberately biased pair of runs (initialized or constrained toward each competing order, e.g. filled versus half-filled stripe) be turned into a controlled decision rule using energy-variance extrapolation of each branch to zero variance, in the spirit of level-crossing detection?
* **Answer**: No, not beyond comparing the two energies. Extrapolating each run to zero energy variance is already standard and cannot detect the competing state mixed into each run. On one 12-site Hubbard cluster where a stripe state and a near-Néel state cross exactly, every rule built from the two trained runs gave the same verdict as picking the lower energy. That choice, abstaining within error bars, never erred on well-trained runs but erred once in 9 on deliberately mistrained ones. The key case was judged predictable and not run.
* **Conclusion**: On one 12-site Hubbard torus with an exact same-symmetry level crossing, no rule built from trained branch energies or from Ritz rotations of trained states beats the variational bias of the branches: the decision is either easy (enough capacity) or undecidable from energies. A plain final-energy comparison that abstains within its sampling error never committed wrong on the trained final states (job 619080) or on the Hard-Core-scale replicates; on adverse-bias snapshot states it committed wrong on 1 of 9 (seed 3456 at $d=-0.0002$, truth $B$). One exact Lanczos step fixes most wrong calls, but sampled at $2\times10^4$ draws per branch it abstains almost everywhere below the Hard-Core gap scale. The case that matters most (a bias pointing against the truth at the Hard-Core gap scale, on trained and saved states) was not measured: the compute gate refused it as predictable, so the leaf is closed at this budget, not answered.
* **Scope**: $4\times3$ periodic Hubbard torus, $N_\uparrow=N_\downarrow=4$ (doping $1/3$), $U=8t$, $t_y=1.11394t$, $t'$ swept through one same-irrep conical intersection between a period-4 spin stripe and a near-Néel state (not stripe against $d$-wave); space-group-projected NNBF at widths 4 and 8 (676 and 1160 parameters against a projected sector dimension of 1618, so width 8 is in the sector-fitting regime, where the network can nearly fit the sector freely); exact sums for training; sampled decisions with i.i.d. draws only.
* **Compute spent**: 1,564 CPU-h in three Slurm jobs (410917: 67; 451852: 1,412; 619080: 84; master Compute Ledger), plus about 7 CPU-minutes of interactive T1 runs and unledgered ED scans and smoke runs. All of it predates the compute gate.

**The question.** Neural quantum states (NQS) are neural-network wave functions trained by variational Monte Carlo (VMC). In the doped Hubbard model, competing orders (stripes, $d$-wave, antiferromagnetic states) lie within $\lvert\delta\rvert/V\sim10^{-3}t$ per site of each other ($V$ sites, hopping $t$), below typical variational errors; this is the *Hard-Core scale*. Practice trains two runs, each seeded toward one order, and compares energies. Each run $X$ carries a bias $b_X\ge0$, so the comparison decides by the sign of $\delta+\Delta b$, with $\delta=E_B-E_A$ the true gap and $\Delta b=b_B-b_A$. The leaf asks whether such a biased pair can become a decision rule that is sound, gains power as variance shrinks, costs a constant factor, and does not depend on the seeds.

**The test bed.** Competitors with different quantum numbers are separated trivially by symmetry projection (R1.2), so a two-parameter scan found an exact same-irrep conical intersection at $t'_c=-0.470482$ (Round 3). With $d=t'-t'_c$, the ground state is a spin stripe ($B$) for $d<0$ and near-Néel ($A$) for $d>0$, told apart by the sign of an order character $c$; the in-irrep gap is $1.4\times10^{-3}t$ at $d=\pm0.0002$ and $0.025t$ at $\pm0.005$. Truth is exact diagonalisation (ED). Branches are projected neural-network backflow (NNBF) states trained by stochastic reconfiguration (SR).

**The chain of evidence.**
1. *Transporting easy states fails.* Same-order eigenvector continuation from snapshots far from the crossing was wrong on 5 of 15 width-4 pairs, always toward $A$, while oracle continuation from exact snapshots was always right (job 451852; R3.5): the snapshots' bias passes through.
2. *Capacity decides the easy case.* The lower final energy wins on all 6 targets for all 3 seeds at width 8, but is wrong on 2 of 3 seeds at width 4 (same seeds, steps and warm starts).
3. *The $2\times2$ lemma.* Near the crossing every energy-based rule has its boundary at $\delta+\Delta b=0$, and the lowest eigenvector of a real symmetric $2\times2$ matrix always lies mostly on the lower diagonal, so Ritz rules in trained states return the lower trained energy. Off-diagonal information is bounded by the coupling $W\approx5\times10^{-4}t$, against $\Delta b=3.6$–$5.4\times10^{-3}t$ on two width-4 seeds.
4. *It holds on real states.* In job 619080 the excited-state Ritz decision equalled the lower-diagonal prediction on 9 of 9 instances (right on 4); the naive rule with final-state sampling error $\sigma_{\mathrm{MC}}$ never committed wrong (2 commits, 7 abstentions); an exact symmetric block-Lanczos step was right on 9 of 9 in-sample.
5. *Sampling removes the Lanczos gain below the Hard-Core scale.* On saved snapshot states with biases $0.5$–$1.6\times10^{-2}t$, the exact energy rule is wrong on 6 of 9 and an exact Lanczos step fixes 4; sampled at $2\times10^4$ draws it commits right on 1.3% and wrong on 0.6% of replicates, because the differences it must resolve ($\le1.7\times10^{-3}t$) are below its $3.0\times10^{-3}t$ standard error (R6.5).
6. *At the Hard-Core scale sampling is not the limit.* At $d=\pm0.005$ sampled plain energies commit right on 100% of replicates and never wrong, but the bias there pointed toward the truth, so only the sampling limit is scoped.

**Why the alternatives fail.** Zero-variance extrapolation of independent branches is practice and echoes the seed (R1.1, R3.4); gap-based certificates are powerless where gaps close (R1.3, R3.3, R5.1, R5.2); cross-order overlaps cost $e^{\mathcal O(V)}$ samples (R1.4a, R7.2); joint and shared-backflow ensembles are existing ansätze with heavy-tailed cross terms (R1.4, R2.1, R2.2); field, hysteresis and stability probes see only biased energies (R2.3, R3.1, R7.4); the crossing certificate's margin was anti-conservative (R5.3); Ritz rotations follow the lower diagonal (R6.1–R6.3); Lanczos is not size consistent and imaginary-time projection has the sign problem (R7.1, R7.3); small-cluster calibration lacks the competitors (R7.5).

**Strongest objection.** All evidence is one 12-site crossing between spin textures forced by $L_y=3$, the easy width is in the sector-fitting regime, and adverse-bias cases were measured only below the Hard-Core scale, so "the decision reduces to capacity" may be an ED-size artefact (cross-node critic, Majors 2 and 6). *Partial answer:* the lemma is algebraic wherever two diabats dominate, and the samples needed, $n\approx8\sigma^2/\delta^2$, fall as $1/V$ when variance and gap are extensive.
