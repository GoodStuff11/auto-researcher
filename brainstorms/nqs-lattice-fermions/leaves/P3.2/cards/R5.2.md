# R5.2 — Doublet certificate (round-5 Route 5.2) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Exploit the isolated doublet (third level about $0.67t$ higher): the weight outside the doublet is at most $\varepsilon/\Delta_2\approx10^{-3}$, so only the rotation inside the doublet needs certifying. Train a second orthogonal in-irrep state with the P3.1 penalty machinery and estimate $c_1$ and the off-diagonal $O_{01}$ from it to tighten the certificate.
* **Lineage**: Operation A on R5.1's failed component (the single-state fidelity bound), round 5.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G2 (2026-11-14).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 2, "does a cheap baseline or the foil reach the same metric?"
* **Evidence**: In a doublet perturbed by an out-of-doublet error $\varepsilon$, the Ritz rotation error is of order $\varepsilon/\Delta_1$, the constraint that killed R5.1. In the exact precheck the two-level bound commits only where $\eta\le0.24$ and only with the exact $O_{01}$; replacing $O_{01}$ by its rigorous bound $\sigma_0(\hat O)\approx0.3$ commits nowhere. The route also needs $H_{01}$ and $O_{01}$ to absolute precision far below $\Delta_1$.
* **Why this kills it**: The doublet removes the out-of-doublet weight but not the in-doublet rotation, so it cannot commit where the foil abstains.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: certificates with exact excited states (a diagnostic, not a method).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: A well-trained excited state could supply $O_{01}$ accurately enough. Answer: the round-6 kill check showed even an exact excited-state partner overrides a correct but biased branch; answered.
