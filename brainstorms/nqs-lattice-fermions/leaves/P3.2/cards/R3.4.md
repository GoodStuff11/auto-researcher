# R3.4 — In-irrep zero-variance extrapolation with a contamination-sign lemma (round-3 Route 3.4) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Inside one irrep, with competitor admixtures $\epsilon_Y$ in branch $A$ and $\epsilon_A'$ in branch $B$, the exact zero-variance intercepts differ by $\delta(1-\epsilon_Y-\epsilon_A')$; so the admixture shrinks the gap but cannot flip its sign before a branch crosses over, making in-irrep extrapolated comparison sign-safe.
* **Lineage**: Operation A.2 on R1.1, round 3.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-11).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0, "is it known?"
* **Evidence**: Projected VMC with variance extrapolation of competing branches is mVMC practice (Tahara & Imada; Darmawan et al., arXiv:1808.06327; Ido et al.).
* **Why this kills it**: The lemma justifies existing practice but adds no new mechanism.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: the lemma itself, which is kept as the reason in-irrep comparisons are sign-safe up to crossover.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: A proven sign-safety statement is a contribution even if the method is old. Answer: accepted as a lemma, not as a route; not raised by a critic.
* **Checked by**: round-3 per-leaf critic (2026-11-11), no objection recorded.
* **Lesson**: route-specific — the lemma is a result about practice, recorded in the round-3 autopsy.
