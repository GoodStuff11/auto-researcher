# R2.2 — State-averaged two-reference shared backflow with order-operator diabatisation, 2R-SA (round-2 Route 2.2) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Same two-reference shared-backflow family as R2.1, trained on the ensemble energy $\theta_0+\theta_1$ so that neither root can be discarded, then diabatised by diagonalising the order operator in the two-state span. The lower root's character decides, and the diabatic energies and coupling describe the crossing.
* **Lineage**: Operation B.3, cross-field translation from state-averaged CASSCF and state-averaged QMC plus Boys-type property diabatisation (round 2).
* **Source analogy**: In quantum chemistry, state-averaged MCSCF (Werner & Meyer, J. Chem. Phys. 74, 5794, 1981; state-averaged QMC, Filippi, Zaccheddu & Buda, JCTC 2009) avoids root flipping by optimising one orbital set for several roots, and Boys diabatisation (Subotnik et al., J. Chem. Phys. 129, 244101, 2008) rotates adiabatic roots to diagonalise a property. Correspondence: roots ↔ order branches, dipole ↔ order operator. It works there because roots share orbitals and the pair is computed exactly. Gap: here the pair is variational and biased, and the cross terms between order-distinct states are heavy-tailed.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-11, round-2 per-leaf critic).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0, "is it known, and is the adoption barrier fixed?"
* **Evidence**: The method is state-averaged VMC (Filippi et al.) on a multi-determinant backflow ansatz (Brown et al., arXiv:1011.4343), with the same cross-term (O3), projection (O1) and instance (O2) objections as R2.1. The markdown said decisions follow diabatisation while the code decided from adiabatic $c$ (O5).
* **Why this kills it**: Every part is existing practice, and the decision-specific part (diabatisation) needs the same heavy-tailed cross terms.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: state averaging as a fix for root collapse in excited-state NQS work.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: For a decision, the compromise of shared parameters is wanted, because it makes the bias common-mode. Answer: no measurement supports common-mode bias, and SO-EC (R3.5) later showed bias pulling toward the better-represented order; unresolved for this ansatz.
