# R3.6 — Affine transport of snapshot expectation vectors, AT (round-3 Route 3.6) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Each snapshot's vector of Hamiltonian-piece expectations $p_i=(\langle\hat H_{t_x}\rangle,\langle\hat H_{t_y}\rangle,\langle\hat H_{t'}\rangle,\langle\hat H_U\rangle)$ gives a rigorous variational energy $x\cdot p_i$ of a fixed order state at any coupling vector $x$. Take the minimum over each order's snapshots at the target and decide by the lower; transport bias was expected to be common-mode because both orders travel the same distance.
* **Lineage**: Operation B.1 (dual frame: the joint numerical range of the coupling pieces, Zauner et al., NJP 18, 113033, 2016), round 3; twin of R3.5.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at CRITIC (2026-11-11, round-3 per-leaf critic).
* **Kill rule**: the critic's question: does the route decide correctly even with exact snapshots, and is it distinct from the variational principle plus a warm start?
* **Evidence**: With exact ED snapshots AT commits the wrong order at $d=-0.0002$: the two diabats have unequal curvature ($\mathrm d^2E/\mathrm dt'^2\approx-14$ against $-10$), giving a differential transport bias of about $2.1\times10^{-3}t$, above the $1.4\times10^{-3}t$ gap. It is the variational principle with a zero-step warm start, and it falls under the $2\times2$ lemma.
* **Why this kills it**: A rule that fails with perfect inputs cannot be rescued by better training, and its transport bias is not common-mode.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: transport over distances short compared with the curvature scale, i.e. targets far from the crossing where the decision is easy anyway.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: The oracle failure is at one target; others were right. Answer: one exact-input failure at the Hard-Core-scale target suffices for a decision rule; unresolved beyond this instance.
* **Checked by**: round-3 per-leaf critic (2026-11-11), with its own ED computation.
