# R2.1 — Two-reference shared-backflow superposition, 2R-SS (round-2 Route 2.1) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: One wave function $\psi_\theta=\sum_{X}C_X\det[\Phi^\uparrow_X+F_\theta]\det[\Phi^\downarrow_X+F_\theta]$ superposes a Néel and a stripe reference, both dressed by one shared backflow network $F_\theta$, with $(C_A,C_B)$ the lowest Ritz vector of the $2\times2$ problem. Sharing the network should make the correlation dressing common-mode, the family is symmetric under exchanging references, and direct sampling avoids cross-overlap ratios.
* **Lineage**: Operation A.2 from R1.4a and A.1 from R1.4 (round 2, 2026-11-11). Its Ritz component was reused, restricted to same-order blocks, in R3.5.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-11, by the round-2 per-leaf critic), with a G1 objection on its cross terms.
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0, "is it known, and is the adoption barrier fixed?"; Gate 1 for the cross terms.
* **Evidence**: Shared backflow over several determinants is multi-determinant Slater–Jastrow–backflow (Brown, Trail, López Ríos & Needs, arXiv:1011.4343) (objection O4). The $2\times2$ Ritz needs $\langle a_A\vert a_B\rangle$ and $\langle a_A\rvert\hat H\lvert a_B\rangle$, heavy-tailed under sampling at the mixed crossing, while treating $C$ as free parameters makes it a plain two-determinant NNBF (O3). The test trained unprojected branches, so the nearest competitor was the cross-sector triplet (O1), and the instance's in-sector gap ($0.273t$) is about 20 times the Hard-Core scale (O2). In a 400-step smoke run the second Ritz root drifted to $\theta_1=-5.6t$ against $\theta_0=-8.85t$, the corrector collapse.
* **Why this kills it**: The object is an existing ansatz class, its decision-specific part needs the heavy-tailed cross terms, and its test could not reach a same-sector near-degeneracy.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: multi-determinant NNBF as an energy ansatz; a shared network as a way to reduce $\Delta b$ if a measurement ever showed common-mode bias.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: The novelty is in the decision use, and a projected re-test might have shown a gain. Answer: the round-3 lemma shows any rule from variational energies of the pair decides at $\delta+\Delta b=0$; unresolved by measurement, since the array (about 60 CPU-h) never ran.
