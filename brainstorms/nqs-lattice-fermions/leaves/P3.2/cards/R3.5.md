# R3.5 — Same-order eigenvector continuation, SO-EC (round-3 Route 3.5) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Train projected NNBF snapshots only far from the crossing ($\lvert d\rvert\ge0.03$, gaps $0.15t$ to $0.4t$), label them by measured $c$, and at the target solve the generalised eigenproblem inside each order block separately using only same-order overlaps and matrix elements. No optimisation happens at the hard point, so neither the reference trap nor crossover can act there, and no cross-order term is needed.
* **Lineage**: Operation B.3, cross-field translation from nuclear-physics eigenvector continuation, and Operation A.1 on the Ritz component of R2.1 (round 3).
* **Source analogy**: In nuclear many-body theory, eigenvector continuation (Frame et al., PRL 121, 032501, 2018) emulates an eigenvector across a parameter range from a few exact snapshots; it works because the eigenvector is analytic in the coupling. Correspondence: snapshot ↔ trained branch far from the crossing; Ritz in snapshot span ↔ block Ritz per order. Gap: across a first-order crossing one side's snapshots span the wrong eigenvector, hence the split by order; the snapshots here are biased.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T3 (2026-11-14, job 451852).
* **Kill rule**: pre-registered round-4 rule, quoted: "FAIL if the subset has fewer than 3 pairs, if $\mathrm{CW}(\mathrm{EC})>0.30$, or if any control dominates EC (CW no higher and DEC no lower)"; global "PASS at one or more valid widths and FAIL at none".
* **Evidence**: Job 451852 (6 tasks, 1,412 CPU-h): at width 4 (676 parameters, 5 of 6 targets hard) SO-EC committed on all 15 hard (target, seed) pairs and was wrong on 5 ($\mathrm{CW}=0.33$), always toward order $A$ on the $B$ side (e.g. $d=-0.0002$: AAA, truth $B$); the projected controls abstained on all 15. Oracle EC built from exact ED snapshots was right on every target. Width 8 was PREMISE-FAIL (control error about $6\times10^{-4}t$, below half the gap everywhere).
* **Why this kills it**: $\mathrm{CW}=0.33>0.30$ fails the rule at a valid width; and because the oracle is always right, the failure is the snapshots' differential bias passing through the continuation, not the continuation itself.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: continuation from near-exact snapshots (oracle EC is perfect); sizes where snapshots of both orders are equally well represented.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: The controls abstained everywhere, so a rule that commits at all is under harsher scrutiny; the aggregate printed NO-ROOM (all controls $\mathrm{CW}=0$ with $\mathrm{DEC}=0$). Answer: SO-EC fails its own absolute threshold regardless of the controls, and the round-3 critic had already found its oracle signal in an overlap direction of about $4\times10^{-6}$, about 30 times below the sampling error at $2\times10^4$ draws.
