# R3.1 — Projected-NQS level-crossing detector (round-3 Route 3.1) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Sweep $t'$ with projected NNBF branches warm-started in both directions, locate the crossing $t'_c$ from the kink of the energy or the jump of $c$ with a sampled-noise model, and decide a target by which side of $t'_c$ it lies on, pooling information across the sweep.
* **Lineage**: Operation B.2, suggested by the round-2 critic (round 3, 2026-11-11). Re-emerged with a bias margin as R5.3 (LCC).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G2 (2026-11-11), also failing G0.
* **Kill rule**: Gate 2, "does a cheap baseline or the foil reach the same metric?"
* **Evidence**: By the $2\times2$ lemma (Round 3) the kink of the lower variational envelope sits at $\delta+\Delta b=0$, the R0+ boundary. Pooling sampled energies across the sweep lowers statistical noise, but at a matched total sample budget putting the samples at the target does as well. Hysteresis-bracketed crossings are how VMC phase diagrams are drawn (Ido et al., arXiv:1712.05490; Darmawan et al., arXiv:1808.06327; Corboz–Mila protocol, arXiv:1802.00874).
* **Why this kills it**: Its boundary equals the foil's, and its noise advantage vanishes at matched budget.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: a detector with an added bias margin (tried as R5.3).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: A warm ladder correlates the errors across points, so the crossing estimate can be far less noisy than per-point energies (found in round 5: residuals $2\times10^{-5}t$ against window noise $2\times10^{-3}t$). Answer: correct for noise, but the bias still shifts the crossing; this led to R5.3.
* **Checked by**: round-3 per-leaf critic (2026-11-11), no objection to the kill.
* **Lesson**: L17 (`library.md`)
