# R7.6 — Sequential (Wald) sample budget for the energy difference (reopening ledger item I2b) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Attack ingredient I2b, the noise of the energy difference, by sampling the two branches sequentially and stopping as soon as the running estimate of $E_B-E_A$ crosses a sequential probability-ratio threshold. Wald's test minimises the expected number of samples at fixed error rates, so the decision could be reached with far fewer draws near the crossing, where a fixed budget abstains.
* **Lineage**: reopening-ledger ingredient I2b; cross-field translation from sequential statistics (Analogy Map row "Sequential statistics"); generated and killed at T0 by the reviewer triage of 2026-11-17.
* **Source analogy**: In sequential hypothesis testing (Wald, Ann. Math. Stat. 16, 117, 1945) observations are drawn one at a time and sampling stops when the likelihood ratio of two hypotheses leaves a band; it works because each observation is an unbiased draw from the true distribution, so stopping early saves samples without changing what is decided. Correspondence: observation ↔ Monte Carlo sample of the local-energy difference; hypotheses ↔ "A lower" and "B lower". Translation gap: the draws estimate $\delta+\Delta b$ (the true gap plus the branches' differential variational bias), not $\delta$, so the test decides the biased quantity faster but not more correctly.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T0 (2026-11-17), also failing G0 (sequential stopping is standard statistics and the route adds no fix for the bias).
* **Kill rule**: T0: "does the route change the sign of the decided quantity $\delta+\Delta b$, or only the cost of deciding it?"; a route that only changes cost is killed when sampling is not the binding constraint.
* **Evidence**: (1) Any estimator built from the two trained branches converges to $E_B+b_B-(E_A+b_A)=\delta+\Delta b$; a stopping rule changes the number of draws, not that limit. (2) Sampling is not binding at the Hard-Core scale: at $d=\pm0.005$ (in-irrep gap $0.025t$) sampled plain energies committed right on 100% of replicates and never wrong (ledger row I4a, `tests/P3.2_reopen_review/aggregate_output.txt`), and the samples needed, $n\approx8\sigma^2/\delta^2$, fall as $1/V$ when variance and gap are extensive. (3) Below the Hard-Core scale the wrong calls come from $\Delta b$ pointing against $\delta$ (6 of 9 exact-energy calls wrong on the snapshot states, R6.5), which no sample count fixes.
* **Why this kills it**: Where the decision fails, it fails because of the bias, which sequential sampling cannot see; where sampling limits the decision, the fixed-budget rule already decides correctly. The route saves samples in a regime where samples were never the problem.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: sequential stopping as a cost saving inside the recommended plain-energy rule; sequential designs that also adapt which branch is retrained (a capacity lever, not a sampling one).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: at sizes beyond ED the per-site gap may shrink faster than the variance, making sampling binding after all. Answer: then a sequential test still decides $\delta+\Delta b$, and the bias problem is unchanged; unresolved only as a cost question.
* **Checked by**: reviewer triage (2026-11-17); not yet reviewed by the per-leaf critic.
* **Lesson**: route-specific — a cost-only lever does not help a decision limited by bias; the general point is already L17.
