# R7.3 — Symmetric finite-$\tau$ imaginary-time step (reopening candidate C3) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Replace the Lanczos step by $e^{-\tau\hat H}\psi_X$ on both branches, whose energy gain is extensive, unlike a fixed number of Lanczos steps, so the symmetric bias reduction would survive growing $V$ (the Gate 4 repair of R7.1).
* **Lineage**: Operation A on ingredient I1, repair of R7.1's Gate 4 failure (2026-11-16).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G1 (2026-11-16), also failing G0.
* **Kill rule**: Gate 1, "does it violate a complexity bound?"
* **Evidence**: Applied by sampling it is projector Monte Carlo with the NQS as trial state; without a fixed-node constraint it has the fermion sign problem with cost $e^{\mathcal O(V\tau)}$, and with one the decision inherits the constraint's bias (lattice fixed-node GFMC was killed in P1.1; Loehr and Clark, arXiv:2510.26906). Fitting $e^{-\tau\hat H}\psi$ back into the network returns the capacity bias.
* **Why this kills it**: No polynomial-cost, exact, extensive symmetric lever exists among products of local operators; each realisation is either exponentially costly or reintroduces a bias.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: short-$\tau$ steps at small sizes; constrained projections whose bias is common-mode (no evidence for that).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: Fixed-node bias might be similar for both orders and cancel. Answer: same common-mode assumption that failed in R3.5 and R2.2; not raised by a critic.
* **Checked by**: leaf-summary critic (2026-11-16).
* **Lesson**: route-specific — the sign-problem barrier is the brainstorm's Hard Core itself.
