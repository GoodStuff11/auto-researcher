# R2.5 — Okamoto–Nomura level spectroscopy with twisted boundaries (round-2 Route 2.5) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Use boundary twists to give the two competitors different quantum numbers, as level spectroscopy does in spin chains (Okamoto & Nomura, Phys. Lett. A 169, 433, 1992), and decide from the crossing of their lowest levels, which are then exact sector energies.
* **Lineage**: Operation B.3, cross-field translation from one-dimensional critical-point spectroscopy, round 2.
* **Source analogy**: In spin chains, twisted boundaries assign the competing phases different parity or momentum, so their lowest levels cross exactly at the transition and finite-size crossings converge fast; it works because the twist separates the phases by symmetry. Gap: on a periodic 2D cluster a flux breaks a mirror but does not separate a stripe from a $d$-wave or Néel state (Nomura & Kitazawa, cond-mat/0201072).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G3 (2026-11-11).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 3, "does it work only by removing the Hard Core?"
* **Evidence**: No twist assigns same-sector stripe and $d$-wave (or near-Néel) states different quantum numbers on a periodic 2D cluster.
* **Why this kills it**: It reduces to the sector-resolved route R1.2, which needs competitors in different sectors.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: quasi-1D geometries where a twist does separate the orders.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: A non-symmetric twist could still shift the two orders' energies differently and be used as a diagnostic. Answer: then it is a field-response probe (R2.3); not raised by a critic.
