# R3.2 — Decision from the curvature or susceptibility of a projected branch (round-3 Route 3.2) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: For an exact eigenstate, second-order perturbation theory gives $\mathrm d^2E_k/\mathrm dx^2=2\sum_{n\ne k}\lvert\langle n\rvert\partial_x\hat H\lvert k\rangle\rvert^2/(E_k-E_n)$, so the sign of the competitor's term reveals whether it lies below or above. A branch with a positive competitor contribution would be the excited one.
* **Lineage**: Operation A.3, suggested by the round-2 critic, round 3.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G1 (2026-11-11), also failing G2.
* **Kill rule**: Gate 1, "is the quantity accessible within the ansatz without an exponential cost?"
* **Evidence**: The competitor is order-distinct, so it is outside the tangent space of the ansatz at the branch optimum and the variational curvature lacks the term. Evaluating it in the two-branch span needs $E_A-E_B$ in the denominator (biased energies) and the cross-order element $\langle\psi_B\rvert\partial\hat H\lvert\psi_A\rangle$, heavy-tailed under sampling.
* **Why this kills it**: The information is either absent from the variational curvature or obtainable only through biased energies and exponentially costly cross terms.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: curvature of exact states as a diagnostic.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: Fidelity susceptibility of a single branch peaks near the crossing even without the competitor term. Answer: a peak locates the crossing of biased energies (the R3.1 boundary); not raised by a critic.
