# R1.4 — Jointly optimised two-state ensemble with character-labelled Ritz extrapolation (round-1 Route 4) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Replace the two branches by one determinant of two states, $\Psi(\mathbf n_1,\mathbf n_2)=\det[\psi_i(\mathbf n_j)]$, trained on the ensemble energy $\mathrm{tr}(S^{-1}H)$ with $S$ and $H$ the overlap and Hamiltonian matrices of the pair. Joint sampling avoids exponentially small overlaps; Ritz roots are labelled by measured $c$, not by seed, and each root is zero-variance extrapolated, so a drifting branch is absorbed by the diagonalisation instead of corrupting a label.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, round 1, as the surviving route. Its Ritz subspace became the $2\times2$ Ritz of R2.1 and the trace objective of R2.2.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-11, by the round-1 per-leaf critic), with a G3 failure of its test.
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0, "is it known, and is the adoption barrier fixed?"; Gate 3 for the test, "does it work only by removing the Hard Core?"
* **Evidence**: The determinant-of-states ensemble is natural-excited-state VMC (Pfau et al., Science 385, eadn0137, 2024, arXiv:2308.16848; lattice version Ma et al., arXiv:2506.08594), and relabelling by measured character is the R0+ foil. The test's competitors were mirror-even and mirror-odd states of a $6\times2$ ladder, i.e. different sectors, and the test replaced trained states by exact block-Krylov vectors with twice the Ritz space of the foil.
* **Why this kills it**: The only new element over existing methods was the decision use, and the test could not show it, because its competitors were separable by symmetry (the R1.2 failure) and its arms were not trained ensembles.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: a joint ensemble on a same-irrep crossing with trained arms; it was in effect retested as R6.3 (PAIR, exact Ritz in the span of the two trained branches), which the $2\times2$ lemma reduces to the energy rule.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: Separate networks per state (as in NES) suffer differential expressivity, but the ensemble's joint training could still reduce $\Delta b$. Answer: R6.3 shows the Ritz root follows the lower trained energy, so joint labelling cannot beat $\Delta b$; partly answered.
