# R6.5 — Sampled single-branch Lanczos step, L1 (closed-leaf falsifier) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: One Lanczos step per branch, Ritz in $\mathrm{span}\{\psi_X,\hat H\psi_X\}$, with the branch of lower Ritz energy winning and the order read from its Ritz vector, all estimated by Monte Carlo from local values. This was the leaf's recommended practice in sampled form.
* **Lineage**: sampled form of R6.4, tested by the closed-leaf falsifier (2026-11-16).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at T1 (2026-11-16, about 3 CPU-minutes).
* **Kill rule**: pre-registered, quoted from `tests/P3.2_closed_falsifier/sampled_bl.py`: `KEEPS if f >= 0.5 and w <= 0.05; LOSES if f < 0.25 or w > 0.10; PARTIAL otherwise`, with $f$ the fraction of exact-naive-wrong instances times replicates where sampled L1 commits right and $w$ the fraction of all instances times replicates where it commits wrong.
* **Evidence**: Saved width-4 snapshot states evaluated at the target without retraining ("W0 states", biases $0.5$–$1.6\times10^{-2}t$), 9 instances, 40 replicates of $2\times10^4$ i.i.d. draws per branch. Exact naive rule wrong on 6 of 9; exact L1 fixes 4 of 6 with no new error (premise holds). Sampled L1: $f=0.013$, $w=0.006$. The exact L1 energy differences on the fixed instances are $2.7\times10^{-5}t$ to $1.7\times10^{-3}t$, against a plain-energy standard error of $3.0\times10^{-3}t$.
* **Why this kills it**: $f<0.25$, so the sampled step LOSES: it abstains almost everywhere because the differences it must resolve are below its sampling error; resolving them would need $2\times10^5$ to $10^9$ samples per branch.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: gaps at the Hard-Core per-site scale, where the reopening-review T1 found sampled L1 right on 85.4% of replicates and never wrong; Markov-chain sampling and trained (not W0) states.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: W0 states are not the trained states of job 619080, and gaps of $10^{-4}t$ per site lie below the Invariants' scale. Answer: accepted as scope; the kill is stated for sub-Hard-Core gaps only.
