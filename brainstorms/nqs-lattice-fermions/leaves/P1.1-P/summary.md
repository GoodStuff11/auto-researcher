* **State**: ANSWERED

* **Question**: Is P1.1's finding that signs dominate, on its three 12-site Hubbard clusters, caused by the network breaking the ground state's lattice symmetries?
* **Answer**: Yes. Trained with the lattice symmetries built in, the same backflow network gets at most 2.4% of its energy error from wrong signs on all three 12-site clusters, against 61–77% without them. Imposing symmetry only after training does not recover this. Wrong signs still carry 8–21% of the pairing-correlation error, but that error is tiny in absolute terms. At this size the network can fit nearly any symmetric state, so this says nothing about larger clusters.
* **What we showed**: With the same network, training length, random seeds and cost per step, training inside the ground state's lattice-symmetry sector cut the energy error carried by wrong signs from 61–77% to under 1% (median) on all three 12-site Hubbard clusters, and the sign error itself by a factor of at least a quarter of a million. The result holds even for the smallest network, whose parameter count is about a tenth of the number of symmetric states, so it does not come from the network simply fitting the whole symmetric space. Projecting a network trained without symmetry onto the sector afterwards removed only 1–90% of its sign error.
* **What it would take**: To overturn it: an independent re-implementation of the symmetry projection or of the sign-only energy giving a sign share of 30% or more on these clusters, or symmetry-projected training with a network much smaller than the number of symmetric states doing the same. The question is settled for these three clusters; larger clusters belong to other leaves.
* **Conclusion**: On the three $4\times3$ periodic Hubbard tori of P1.1, a neural-network backflow trained inside the ground state's space-group symmetry sector has almost no sign error: the sign carries at most 2.4% of its energy error at every width (largest single run 2.34%), against 61–77% for the same network trained without projection. P1.1's "sign is the binding error" result was symmetry contamination from a broken-symmetry start, not an intrinsic nodal error. The pre-registered rule returns SYMMETRY-DOMINATED on all three instances.

Not covered: larger lattices, open boundaries, $t$–$J$ models, sampled VMC.

### The argument
**Question.** A neural quantum state (NQS) outputs the amplitude $\psi_\theta(n)$ of each occupation configuration $n$ and must get both magnitudes and signs right. P1.1 split the energy error $\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}=E(\psi_\theta)-E_0$ ($E_0$ exact, from exact diagonalisation) into a sign part $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}=E_{\mathrm{sign}}-E_0$, with $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ the lowest energy reachable keeping $\psi_\theta$'s signs and choosing the best non-negative magnitudes, and an amplitude part. $f_E=\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}/\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}$; $f_C$ is the same split for the $d$-wave pair-correlation error. For neural-network backflow (NNBF: a Slater determinant whose orbitals are shifted by a one-hidden-layer network of width $H$) started from unrestricted Hartree–Fock (UHF), P1.1 found $f_E=0.61$–$0.77$. UHF breaks the lattice symmetries, so the round-2 critics asked whether that "sign error" is a failure to restore symmetry; the answer decided whether the sign branch (P1.2, P1.3) had a target.

**Mechanism.** The exact ground state is invariant under all 48 elements $g$ of the torus space group $G$. The projector $P_\chi=\lvert G\rvert^{-1}\sum_g\chi(g)U_g$ maps any state into that sector. If the wrong signs mostly encode a broken-symmetry pattern inherited from UHF, training $\psi_P=P_\chi\psi_\theta$ directly should remove them; a genuine nodal error would survive.

**Evidence.** Arm U repeats P1.1; arm P trains $\psi_P$ with the same widths, seeds, steps and SR settings at the same cost per step (1.9 against 2.1 s). Medians over 3 seeds at width 128, instances $5+5/U{=}8$, $4+4/U{=}8$, $5+5/U{=}4$ (407525):
* $f_E$: U 0.61, 0.77, 0.66; P 0.008, 0.006, 0.005.
* $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ ($t$): U 0.10, 0.076, 0.032; P $3.6\times10^{-7}$, $4.3\times10^{-8}$, $7.2\times10^{-8}$ (P/U ratio ≤$3.5\times10^{-6}$).
* $\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}$: U 0.17, 0.099, 0.049; P $4\times10^{-5}$, $8\times10^{-6}$, $2\times10^{-5}$.

The pre-registered rule needed $\rho\le0.3$ or $f_P\le0.3$ on all three ($\rho$ the P/U ratio of $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}$, $f_P$ the P median of $f_E$). Guards held: calibration error ≤$1.3\times10^{-13}t$, multi-start spread ≤$2\times10^{-8}$ of $\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}$ (guard 0.05), arm U reproducing P1.1 within 2.4% at every width. Arm P reaches $\langle\hat S^2\rangle=0.000$ without spin projection.

**Why the alternatives fail.** Projecting the trained U state afterwards (U→P, R2) removes only 35%, 90% and 1% of its sign error; exact spin projection of U leaves $f_E=0.37$, 0.67, 0.25. The gain comes from training inside the sector (R1).

**Beyond this leaf.** L1 (R1, R2): a budget measured on a network trained from a broken-symmetry start measures symmetry contamination; symmetry must be imposed during training.

**Strongest objection: sector fitting** (cross-node critic, Major 2). Once the parameter count $P$ reaches about half the symmetric singlet sector dimension $d_{S\chi}$ (3752 for $5+5$, 1618 for $4+4$), any network fits it; width 128 ($P=18800$, 15680) is deep in that regime. **Answer.** At width 8 ($P=1400$), both $5+5$ tori sit at $P/d_{S\chi}=0.37$, and arm P is only space-group projected ($d_\chi=13176$, $P/d_\chi=0.11$); there it still has median $f_E=0.019$ and 0.011 and $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}\le5\times10^{-5}$ in every seed, against U's 0.43 and 0.38. A second objection (only the upper estimate of $f_E$ is known) is biased against this verdict.

**Limits.** 12-site tori, exact sums. $f_C$ stays larger than $f_E$ (median 0.08–0.21 at width 128) on a tiny absolute error. A sign error that survives projection exists elsewhere: on the $4\times4$ open $t$–$t'$–$J$ $6+6$ cluster projected NNBF keeps $f_E=0.22$–0.24 (P2.1 jobs 410988, 451706).
