* **Question**: Is there an antisymmetric construction at $\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$ cost (or slightly above) whose nodal surface is provably more expressive than a backflow single determinant or Pfaffian, without a sum over many determinants?
* **Answer**: Not known yet; we found none that helps in practice. A trained network can locate its own remaining sign errors, so the information exists. But no extra sign-fixing layer we tried, at about the same cost, cut the sign error more than spending the same parameters on a wider ordinary network. This held on ladder-shaped clusters up to 12 sites and one 16-site cluster, where the one gain came from the magnitudes. Nothing was proved about expressivity, and the larger-cluster test was judged predictable and not run.
* **What we showed**: The ratios a trained backflow network already computes (how its determinant changes when one electron hops) pick out the configurations where its sign is still wrong far better than the occupations do. Yet every layer we built to use that information, whether discrete sign flips, positive correction factors, a second backflow pass, a low-rank orbital update or a smooth phase, tied with or lost to simply widening the ordinary network to the same parameter count, on doped t–J ladders of up to 12 sites. On a 16-site cluster one layer won by 16%, but switching its sign flips off changed nothing, so the gain was in the magnitudes. The last phase layer flipped signs mostly where the wave function nearly vanishes, and lost even with ten times the parameters.
* **What it would take**: A new sign layer whose parameter count does not grow with system size, that first passes a corrected quick screen and then beats a wider network at 12 sites with the gain traced to its signs; only then is the 16-site test (about 350 CPU-hours) worth running. A proof of greater nodal expressivity would settle the question directly; none was attempted.
* **Conclusion**: A trained neural backflow's own determinant ratios tell where its remaining wrong signs are (held-out AUC 0.93 at 12 sites), but no sign layer or phase carrier tested turned that knowledge into a lower sign error than the same parameters spent on backflow width: the warm-started layers and carriers tied or lost ($r$ between 0.96 and 1.08 at the counted sizes, the last carrier at 1.08 and 1.01), and the two wins were not sign wins (on the $4\times4$ instance FRD-K's 16% gain was amplitude-borne; on a narrow projected 6×2 toy LRK's per-step win shrank to 10% at equal CPU time). The last carrier, a linear phase on a span that couples neighbouring edges, did flip signs, but mostly on near-node configurations, and still lost to width despite 10–12 times more parameters. The step that would test continuous carriers on larger clusters was refused by the compute gate as predictable (no carrier ever passed at 12 sites), so the leaf is closed at this budget, not answered.

**The question.** A neural quantum state (NQS) writes $\psi(\mathbf n)$ on occupation configurations of $V$ sites; for doped, frustrated fermions the sign (the nodes) is the hard part. Neural-network backflow (NNBF) is a Slater determinant whose orbitals a small network computes from $\mathbf n$, so its nodes move only by deforming orbitals from a mean-field anchor. P1.2 asks whether an added sign-changing layer at about one determinant's cost reaches better nodes. The score is $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ (exact upper bound on the energy error of the best non-negative amplitudes under the ansatz's sign), compared with a matched-width foil (NNBF widened to the same parameter count, same SR recipe) via $r=\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}[\text{route}]/\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}[\text{foil}]$; $r<1$ favours the route.

**The mechanism tried.** The true sign at $\mathbf n$ is a weighted vote of its Hamiltonian neighbours ($\psi_0(\mathbf n)(E_0-H_{\mathbf n\mathbf n})=\sum_{\mathbf m}H_{\mathbf n\mathbf m}\psi_0(\mathbf m)$), which the determinant supplies through ratios $R_e=c_e\Phi(\mathbf m_e)/\Phi(\mathbf n)$ over edges $e$.
