# R2 — Pairwise antisymmetric product (Route 2) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Replace the determinant by a product of pair functions $\prod_{i<j}f(x_i,x_j)$ times a symmetric factor, a generalized Vandermonde form costing $\mathcal O(N_e^2)$ instead of $\mathcal O(N_e^3)$, hoping the pair function can place nodes without a mean-field orbital anchor.
* **Lineage**: initial batch (Round 1, 2026-11-10); a configuration-dependent $f$ would reduce it to R1.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G1 (2026-11-10).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 1: does it respect the symmetries and the complexity bounds, here, can its sign class even contain mean-field nodes?
* **Evidence**: With a configuration-independent $f$, the sign in the site-ordered basis is $(-1)^{\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}n_in_j}$ with $b_{ij}\in\{0,1\}$, a quadratic polynomial over GF(2). On a $3\times4$ open lattice with 4 free spinless fermions at $t'=-0.3$, the exact sign on the 396 nonzero configurations is not any such polynomial (`pairwise_check` prints `False`).
* **Why this kills it**: a family that cannot reproduce free-fermion nodes cannot strictly contain the foil's signs (solution condition (i) of section 1); making $f$ configuration-dependent restores universality but turns the route into R1.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: pair products with configuration-dependent pair functions on top of a determinant (that is backflow-like and covered by other routes).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: one 12-site free-fermion check, not a proof for all lattices. Not raised by a critic; the algebraic argument (GF(2) quadratic signs) covers every size, so the check only illustrates it.
* **Checked by**: not reviewed.
* **Lesson**: route-specific — a fixed-pair-function sign class is too small; a property of this construction only.
