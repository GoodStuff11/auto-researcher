# Subproblem: P1.3 Escaping wrong-sign basins during optimization
**Parent Chain**: P1 -> P1.3  **Status**: EXHAUSTED at this budget (2026-11-17; the last reopening-ledger row, the direct basin probe R7.7 on the 4×4 instance, was refused by the compute gate's G2 and G4 rules; see the Leaf Status Summary)
### 1. Mathematical Abstraction & Barriers
* **Formulation**: Let $\mathcal{F}=\{\psi_\theta\}$ be a real fermionic NQS on fixed-$(N_\uparrow,N_\downarrow)$ occupation configurations $\mathbf{n}$, of the form
$$\log\psi_\theta(\mathbf{n})=A_\theta(\mathbf{n})+\log\det\Phi^{\uparrow}_\theta(\mathbf{x})[\mathbf{n}_\uparrow]+\log\det\Phi^{\downarrow}_\theta(\mathbf{x})[\mathbf{n}_\downarrow],$$
where $A_\theta$ is a real "amplitude" network (Jastrow plus hidden units, so $e^{A_\theta}>0$), $\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{n}_\uparrow,\mathbf{n}_\downarrow)$, and $\Phi^\sigma_\theta(\mathbf{x})=\Phi^\sigma_0+\sum_h\tanh(W_h\cdot\mathbf{x}+c_h)B^\sigma_h$ is a backflow orbital matrix whose occupied rows $[\mathbf{n}_\sigma]$ form the determinant. The sign function is $s_\theta(\mathbf{n})=\mathrm{sgn}\,\psi_\theta(\mathbf{n})$, set by the determinants only. An optimizer is a map $\mathcal{O}:\theta_0\mapsto\theta_T$ after a budget of $T$ natural-gradient (SR) steps. The sign quality of a state is measured by the **sign-only fixed-node error**
$$\varepsilon_s(\theta)=\frac{E_{\mathrm{FN}}\big[s_\theta;\lvert\psi_0\rvert\big]-E_0}{\lvert E_0\rvert},$$
where $E_{\mathrm{FN}}[s;a]$ is the lattice fixed-node energy (ten Haaf et al., DOI:10.1103/PhysRevB.51.13039) for the guiding function $g=s\,a$ (exact amplitudes $a=\lvert\psi_0\rvert$, trial signs $s$): off-diagonal elements with $H_{\mathbf{n}\mathbf{m}}g_\mathbf{n}g_\mathbf{m}>0$ are removed and $H_{\mathbf{n}\mathbf{m}}g_\mathbf{m}/g_\mathbf{n}$ is added to $H_{\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}}$. Then $E_{\mathrm{FN}}\ge E_0$ with equality iff $s=\mathrm{sgn}\,\psi_0$ on the support of $\psi_0$, so $\varepsilon_s$ isolates the sign from the variational amplitude. A **wrong-sign basin** of $\mathcal{F}$ is a set of initial conditions (in particular the mean-field start $\theta_0^{\mathrm{MF}}$) for which plain SR converges to $\theta^\ast$ with $\varepsilon_s(\theta^\ast)$ well above $\varepsilon_s^{\mathrm{best}}(\mathcal{F})$, the best value reachable in the same family from any start. **Solved** means: an optimizer modification with at most a constant-factor overhead in SR steps, polynomial in $V$ per step, that from $\theta_0^{\mathrm{MF}}$ closes at least half of the basin gap $\varepsilon_s^{\mathrm{SR}}-\varepsilon_s^{\mathrm{best}}$ on a doped, $t'\ne0$, 2D cluster, without raising the variational energy.
* **The Node Barrier**: Where existing approaches halt against the Hard Core:
    * *Plain SR / natural gradient* is linearized imaginary time in $\log\psi$: a step changes $\psi(\mathbf{n})\to\psi(\mathbf{n})(1-\tau[E_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{n})-E])$ with small $\tau$, so it never flips a sign directly. A sign at a high-weight configuration flips only if the amplitude passes through zero, which costs energy along the path. This is the "residual sign problem" seen with frustrated spins (Szabó & Castelnovo, arXiv:2002.04613; Bukov, Schmitt & Dupont, arXiv:2011.11214, who name landscape ruggedness, not expressivity or sampling, as the obstacle).
    * *Finite-step projector fits* (supervised wave-function optimization, Kochkov & Clark arXiv:1811.12423; self-learning power method, Giuliani et al. DOI:10.22331/q-2023-08-29-1096; adaptive one-step ITE with energy-gated target, Ledinauskas & Anisimovas arXiv:2307.15521) use a finite step, which can flip signs in the target, but the fit to the target is again a gradient method in the same parametrization. With a determinant layer the flips that matter are global (they move whole nodal surfaces), and a one-step target $(1-\tau H)\psi$ only flips signs where $\tau(E_{\mathrm{loc}}-E)>1$, which is local.
    * *Separate learning rates for sign and amplitude* (arXiv:2510.02051) and *fixed-sign amplitude optimization* (arXiv:2308.09664) were developed for spin phase networks. They do not apply to the fermionic case, where the sign is tied to the determinant and cannot be given its own learning rate without also moving the amplitude.
    * *Multi-start from different mean-field references* (practice in Gu et al. arXiv:2507.02644 vs. Roth et al. arXiv:2511.07566; Viteritti et al. arXiv:2604.21978 report initialization-dependent orders) works, but each start is a full optimization, and which references to try is chosen by hand.
  *Fundamental barrier*: finding the globally best sign is a non-stoquastic ground-state problem, which is QMA-hard in general (Invariants §5). No optimizer escapes all basins in polynomial time. Any route must restrict the move set to a structured family. *Practical barrier*: for fermionic NQS, the ground state of a doped cluster with PBC typically lies in a total-momentum or point-group sector different from the free-fermion shell filling, so the mean-field start can sit in the wrong symmetry sector. Moving between sectors means a global node rearrangement that SR does not make. Whether this happens on ED-sized doped clusters is **unverified** for fermionic NQS (the Szabó–Castelnovo evidence is for spins).
  *Anti-Solution Space*: (a) bigger networks or more parameters (massive compute, and Loehr & Clark arXiv:2510.26906 show backflow width saturates); (b) supervised fitting to the exact ground-state sign (needs $\psi_0$); (c) sign "cures" by basis rotation (NP-complete, Marvian et al.); (d) any test at half filling with $t'=0$ or $U=0$, where the free-fermion node is close to exact; (e) sampler-only changes (see Route 3); (f) relabeled SWO/ITE with a new step schedule.

### 2. Candidate Routes & Falsification Gates
* **Route 1**: Lanczos-target sign refit. Every $K$ SR steps, form the one-step Lanczos state $\psi_L=\psi_\theta+\alpha H\psi_\theta$ with $\alpha$ chosen by $2\times2$ Rayleigh–Ritz (Sorella, DOI:10.1103/PhysRevB.64.024512). The ratio $\psi_L/\psi_\theta=1+\alpha E_{\mathrm{loc}}$ is free in VMC. Fit only the antisymmetric-layer parameters to $\psi_L$ by fidelity, and accept the result if the energy drops.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): FAIL. Fitting a network to a finite projector step is SWO (arXiv:1811.12423) and the self-learning power method (DOI:10.22331/q-2023-08-29-1096). Holding the target fixed until the energy decreases is Ledinauskas & Anisimovas (arXiv:2307.15521). The remaining differences (Lanczos-optimal $\alpha$, restricting the fit to the determinant layer) do not fix the reason these methods have not displaced SR: the fit is still a gradient flow in the same parametrization, and a one-step target only flips signs locally, where $\alpha E_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{n})<-1$. Global node moves stay out of reach.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Conserves $N_\sigma$ and is polynomial per step.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. Not reached.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. Not reached.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
* **Route 2**: Orbital-swap basin hopping (OSBH) on a trained NQS. After a plain-SR warm-up, diagonalize the one-body hopping matrix $T$ inside the occupied span of $\Phi^\sigma_0$ and inside its orthogonal complement. Propose discrete swaps that replace one of the $w$ highest occupied one-body directions with one of the $w$ lowest virtual directions, for spin $\uparrow$, spin $\downarrow$, and both together ($3w^2$ proposals). Keep the trained Jastrow, amplitude and backflow parameters. Relax each proposal for $r$ SR steps and accept the lowest-energy one only if it beats the current state relaxed for the same $r$ steps. All proposal steps are charged to the same budget $T$ as plain SR. A swap changes the reference's occupied subspace, hence its total momentum and nodal structure, in one discrete move. This is the global node rearrangement that SR cannot make continuously. Point of departure: multi-start from hand-chosen references, and occupied/virtual swaps as in the maximum-overlap method of quantum chemistry (Gilbert, Besley & Gill, DOI:10.1021/jp801738f), used there for excited states, not for basin escape of a correlated ansatz.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS (conditional). Searches found no fermionic NQS work that makes discrete orbital-occupation moves mid-optimization on a trained correlator; the closest practice is full restarts from different references (arXiv:2507.02644, arXiv:2511.07566, arXiv:2604.21978). Multi-start is not routine because each restart costs a full NQS optimization and the references are picked by hand. OSBH targets that cost: each proposal costs $r\ll T$ steps because it reuses the trained correlator, and the move set is generated automatically from the one-body spectrum. If the test shows OSBH is no better than restarts at matched cost, the route is a relabeling and dies.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Swaps preserve $N_\uparrow,N_\downarrow$ and $S^z$. The move set is a structured restriction ($3w^2$ single-determinant occupation changes near the "Fermi level" of the current reference), so there is no claim of global optimality, consistent with the QMA-hardness boundary. Cost per cycle is $(3w^2+1)r$ SR steps, independent of $V$ apart from the per-step cost.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. The metric is defined relative to plain SR on the identical ansatz and budget, so a method that does nothing scores $M=0$. The foil (Jastrow–Slater with SR) is run as a separate arm with the same swaps. If foil-plus-OSBH matches NQS-plus-OSBH, the result says that basin escape is an optimizer effect, not an NQS advantage. That is reported, not hidden.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. The test instance is doped ($\delta=1/6$), has $t'=-0.3$, is 2D with PBC, and has $U=8$. Swaps are relevant precisely because the doped shell structure differs from the interacting ground state's quantum numbers. Nothing relies on half filling, 1D, or $U=0$. Caveat: OSBH only reaches sign structures adjacent to *another* single-reference anchor. It fixes a *wrong* anchor, not the existence of an anchor (that is P1.2).
  - *Verdict*: SURVIVED
* **Route 3**: Sign-flip-aware Metropolis moves (proposals that cross nodal regions, e.g. pair hops or exchange moves biased toward sign changes).
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS. Specialized moves exist, but not as a sign-basin remedy.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): FAIL. On a discrete lattice, Metropolis on $\lvert\psi\rvert^2$ is not confined by nodes: a hop jumps between configurations and zero-amplitude intermediate configurations are not visited. In the infinite-sample limit the fixed points of the SR flow do not depend on the sampler at all, so a sampler change cannot change which basin is reached. Only the noise changes. (Exact-sum SR in the decisive test has no sampler, and a basin found there would persist under any sampler.)
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. Not reached.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. Not reached.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
* **Route 4**: Hamiltonian continuation (anneal $U$ from 0, $t'$ from 0, or a sign-free to signful path, warm-starting each step).
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): FAIL. Starting from the $U=0$ Slater determinant *is* continuation from the non-interacting point and is already the default. Annealing in Hamiltonian parameters is common, and its known failure is that it follows the metastable branch through first-order level crossings. Nothing in the route fixes that.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. Not reached.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): FAIL. The path starts at the sign-trivial or non-interacting point, and the near-degenerate competing orders of the Hard Core are exactly the level crossings the path must pass, where adiabatic continuation keeps the wrong branch.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
### 3. Top Surviving Route & Sharpest Gap
* **Mechanism**: Orbital-swap basin hopping. Discrete occupied-to-virtual swaps of the determinant's reference orbitals are applied to an already-trained fermionic NQS, each followed by a short SR relaxation that reuses the trained correlator, with energy-gated acceptance under a step budget matched to plain SR. It turns the "which mean-field reference" choice, currently made by hand with full restarts, into an automatic, cheap move set that changes the global nodal structure in one step.
* **Sharpest Gap**: It is unverified that fermionic NQS from a mean-field start actually land in wrong-sign basins on ED-sized doped clusters, i.e. that $G=\varepsilon_s^{\mathrm{SR}}-\varepsilon_s^{\mathrm{best}}>0$ by a margin. If the gap exists, it is unverified that it is "anchor-type" (reachable by a few one-body swaps plus $r\ll T$ relaxation steps) rather than requiring a change the single-reference backflow cannot make. A second unverified assumption is that the energy after $r$ relaxation steps ranks the basins correctly, given that the basins' energies differ by of order $10^{-3}t$ per site.

### 5. Local Autopsy Log
* **Route 1 (Lanczos-target sign refit)**: killed at Gate 0. It is SWO, the self-learning power method and energy-gated one-step ITE under a new step choice (arXiv:1811.12423; DOI:10.22331/q-2023-08-29-1096; arXiv:2307.15521). The fit remains a gradient flow in the same parametrization, and a one-step projector flips signs only locally, where $\alpha E_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{n})<-1$, so the global nodal rearrangements that define fermionic basins stay out of reach.

    - **Move generator.** After a warm-up, compute the one-body density matrix $\rho^\sigma_{ij}=\langle c^\dagger_{i\sigma}c_{j\sigma}\rangle$ of the trained state for each spin $\sigma$ (a VMC estimator), average it over the space group $G$ of the cluster, $\bar\rho^\sigma=\lvert G\rvert^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}P_g\rho^\sigma P_g^{\mathsf T}$ with $P_g$ the site permutation of $g$, and diagonalise it. Degenerate shells are split by an infinitesimal mirror-parity term, so every natural orbital (NO) carries definite parities. Candidates replace the reference orbitals $\Phi^\sigma_0$ of NNBF by the top-$N_\sigma$ NOs ("sno"), or by the same set with the last occupied and first empty NO exchanged for $\uparrow$, $\downarrow$, or both. All backflow weights are kept.
    - **Acceptance.** The incumbent and each candidate are relaxed $r$ SR steps and scored by the guided fixed-node energy $E_{\mathrm{FN}}[\lvert\psi\rvert]$: the lowest eigenvalue of the ten Haaf effective Hamiltonian built from the candidate's signs with guide $\lvert\psi_{\mathrm{cand}}\rvert$. This is the quantity lattice fixed-node GFMC estimates (ten Haaf et al., DOI:10.1103/PhysRevB.51.13039), so at scale it is a sign-problem-free projector-MC number with polynomial cost per candidate. The score replaces the candidate's amplitudes by the optimal stoquastic amplitudes for its sign, so the amplitude error left by a short relaxation (the quantity that defeated Route 2.0) drops out. P1.1 measured $E_{\mathrm{FN}}-E_{\mathrm{sign}}=0.005$ to $0.016$ for NNBF, against $\Delta E_{\mathrm{amp}}=0.02$ to $0.07$ and basin-scale sign differences of order $0.05$ to $0.1$ on the $4+4$ cluster.

Natural orbitals from QMC density matrices exist (Kent et al., arXiv:cond-mat/9804010) and iterative reference updates of Jastrow–Slater states are old (Fahy & Filippi, arXiv:cond-mat/9906305). Choosing nodal surfaces by DMC energy is self-healing DMC (Reboredo, Hood & Kent, PRB 79, 195117, 2009). The move set alone is a shell-occupation scan, as the critic says. Why re-anchoring is not standard for NQS: each re-anchor discards or disturbs the trained correlator, and the variational energy after a short relaxation cannot tell which anchor is better at the Hard Core, so practitioners pick references by hand and pay for full restarts. The gradient of $F$ is a VMC estimator (ratio $\psi_{\mathrm{ref}}/\psi_\theta$ on samples of $\lvert\psi_\theta\rvert^2$). Recurring terms: a neural quantum state (NQS) is a neural network that returns the wave-function amplitude $\psi_\theta(\mathbf n)$ of each occupation configuration $\mathbf n$; neural-network backflow (NNBF) is an NQS whose Slater-determinant orbitals depend on the whole configuration through a small network; stochastic reconfiguration (SR) is the natural-gradient optimiser used to train it; exact diagonalization (ED) gives the exact ground state $\psi_0$ and energy $E_0$; unrestricted Hartree–Fock (UHF) is the mean-field state that seeds the determinant; and the sign error is $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}=E_{\mathrm{sign}}-E_0$, where $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ is the lowest energy reachable with the trained signs held fixed and the best non-negative amplitudes (zero only for the exact signs, and blind to amplitude error). Energies are in units of the hopping $t$.

### R1 — Lanczos-target sign refit (round-1 Route 1) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Every $K$ SR steps, build the one-step Lanczos state $\psi_L=\psi_\theta+\alpha H\psi_\theta$, with $\alpha$ chosen by a $2\times2$ Rayleigh–Ritz problem, and fit only the determinant (sign-carrying) parameters of the NQS to $\psi_L$ by maximising the fidelity, keeping the result if the energy drops. It was expected to work because a finite projector step can flip signs, which an infinitesimal SR step never does.
* **Lineage**: initial batch (round 1, 2026-11-10). Not mutated further.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-10).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0: is the idea known, and is the reason it is not standard practice fixed?
* **Evidence**: Fitting a network to a finite projector step is supervised wave-function optimisation (SWO, Kochkov and Clark, arXiv:1811.12423) and the self-learning power method (Giuliani et al., DOI:10.22331/q-2023-08-29-1096); holding the target fixed until the energy drops is Ledinauskas and Anisimovas (arXiv:2307.15521). The ratio $\psi_L(\mathbf n)/\psi_\theta(\mathbf n)=1+\alpha E_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf n)$, with $E_{\mathrm{loc}}=(H\psi_\theta)/\psi_\theta$ the local energy, changes sign only where $\alpha E_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf n)<-1$, a condition on single configurations.
* **Why this kills it**: The two differences from prior art (the Lanczos-optimal $\alpha$ and the restriction of the fit to the determinant) leave untouched the reason these methods have not replaced SR: the fit is still a gradient flow in the same parametrisation, and the target flips signs only locally, while fermionic sign basins differ by global moves of the nodal surface.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: targets built from several Krylov steps, whose sign changes are not local; and refits in a different parametrisation of the sign.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: a fit restricted to the determinant might move the nodal surface globally even when the target flips only local signs, because a determinant couples all configurations (not raised by a critic). Unresolved as stated, and moot at ED size after R3.1's premise tests found no basin to escape.
* **Checked by**: not reviewed by a critic as a kill; the orchestrator lists it under the master anti-pattern "re-labelled sign networks".
* **Lesson**: L21 (`library.md`)

### R2 — Orbital-swap basin hopping, OSBH (round-1 Route 2, re-audited as Route 2.0) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: After a plain-SR warm-up, propose discrete swaps of the determinant's reference orbitals: one of the $w$ highest occupied one-body directions is exchanged with one of the $w$ lowest empty ones, for spin up, spin down or both. The trained correlator is kept, each proposal is relaxed for $r$ SR steps, and the lowest variational energy is accepted if it beats the incumbent relaxed for the same $r$ steps. All steps count against the same budget as plain SR. A swap changes the occupied subspace, hence the total momentum and the nodal structure, in one move, which SR cannot do continuously.
* **Lineage**: initial batch (round 1); the only round-1 survivor. Made conditional by the round-1 critic, re-audited and killed in round 2 as Route 2.0 (card R2.0), and kept as the ablation arm AE of the round-2 test. Mutated into R2.1 (Operation A.3: acceptance retargeted to a sign-only score).
* **Source analogy**: basin hopping in cluster global optimisation (Wales and Doye, J. Phys. Chem. A 101, 5111, 1997) and occupied–virtual swaps of the maximum-overlap method in quantum chemistry (Gilbert, Besley and Gill, DOI:10.1021/jp801738f). There, a relaxed or mean-field energy ranks solutions without amplitude error; here the trained correlator leaves a large amplitude error after any swap (Analogy Map B1).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G3 (2026-11-11; it also failed G0 and G2).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 3: does the route work only by removing the Hard Core? Concretely, can an energy compared after $r$ relaxation steps rank basins at the energy scale of competing orders? Gates 0 and 2 asked whether the move set is known and whether a null can match the metric.
* **Evidence**: The amplitude error left after a short relaxation is of order $0.1t$ to $1t$: P1.1 measured an amplitude share $\Delta E_{\mathrm{amp}}=0.023t$ even after 1500 steps at width 128, and the smoke run had re-anchored candidates about $2t$ above the incumbent before relaxation. The quantities to be ranked are smaller: the in-sector gap at the 4×3 avoided crossing is $0.273t$ ($0.023t$ per site), and the Hard-Core scale is $10^{-3}t$ per site. Gate 0: the move set is SCF metadynamics, the maximum-overlap method and shell-occupation scans (Thom and Head-Gordon, PRL 101, 193001, 2008). Gate 2: the round-1 test had no matched-restart null, so its gap and escape fraction were order statistics of 3 to 12 runs, and its "NQS" arm had 378 parameters (effectively Jastrow–Slater). The 6-site smoke run accepted no swap and gave an escape fraction of $-0.24$.
* **Why this kills it**: An acceptance score whose error exceeds the differences it must rank chooses by noise, so the route cannot find the better basin at the Hard Core. Without a null, any gain could be extra restarts, and the move set itself is known.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: discrete re-anchoring with an acceptance that is blind to amplitude error (R2.1, R3.1), or with relaxations run to convergence; instances whose basin gaps exceed the relaxation error.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: with a long enough relaxation the energy does rank correctly (not raised by a critic). Answered: a relaxation that long costs as much as a restart, which is the multi-start baseline the route had to beat.

### R3 — Sign-flip-aware Metropolis moves (round-1 Route 3) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Change the Monte Carlo sampler, adding proposals such as pair hops or exchange moves biased toward sign changes, so that sampling crosses nodal regions and the optimiser sees the configurations whose signs are wrong. It was expected to help if sampling, not optimisation, kept the network in a wrong-sign basin.
* **Lineage**: initial batch (round 1). Not mutated.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G1 (2026-11-10).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 1: does the route violate a structural property of the method? Here: can a sampler change which stationary point SR converges to?
* **Evidence**: On a discrete lattice, Metropolis sampling of $\lvert\psi\rvert^2$ jumps between configurations and never visits the zero-amplitude points between them, so nodes do not confine it. The SR update is a function of expectation values, so in the infinite-sample limit its fixed points do not depend on the sampler. The decisive tests used exact sums, with no sampler at all.
* **Why this kills it**: A sampler change alters only the noise of the expectation values, not the flow they define, so it cannot change which basin is reached.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: noise-driven escape at finite sample size, where the sampler shapes the noise (that is the tempering idea R7.5); variance reduction as an efficiency gain.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: finite-sample SR noise could kick the state between basins, and a sampler shapes that noise (not raised by a critic). Answered indirectly: the tempering version R7.5 was killed because no probed instance had a better basin to escape to.
* **Checked by**: not reviewed by a critic; listed in the master's per-leaf kills.
* **Lesson**: L19 (`library.md`)

### R4 — Hamiltonian continuation from the sign-free or non-interacting limit (round-1 Route 4) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Anneal a Hamiltonian parameter (the interaction $U$ from 0, the next-nearest hopping $t'$ from 0, or a path from a sign-free to a signful model) and warm-start each step from the last, so the network follows the ground state continuously from a limit where its sign is known.
* **Lineage**: initial batch (round 1). Mutated in round 3 into R3.3 (tracking both diabatic branches).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-10; it also failed G3).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0 (known, adoption barrier not fixed) and Gate 3 (does it work only by removing the Hard Core?).
* **Evidence**: Starting from the $U=0$ Slater determinant already is continuation from the non-interacting point and is the default. Annealing in Hamiltonian parameters is common, and its known failure is that it follows the metastable branch through first-order level crossings. The Hard Core consists of exactly such crossings between near-degenerate competing orders.
* **Why this kills it**: The route adds nothing to the default start and inherits its failure at the crossings it must pass.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: paths that start inside the target phase rather than at the non-interacting point, and continuation that tracks several branches (R3.3, killed separately at G0).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: a path in $t'$ that starts where the desired order is the ground state could stay on the right branch (not raised by a critic). Unresolved here; it needs knowledge of the target phase, which is what the problem lacks.

### R2.1 — Symmetrised natural-orbital re-anchoring with fixed-node acceptance, SNO-FN (round-2 Route 2.1, re-audited as Route 3.0) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: After a warm-up, estimate the one-body density matrix of the trained NNBF, average it over the lattice symmetry group and diagonalise it, giving natural orbitals of definite parity. Candidates replace the reference orbitals with the top natural orbitals or frontier swaps, keeping the backflow weights. After $r=40$ relaxation steps each candidate is scored by the guided fixed-node energy (the ten Haaf construction with the candidate's signs), which replaces its amplitudes by the best amplitudes for that sign, so the relaxation-limited amplitude error that killed R2 drops out.
* **Lineage**: Operation A.3 from R2 after its kill at G3 (acceptance retargeted to a sign-only score; moves regenerated from the trained state). Re-audited in round 3 as Route 3.0 (card R3.0); mutated into R3.1.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-11, round-3 re-audit; it also failed G2 and G3).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0 (known, adoption barrier not fixed), Gate 2 (can a fair null match it?) and Gate 3 (does it win only on an instance without the Hard Core?).
* **Evidence**: Gate 0: at scale the acceptance is lattice fixed-node Green's-function Monte Carlo guided by the NQS, which P1.1 killed at Gate 0 (Loehr and Clark, arXiv:2510.26906: its energy depends on the guide). The test computed it as a noise-free eigenvalue, so it could not fail on noise or population-control bias, and its roughly 10 fixed-node solves per cycle were outside the matched budget. Gate 2: the null restarted from the same symmetry-broken UHF, so it had less information than the route. Gate 3: on the target instance (4×3 Hubbard torus, $4+4$ electrons, $t'=-0.25$, $U=8$) projected SR from the same UHF reaches $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}\approx5\times10^{-6}t$ at width 8, against $0.082t$ for unprojected SR at width 32 (job 407525, three seeds). Projecting a 400-step unprojected state after training already cut its sign error from $0.084t$ to $0.019t$.
* **Why this kills it**: Its novel ingredient is an already-killed estimator used without its costs, and on its instance any symmetry-restoring move beats the unprojected null by construction, so a win would say nothing about basins.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: the re-anchoring move with a sampled, budgeted acceptance against projected controls (R3.1).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: the exact fixed-node score is a fair idealisation for a T1 test, and its bias at scale might be small (not raised by a critic). Answered: P1.1's kill rests on the guide dependence, not noise, and the target vanished under projection anyway.

### R2.2 — Fidelity-capped deflation excursions, FCD (round-2 Route 2.2) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: From the incumbent state, run $X$ SR steps on $E(\theta)+\lambda\max(0,F(\theta)-c)^2$, where $F$ is the normalised fidelity with the incumbent, $c$ a cap and $\lambda$ a strength. This pushes the whole network a bounded distance out of its basin; it is then released for $r$ plain SR steps, scored by the fixed-node energy and kept if better. Unlike R2.1 it moves the network, not the reference.
* **Lineage**: Operation B.3, cross-field translation from SCF metadynamics and deflation (round 2). Repaired in round 3 as R3.2 (projection added).
* **Source analogy**: SCF metadynamics biases a self-consistent-field search away from known solutions (Thom and Head-Gordon, PRL 101, 193001, 2008); deflation removes known solutions of nonlinear PDEs so Newton's method finds new ones (Farrell, Birkisson and Funke, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 37, A2026, 2015). Correspondence: known solution ↔ incumbent NQS; deflation factor ↔ capped fidelity penalty. It works there because every distinct solution is wanted. Gap: a ground-state search wants a lower state, and full deflation drives the network to an excited state; the cap was meant to bridge that (Analogy Map B1).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-11, by the round-2 critic).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0: is it known, and is the adoption barrier fixed?
* **Evidence**: The critic found that a capped penalty is SCF metadynamics with a cap and has no independent mechanism; its acceptance is R2.1's fixed-node score, killed at the same time. On the smoke run the excursion was accepted once (cap 0.8) and still ended worse than plain SR (sign-error reduction $-0.038$, one seed, uninformative by design).
* **Why this kills it**: Without a mechanism beyond the cap, the route is a tuning of a known method, and its acceptance is not usable.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: the same excursion inside the target sector with a sampled acceptance (R3.2, killed separately).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: a bounded excursion followed by release is a different dynamic from full deflation, which converges to excited states (not raised by a critic). Unresolved as a mechanism question; no test isolated the cap.
* **Checked by**: the round-2 per-leaf critic (2026-11-11).
* **Lesson**: route-specific — a novelty kill of one construction; the general point about deflation is carried by R3.2's card.

### R2.3 — Irrep scan with fixed-node-ranked selection (round-2 Route 2.3) (KILLED)

The hope was that the sign-only score picks the ground state's symmetry sector where the variational energy does not.
* **Lineage**: Operation B.1 (frame change into the irrep frame), round 2. Kept as diagnostic arm P of the round-2 test; its finding (most of the sign error is symmetry contamination) drove the round-3 pivot to projected training.

On the smoke run both rankings picked the ground state's irrep (1 of 1), so the scored verdict was FAIL.

* **What the kill does not rule out**: its use as a diagnostic, which it remained, and projected training (adopted in round 3).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: on small clusters the competitors do sit in different sectors, so the scan would help there (not raised by a critic). Answered: there plain energy ranking does as well.

### R3.1 — Symmetric natural-orbital re-anchoring of a projected NNBF with sampled-energy acceptance, SNO-PE (round-3 Route 3.1) (KILLED)

### R3.2 — In-sector orthogonal-complement probe, OCP (round-3 Route 3.2) (KILLED)

* **Lineage**: Operation B.2 (active probing), round 3; a translation repair of R2.2 (deflation) by adding sector projection.
* **Source analogy**: deflation in numerical analysis (Farrell, Birkisson and Funke, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 37, A2026, 2015) and overlap-penalty excited states in VMC and NQS (Pathak et al., J. Chem. Phys. 154, 034101, 2021; Choo et al., arXiv:1807.03325). There the target is another eigenstate; here the probe was to certify or beat the ground state of one sector. Gap: the probe still finds a different state, not a lower one, unless the incumbent is not the sector's ground state (Analogy Map B1).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-12, round-3 per-leaf critic).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0: is it known, and is the adoption barrier fixed?
* **Evidence**: The critic found it is SCF metadynamics or deflation, the family already killed as R2.2; arXiv:2605.30944 already combines symmetry projection with an orthogonality penalty; and it cannot escape a basin that is a mixture of two diabatic states. Its premise also failed: 410199 found no trapped run on the tori. On the smoke run its penalty ablation changed nothing (median difference 0.000 over 2 seeds).
* **Why this kills it**: The construction is known, and no instance had a basin for it to escape.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: its use as a certificate that the incumbent is the sector's ground state, which is a diagnostic, not an escape.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: no source found uses the probe as a ground-state escape inside a matched budget (the leaf's own Gate-0 note). Answered by the critic: the use is new but the mechanism is not, and the premise was false.

**The question.** A neural quantum state (NQS) is a neural network that returns the amplitude $\psi_\theta(\mathbf n)$ of a many-body wave function for each occupation configuration $\mathbf n$. For fermions the sign of $\psi_\theta$ carries the antisymmetry, and on doped two-dimensional lattices the signs are the hard part. The ansatz is neural-network backflow (NNBF), a Slater determinant whose orbitals depend on the whole configuration through a small network, trained by stochastic reconfiguration (SR), a natural-gradient method that acts like a small imaginary-time step and never flips a sign directly. A network started from unrestricted Hartree–Fock (UHF) might therefore stay in a *wrong-sign basin*: signs worse than the best the network could hold, which SR never leaves. If such basins exist, a cheap optimiser fix beats a bigger network. Sign quality is $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}=E_{\mathrm{sign}}-E_0$, where $E_0$ is the exact ground energy from exact diagonalization (ED) and $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ the lowest energy reachable with the trained signs fixed and the best non-negative amplitudes; it vanishes only for the exact signs. "Projected" means the output is summed over the lattice symmetry group $G$, $\psi_P=P_\chi\psi_\theta$, so the state carries the ground state's symmetry labels $\chi$.

**The chain of evidence.** First, the early basins were symmetry contamination. On a 4×3 Hubbard torus an unprojected width-32 NNBF had $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}=0.082t$; training with projection from the same UHF start reached $5\times10^{-6}t$ at width 8 (job 407525). After projection only a same-sector competitor can be a basin, and on the smallest-gap torus found ($\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}=0.234t$) all nine projected cold runs reached $\psi_0$ (410199). Second, on the 4×4 OBC cluster (dimension 1,681,680) a projected width-6 NNBF keeps $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}=0.0125t$ (451706). Three cold arms from different references and an oracle arm W, fidelity-trained toward $\psi_0$ and released, got equal energy steps under a pre-registered rule. The best cold error fell 0.0192, 0.0117 and 0.0070 at widths 4, 6 and 10 (1284, 1734, 2634 parameters), and W ended at 0.0189, 0.0115 and 0.0070, within margins of 0.003 to 0.005 (507179, 625182). Every width stayed formally unconverged, so the pre-registered width-trend tie-break returned CAPACITY. Third, because W never started below the cold level, two toy probes placed a better-signed state directly: the target keeps the cold state's amplitudes and inserts the exact signs. On a projected 4×3 $t$–$J$ toy (675 parameters), first-order SR reached 0.0092 against a cold 0.0109 and Levenberg–Marquardt (LM, second order) 0.0085, both far from the threshold 0.0055 (R7.1). Released, the states returned to the cold level at slightly higher energy. At $\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}=0.12t$ no cold run was captured by the competitor (R7.3).

**Why the alternatives fail.** Energy-accepted orbital swaps cannot rank basins below the relaxation's amplitude error (R2); fixed-node acceptance reuses a killed estimator (R2.1); capped deflation and orthogonal probes are SCF metadynamics (R2.2, R3.2); sampler changes cannot move SR's fixed points (R3); continuation keeps the metastable branch (R1, R4, R3.3); better local optimisers converge into the same basin (R2.4, R7.2); tempering needs a basin that is absent (R7.5); projection-cost ideas cannot be judged with exact sums (R3.4, R7.4); and the one surviving escape route lost its premise (R3.1).
