### 1. Mathematical Abstraction & Barriers
* **Formulation**: Let $\hat H$ be the doped Hubbard Hamiltonian of the Invariants on a finite cluster, and let $\{\hat O_X\}_{X\in\{A,B\}}$ be order observables (e.g. the stripe or $d$-wave correlation $C(r)$ at the longest distance) that separate two competing orders. Define the competitor energies $E_X^\star=\min\{\langle\psi\rvert\hat H\lvert\psi\rangle:\ \psi\ \text{has order character}\ X\}$; on a finite cluster these are sharp only when the characters are separated by a quantum number, otherwise they are the eigenvalues of the lowest two eigenstates whose characters differ. The gap is $\delta=E_B^\star-E_A^\star$, with $\lvert\delta\rvert/V$ of order $10^{-3}t$ or smaller ($V$ is the number of sites).
  *Input*: two families of variational states $\{\psi_X^{(p)}\}_{p=0}^{P}$ (accuracy level $p$, e.g. NQS size or number of Lanczos steps), each initialised or constrained toward order $X$, with Monte Carlo estimates of the energy $E_X^{(p)}=\langle\hat H\rangle$, the variance $\sigma^{2\,(p)}_X=\langle\hat H^2\rangle-\langle\hat H\rangle^2$ and the order character $c_X^{(p)}=\langle\hat O\rangle$.
  *Output*: a decision $D\in\{A,B,\text{undecided}\}$ together with a confidence level.
  *Solved means*: (i) **soundness**: $\Pr[D\neq\text{undecided}\ \wedge\ D\neq\mathrm{sgn}\text{-truth}]$ is below a stated level (the error bars are calibrated, i.e. the intervals around the extrapolated intercepts cover $E_X^\star$ at the nominal rate); (ii) **power**: $D$ is not undecided whenever $\lvert\delta\rvert$ exceeds a resolution that shrinks as $\sigma^2\to0$; (iii) the cost is the cost of the two runs times a constant (no massive compute); (iv) the result does not depend on which mean-field reference seeded which branch (Unacceptable Outcome, first bullet).
* **The Node Barrier**: The standard implementation (zero-variance extrapolation of each branch, then compare intercepts) is already the method of choice: Imada group VMC stripe vs. $d$-wave studies (Ido, Ohgoe & Imada, arXiv:1712.05490; Darmawan et al., PRB 98, 205132, 2018, arXiv:1808.06327), the stripe benchmark (Zheng et al., Science 358, 1155, 2017), and the latest NQS paper on intertwined orders (Viteritti et al., arXiv:2604.21978, zero-variance fit of the Pfaffian at each accuracy level). It halts against the Hard Core at four specific points:
  1. *Variance-invisible contamination (fundamental)*. Write a branch as $\psi=\sqrt{1-\epsilon}\,\lvert X^\star\rangle+\sqrt{\epsilon_Y}\,\lvert Y^\star\rangle+\sqrt{\epsilon_r}\,\lvert r\rangle$, with $\lvert Y^\star\rangle$ the competitor and $\lvert r\rangle$ the high-energy residual. Then to leading order $E-E_X^\star\approx\epsilon_Y\delta+\epsilon_r(\bar E_r-E_X^\star)$ while $\sigma^2\approx\epsilon_r\langle(\hat H-E_X^\star)^2\rangle_r+\mathcal O(\epsilon_Y\delta^2)$. The admixture $\epsilon_Y$ moves the energy by up to $\delta$ and leaves no trace in $\sigma^2$, so no extrapolation in $\sigma^2$ can remove it. When $\epsilon_Y>1/2$ the branch has *crossed over*, both intercepts converge to the same eigenvalue, and the initial label is wrong.
  2. *Nonlinearity and unequal extrapolation lengths (practical)*. Linearity $E\approx E^\star+a\sigma^2$ holds only once $\epsilon_r\ll1$. NQS branches typically sit at $\sigma^2/V\sim10^{-2}t^2$, and the two branches have different slopes $a_X$ because their residuals differ. The fit-form systematic (linear versus quadratic intercept) is then comparable to $\delta$.
  3. *Boundary and finite-size pinning (practical)*. On cylinders, open edges pin one order (review arXiv:2608.21291 §3.2, Gu et al. arXiv:2507.02644), so $\delta$ itself changes sign with geometry.
  4. *Differential expressivity (fundamental)*. If the ansatz represents order $A$ worse than order $B$, both branches carry a bias that differs by more than $\delta$. Only the $\sigma^2$-correlated part of that bias is extrapolated away.
* **Anti-Solution Space**: (a) comparing raw variational energies (that is the problem itself); (b) quoting the linear-fit standard error alone as the error bar (it ignores fit-form systematics and contamination); (c) declaring a winner from the order character of a *single* run seeded by one reference (the Unacceptable Outcome); (d) rigorous eigenvalue inclusion bounds at finite energy density (see Route 3); (e) any route that needs $\lvert\langle\psi_A\vert\psi_B\rangle\rvert$ estimated by ratio sampling (exponentially small for macroscopically distinct orders); (f) larger networks or larger sample counts as the fix (massive compute).

* **What the map shows.** Every source technique that carried over relies on one of three properties: a controllable error knob with known form, overlapping supports between the two hypotheses, or errors that are common-mode because both sides share local structure. The Hard Core removes all three at once for order-distinct same-irrep competitors, which is why every tried translation died at the same mechanism (the differential bias $\Delta b$ passes through unchanged). The one translation that worked in exact arithmetic (a symmetric Lanczos step, from the error-extrapolation row) loses under sampling below the Hard-Core scale and is not size consistent.
### 2. Candidate Routes & Falsification Gates
* **Route 1**: *Calibrated independent-branch extrapolation*. Formalize current practice: per-branch linear $E$-vs-$\sigma^2$ fit, error bar $=\sqrt{\mathrm{SE}_{\mathrm{lin}}^2+(I_{\mathrm{lin}}-I_{\mathrm{quad}})^2}$ (here $\mathrm{SE}_{\mathrm{lin}}$ is the standard error of the linear-fit intercept and $I_{\mathrm{lin}},I_{\mathrm{quad}}$ are the linear and quadratic intercepts), bootstrap over fit windows, decide when the intercept difference exceeds $z$ times the combined error ($z$ is the confidence multiplier).
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): FAIL. This is exactly what Ido, Ohgoe & Imada (arXiv:1712.05490), Darmawan et al. (arXiv:1808.06327) and Viteritti et al. (arXiv:2604.21978) already do, and Sorella's VMC+Lanczos extrapolation (PRB 64, 024512, 2001) underlies it. It has not settled the NQS disagreement (Gu et al. arXiv:2507.02644 versus Roth et al. arXiv:2511.07566) because of barriers 1 and 2. A better error model does not touch the variance-invisible $\epsilon_Y\delta$ term or crossover. Relabelling without a fix.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Consistent as $\sigma^2\to0$.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): FAIL. Branch labels come from the seeding reference. For Minimal-Contrast-Foil branches (Jastrow–Slater with stripe versus uniform BCS references) that never leave their basin, the rule reports the reference.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. No gap or filling assumption.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
* **Route 2**: *Sector-resolved branches*. Bias each branch by a quantum number instead of an initialization (momentum, point-group parity, total spin $S$), project exactly (cost $\propto\lvert G\rvert$ for symmetry group $G$), add $p$ Lanczos steps inside the sector, and detect the level crossing between sector energies.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS for novelty as applied to fermionic stripe decisions, but the machinery is standard. Choo et al., PRL 121, 167204 (2018) handle symmetry-resolved NQS excited states; Hu, Becca, Parola & Sorella, PRB 88, 060402 (2013) apply sector VMC+Lanczos to $J_1$–$J_2$.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Sectors are exact, and branches cannot cross over.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. Sector energies do not depend on the reference.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): FAIL. The competitors of interest (filled $\lambda=8$ versus half-filled $\lambda=4$ stripe, with or without $d$-wave) are symmetry-broken states whose translation- and point-group-symmetrised versions land in the *same* sector on any cluster commensurate with both. The route only works when competitors carry different quantum numbers, which removes the same-sector near-degeneracy half of the Hard Core.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
* **Route 3**: *Rigorous eigenvalue brackets*. Pair each branch's variational upper bound with a Temple lower bound $E_0\ge E-\sigma^2/(\beta-E)$, where $\beta$ is a lower bound on the first excited energy (Temple, Proc. R. Soc. A 119, 276, 1928, doi:10.1098/rspa.1928.0098), or with Weinstein's inclusion $[E-\sigma,E+\sigma]$. Decide when the brackets separate.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS. These bounds are classical but unused for NQS order decisions.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): FAIL. Temple needs $\beta$ with $E<\beta\le E_1$, but $E_1-E_0\to0$ with $V$ in the doped (gapless or nearly degenerate) regime, so the bound degenerates. Weinstein's window has width $2\sigma\propto\sqrt V$ at fixed variance per site, and at finite energy density it contains exponentially many eigenvalues, so it never identifies *which* state the branch approximates.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): not evaluated (killed).
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): FAIL. The route relies on a large gap.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
* **Route 4**: *Jointly optimized orthogonal two-state ensemble with character-labelled Ritz extrapolation*. Replace the two independent branches by one two-state variational object, $\Psi(\mathbf n_1,\mathbf n_2)=\det\begin{pmatrix}\psi_1(\mathbf n_1)&\psi_1(\mathbf n_2)\\\psi_2(\mathbf n_1)&\psi_2(\mathbf n_2)\end{pmatrix}$, as in natural-excited-state VMC (Pfau et al., Science 385, eadn0137, 2024, arXiv:2308.16848; lattice variant: Ma et al., arXiv:2506.08594). Initialise $\psi_1$ toward order $A$ and $\psi_2$ toward order $B$, and minimise the ensemble energy $\mathrm{tr}\,(S^{-1}H)$. Here $S_{ij}=\langle\psi_i\vert\psi_j\rangle$ and $H_{ij}=\langle\psi_i\rvert\hat H\lvert\psi_j\rangle$. By the Gross–Oliveira–Kohn ensemble principle (PRA 37, 2805, 1988) the trace is an upper bound to $E_0+E_1$. Joint sampling from $\lvert\Psi\rvert^2$ gives an estimator of the matrix $S^{-1}H$ whose variance does not blow up when the two states are nearly orthogonal. This is the concrete fix for the exponential cost of independent-branch cross estimators. At every accuracy level, diagonalise $S^{-1}H$ to get Ritz roots $\chi_0,\chi_1$ with energies $\theta_0\le\theta_1$. Measure each root's variance and order character $c(\chi_i)=\langle\hat O\rangle_{\chi_i}$. Extrapolate each root along its own $(\sigma^2,\theta)$ path, label the roots by *measured* character, and decide using the character-labelled intercepts. If both roots carry the same pure character, decide that character: the competitor then lies above the two lowest states.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS. Ensemble or NES excited-state methods exist (Pfau et al. 2024; Ma et al. 2025), and per-branch variance extrapolation exists (Route 1). A WebSearch found no use of a jointly optimized ensemble as a *ground-state order decision rule* for doped Hubbard NQS. The adoption barrier of Route 1 is crossover and seed-label dependence (barrier 1). The mechanism here fixes exactly that: the pair spans a subspace, the labels come from Ritz diagonalisation plus measured character rather than from the seed, and a branch that drifts toward the other order is absorbed by the $2\times2$ diagonalisation instead of corrupting a label.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. $N_e$, $S_z$ and lattice symmetries are untouched. As $\sigma^2\to0$ the Ritz roots become the two lowest eigenstates (Hylleraas–Undheim–MacDonald interlacing). The cost is about $2\times$ a single run plus $2\times2$ algebra per sample. The naive non-orthogonal-CI alternative, which builds $\langle\psi_A\rvert\hat H\lvert\psi_B\rangle$ by ratio sampling from $\lvert\psi_A\rvert^2$, has variance $\propto\lvert\langle\psi_A\vert\psi_B\rangle\rvert^{-2}=e^{\mathcal O(V)}$ and is excluded. The joint-sampling estimator avoids this.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. The decision is invariant under swapping which reference seeded which state, because it depends only on the spanned subspace and measured characters. A foil pair stuck in its references still gives an answer, but that answer comes from the measured energies of both orders inside one variational object, not from the seed. The test's R0 versus R0+ versus R4 ablation quantifies exactly this.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. No gap is assumed for the *decision* (the lowest root's character). The gap above the two-state manifold enters only the error bar, through linearity of the Ritz extrapolation. The route works at any doping and $t'$, and the test runs doped with $t'\neq0$.
  - *Verdict*: SURVIVED
### 3. Top Surviving Route & Sharpest Gap
* **Mechanism**: Route 4. Two biased branches become one jointly optimized, orthogonality-free two-state ensemble (determinant of states). Branch labels are reassigned after Ritz diagonalisation by the measured order character, and each Ritz root is zero-variance-extrapolated along its own path with a fit-form systematic in the error bar. This removes crossover and seed-label dependence, which are the two failure modes that keep independent-branch extrapolation (current practice) from settling NQS stripe disputes. Its cost is a constant factor (about $2\times$).
* **Sharpest Gap**: It is unverified that the Ritz roots of an imperfect two-state subspace have *pure* order character, and that their $\theta$-vs-$\sigma^2$ paths are linear enough for calibrated error bars, near a crossing where $\lvert\delta\rvert$ is far below the variational error. The smoke test already shows that with poor references (initial energy error of about 40%) the joint roots can be strongly mixed ($\lvert\langle\hat R\rangle\rvert$ below $0.06$ at one control value), which makes the rule abstain. Whether the joint step beats simply relabelling independent branches by measured character (ablation R0+) is the open question the test decides. A second, unproven assumption is that the NES $S^{-1}H$ estimator keeps bounded variance for *lattice-fermion* states whose orders differ macroscopically. It is demonstrated for molecules (Pfau et al.) and spins/ions (Ma et al.) only.
### 4. 24-Hour Decisive Test & Compute Execution
* **Minimal Instance**: Two-leg Hubbard ladder $6\times2$ with periodic legs and open rungs ($V=12$), $N_\uparrow=N_\downarrow=5$ (doping $\delta_h=1/6$), $U=8t$, control $g=t'\in[-0.4,0.4]$ on 17 points. The leg mirror $\hat R:(x,y)\mapsto(5-x,y)$ defines competitor $A$ (lowest $\hat R$-even state) and $B$ (lowest $\hat R$-odd state), with exact energies from sector Lanczos ED (Hilbert dimension $627{,}264$). A login-node check found a genuine crossing: $\delta=E_B^\star-E_A^\star=-0.0549t$ at $g=-0.4$, $+0.0025t$ at $g=0$ (that is $2\times10^{-4}t$ per site, at the Hard-Core scale) and $+0.145t$ at $g=0.4$. The system is doped and has $t'\neq0$, so it is non-stoquastic; the periodic two-leg geometry is not the excluded open 1D chain. Biased branches emulate NQS runs. The reference is $\phi_X\propto\mathrm{mask}_{f_0}(\psi_X^\star)\odot(\text{sign flips on a fraction }q)+\eta\,\psi_Y^\star$. Here $\mathrm{mask}_{f_0}$ keeps the top fraction $f_0$ of configurations by $\lvert\psi_X^\star\rvert$, $q$ is the fraction of kept small-amplitude configurations with flipped sign (an emulated sign error), and $\eta\sim\mathcal U[0,\eta_{\max}]$ is the admixture of the competing order. Accuracy level $p=0,\dots,4$ is the Krylov space $K_p(\hat H,\phi_X)$ for independent branches and the block Krylov space $K_p(\hat H,\{\phi_A,\phi_B\})$ (same number of $\hat H$ applications per state) for the joint ensemble. The order observable is $\langle\hat R\rangle$, a stand-in for $C(r)$. The rules never see the sector projectors. Three rules are compared: R0 (seed labels, current practice), R0+ (independent branches relabelled by measured character) and R4 (joint ensemble Ritz roots).
* **Scalar Metric $M$**: Let $\mathcal I$ be the set of (g, seed) instances ($17\times8=136$). For rule $R$, $\mathrm{CW}(R)=\lvert\{i\in\mathcal I: D_R(i)\notin\{\text{undecided},\text{truth}(i)\}\}\rvert/\lvert\mathcal I\rvert$ (the confident-wrong rate) and $\mathrm{DEC}(R)=\lvert\{i: D_R(i)\neq\text{undecided}\}\rvert/\lvert\mathcal I\rvert$, where $\text{truth}(i)=A$ if $\delta>0$ else $B$. The rule decides when the labelled intercept difference exceeds $z=2$ times $\sqrt{\mathrm{err}_A^2+\mathrm{err}_B^2}$, where $\mathrm{err}=\sqrt{\mathrm{SE}_{\mathrm{lin}}^2+(I_{\mathrm{lin}}-I_{\mathrm{quad}})^2}$ is computed over the last 3 levels. * **A structural lemma that drives the batch (2×2 effective model)**. Near the crossing, write the irrep problem as $\hat H_{\mathrm{eff}}=\begin{pmatrix}E_A&W\\W&E_B\end{pmatrix}$, where $E_A,E_B$ are the diabatic energies and $W$ their coupling, with $\delta=E_B-E_A$. A variational method sees diagonal energies $E_X+b_X$, where $b_X\ge0$ is the variational bias of order $X$, and a coupling $W'$. Every rule that orders the two states from variational energies alone therefore has its decision boundary at $\delta+\Delta b=0$, with $\Delta b=b_B-b_A$. This includes energy comparison, the kink of the lower envelope, the curvature peak, the fidelity-susceptibility peak, and the field-envelope kink. Two consequences follow:
    * The Hellmann–Feynman slopes add no information. For a variational optimum, $\mathrm{d}E_{\mathrm{var}}/\mathrm{d}t'=\langle\hat H_{t'}\rangle_{\mathrm{var}}$, the derivative of the same biased curve.
    * The only levers are a smaller differential bias (common-mode or extrapolated), or avoiding the place where the bias is worst: the hard point itself, where projected branches in one irrep can slide into each other's basin.

        * $\hat E_0$ is a zero-variance extrapolation from sampled quantities only: the smaller of two intercepts of $E$ against $\sigma^2$ (all branch states at $d$, and the lower-energy order only), minus 2 standard errors of the intercept;
        * rationale: $E_B-E_A=\beta(d-d_c)+\Delta b$ with $\lvert\Delta b\rvert\le\max_X\varepsilon_X$ near the crossing.
    4. Commit B if $d<\hat d_c-(t_{S-1}\mathrm{SE}+b)$, A if $d>\hat d_c+(t_{S-1}\mathrm{SE}+b)$, otherwise abstain. Also abstain if fewer than 4 usable ladder points remain or $\beta_{\mathrm{lo}}\le0$.
* **Certificate.** The interval $\hat d_c\pm(t_{S-1}\mathrm{SE}+b)$ has two parts:
    * a noise part, from the seed scatter of $\hat d_c$;
    * a bias part, from the sampled $\hat\varepsilon$ divided by the slope.
    * A commit asserts that $d$ lies outside this interval. Every input is a sampled quantity: VMC energies and variances, and the sign of $c$.
    * It is an *estimated* certificate, not a theorem. Its weakest link is $\hat E_0$, the zero-variance intercept; with the exact $E_0$ the oracle variant is printed alongside.
* **Rule 4.** The decision energies are replaced by i.i.d.-VMC estimates with $10^6$ draws per final state (Gaussian noise of variance $\sigma^2_X/10^6$), over 50 replicas. The script reports CW, DEC and the flip rate against the exact-energy decision. This is an emulation: the round-4 script does not save the target-state parameters, so real sampling of the final states is not possible.

#### $2\times2$-lemma check: K1 and ESR0 are KILLED
* **Argument (exact for K1).** Write the trained pair in orthonormalised form. The Ritz matrix is $h=\begin{pmatrix}E_\psi&w\\w&E_\phi\end{pmatrix}$ with $w=\langle\psi\rvert\hat H\lvert\phi_1\rangle$. For any real symmetric $2\times2$ matrix the lowest eigenvector carries weight $\ge\tfrac12$ on the basis vector with the lower diagonal, whatever $w$ is. So the character of $\chi_0$ has the sign of the lower-energy trained state, up to the cross term $2\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\langle\psi\rvert\hat O\lvert\phi_1\rangle$. That term is small for near-diabatic states, because the diabats nearly diagonalise $\hat O$.
    * Hence the K1 decision is the energy rule between the two trained states, with boundary $\delta+\Delta b'=0$. Here $\Delta b'$ is the differential bias of the pair, and $\Delta b'\ge\Delta b$ of the order-seeded pair, because the penalty constraint can only raise $b$ of $\phi_1$.
    * $w$ only sets how far $\chi_0$ rotates, never which basis vector dominates it.
* **ESR0 (adds $(\hat H-E_\psi)\psi$).** In the diabatic picture $\psi=\lvert A\rangle+r_A$, and $(\hat H-E_\psi)\psi$ contains the competitor direction only with amplitude $W$, the diabatic coupling. Its norm is dominated by the residual term $\lVert(\hat H-E)r_A\rVert^2\approx\sigma^2_\psi$.
    * Width-4 numbers (job 451852, warm arm): $\sigma^2_A\approx0.04$ and $\sigma^2_B\approx0.10$–$0.12$, against $W^2\approx(5\times10^{-4})^2$ (half the $1.0\times10^{-3}t$ gap minimum on the path).
    * The added vector therefore acts as a one-sided Lanczos step: it lowers $b$ of $\psi$ only. When $\psi$ is the wrong (better-represented) order, it widens the wrong margin.
* **Size of the gap to close.** Recomputed from 451852 (warm, width 4), the differential bias $\Delta b=b_B-b_A$ is $+3.6\times10^{-3}t$ to $+5.4\times10^{-3}t$ on every inner target of seeds 1234 and 3456, and $+0.4\times10^{-3}t$ to $+1.4\times10^{-3}t$ on seed 2345. The diabatic coupling is $W\approx5\times10^{-4}t$. On a conical-intersection path, $W$ is half the minimum gap and vanishes at the CI by construction, so any information carried only by off-diagonal elements is bounded by $W\ll\Delta b$.
* **Verdict: K1 and ESR0 KILLED by the lemma** (Gate 2: they reduce to the naive energy rule between two trained states). $K=2$ adds nothing: the third in-irrep level sits about $0.67t$ higher.
* **What survives the argument.** Only a *symmetric* reduction of both biases moves the boundary: one block-Lanczos step, $\mathrm{BL}=\mathrm{span}\{\psi,(\hat H-E_\psi)\psi,\phi_1,(\hat H-E_1)\phi_1\}$. The larger-variance branch gains more, which shrinks $\Delta b$. That lever is an energy (capacity) lever and not non-energy information, and it fails Gate 0 as a method. VMC plus Lanczos steps is Sorella, PRB 64, 024512 (2001), is used in mVMC competing-order studies (Ido, Ohgoe & Imada, arXiv:1712.05490), and has been applied to NQS (Chen, Hendry, Weinberg & Feiguin, arXiv:2206.14307).

### R1.1 — Calibrated independent-branch extrapolation (round-1 Route 1) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Formalise current practice. Each of two independently trained branches, seeded toward order $A$ or $B$, is extrapolated linearly in energy against energy variance to zero variance; the error bar adds the fit standard error and the difference between linear and quadratic intercepts, and the rule decides when the intercept difference exceeds twice the combined error. The hope was that honest error bars alone make the decision controlled.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, round 1 (2026-11-10). Its zero-variance component reappeared as R3.4 (in-irrep version) and its relabelling as the R0+ foil.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-10), with a G2 failure as well.
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0, "is it known, and is the reason it is not decisive in practice fixed?"; Gate 2, "does the foil reach the same metric?"
* **Evidence**: The method is what Ido, Ohgoe & Imada (arXiv:1712.05490), Darmawan et al. (arXiv:1808.06327) and Viteritti et al. (arXiv:2604.21978) already do, on the basis of Sorella's VMC+Lanczos extrapolation (PRB 64, 024512, 2001). The variance-invisible admixture of the competitor moves the energy by up to $\epsilon_Y\delta$ ($\epsilon_Y$ the competitor weight) while leaving the variance unchanged at order $\epsilon_Y\delta^2$ (section 1, barrier 1).
* **Why this kills it**: Better error bars do not touch the admixture term or branch crossover, the reasons practice has not settled the NQS stripe dispute; and for foil branches that never leave their basin the rule returns whichever reference seeded the lower branch, the same answer as the foil.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: in-irrep zero-variance comparison as a sign-safe tool when neither branch has crossed over (kept as the R3.4 lemma); better error models as practice.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: Calibrated bars could at least make the rule abstain correctly instead of committing wrongly, which is a gain in soundness. Answer: a gain in soundness without power is what the naive rule with final-state sampling error already gives (round 6); not raised by a critic.
* **Checked by**: round-1 per-leaf critic (2026-11-11), no objection to this kill.
* **Lesson**: route-specific — it restates the problem (seed-dependent answers) rather than a mechanism.
### R1.2 — Sector-resolved branches (round-1 Route 2) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Bias each branch by an exact quantum number (momentum, point-group parity, total spin) instead of by an initialisation, project exactly at a cost proportional to the group size, add Lanczos steps inside each sector, and decide from the crossing of the sector energies. Sectors are exact, so branches cannot cross over into each other.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, round 1. Its machinery (exact irrep projection) became the basis of every arm from round 3 on.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G3 (2026-11-10).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 3, "does it work only by removing the Hard Core?"
* **Evidence**: The competitors of interest (filled $\lambda=8$ versus half-filled $\lambda=4$ stripes, with or without $d$-wave) are symmetry-broken states whose symmetrised versions land in the same sector on any cluster commensurate with both. The round-2 ED sector analysis confirmed the point on $4\times3$: the earlier "same-sector" competitor was a spin triplet at momentum $(\pi,\pm2\pi/3)$, $0.086t$ above $E_0$, while the in-sector gap was $0.515t$.
* **Why this kills it**: The route needs the competitors to carry different quantum numbers, which removes the same-sector near-degeneracy half of the Hard Core.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: competitors that genuinely differ by a quantum number, where the route is standard and correct (Choo et al., PRL 121, 167204, 2018; Hu, Becca, Parola & Sorella, PRB 88, 060402, 2013).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: On large clusters, stripe periods could carry different momenta under translations that are not symmetries of both. Answer: on clusters commensurate with both the symmetrised states share sectors, and the comparison must be made on such clusters; not raised by a critic.
* **Checked by**: round-1 per-leaf critic (2026-11-11), which used the same argument to kill R1.4's test.

### R1.3 — Temple and Weinstein eigenvalue brackets (round-1 Route 3) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Pair each branch's variational upper bound with a rigorous lower bound, Temple's $E_0\ge E-\sigma^2/(\beta-E)$ with $\beta$ a lower bound on the first excited energy, or Weinstein's inclusion window $[E-\sigma,E+\sigma]$, and decide when the brackets of the two orders separate. This would give a certified decision.
* **Lineage**: initial batch, round 1.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G1 (2026-11-10), also failing G3.
* **Kill rule**: Gate 1, "does it violate a conservation law, a symmetry, or a complexity bound?" (here, an asymptotic bound in $V$).
* **Evidence**: Temple needs $E<\beta\le E_1$, and $E_1-E_0\to0$ with $V$ in the doped regime. Weinstein's window has width $2\sigma\propto\sqrt V$ at fixed variance per site and at finite energy density contains exponentially many eigenvalues (Temple, Proc. R. Soc. A 119, 276, 1928).
* **Why this kills it**: The Temple bound degenerates exactly where orders compete, and the Weinstein window cannot identify which eigenstate a branch approximates, so neither bracket can separate.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: bounds on small gapped clusters, and gap-based certificates far from the crossing (the R5.1 abstain rule, valid at $\lvert d\rvert\ge0.002$ at width 8).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: In one irrep the relevant gap is the in-irrep gap, not the global one, and it can stay finite. Answer: at a same-irrep crossing the in-irrep gap is the one that closes; not raised by a critic.

### R1.4a — Non-orthogonal CI of independently optimised branches (sub-variant of round-1 Route 4) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Diagonalise $\hat H$ in the span of two independently optimised order branches, building the cross terms $\langle\psi_A\rvert\hat H\lvert\psi_B\rangle$ by ratio sampling from $\lvert\psi_A\rvert^2$. The $2\times2$ diagonalisation would mix the orders and produce a ground state whose character decides.
* **Lineage**: sub-variant considered inside round-1 Route 4; mutated (Operation A.2) into R2.1, which samples the superposition directly.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G1 (2026-11-10).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 1, "does it violate a complexity bound?"
* **Evidence**: The ratio estimator has variance proportional to $\lvert\langle\psi_A\vert\psi_B\rangle\rvert^{-2}$, which is $e^{\mathcal O(V)}$ for macroscopically distinct orders. At 12 sites the Bhattacharyya overlap $B=\sum_n\lvert\psi_A(n)\psi_B(n)\rvert$ of the two order snapshots is already $0.20$ (reopening-review T1).
* **Why this kills it**: The sample cost of the cross terms grows exponentially with system size, against the solved-criterion (iii) of constant-factor cost.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: overlaps between states of the same order, which stay $\mathcal O(1)$ (used by R3.5).

### R2.1 — Two-reference shared-backflow superposition, 2R-SS (round-2 Route 2.1) (KILLED)

Fit $E_B-E_A$ linearly along a common-start (warm) ladder of target points for each seed, pool the crossing estimates $\hat d_c$ over seeds, and commit only outside the bracket $\hat d_c\pm(t\,\mathrm{SE}+b)$, where $b=\hat\varepsilon/\beta_{\mathrm{lo}}$ is a sampled bias margin (energy error over the lower slope bound). It never optimises at the target, never needs $\Delta_1$, and needs no cross-order term.
* **Lineage**: Operation B (new mechanism), round 5; repair of R3.1 with a bias margin. Its key finding (window noise overstates the decision noise) retired the window-noise rule.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at CRITIC (2026-11-14, round-5 per-leaf critic), before its decisive array (about 90 task-hours on 16 CPUs) was submitted.
* **Kill rule**: the critic's questions: is it distinct from the killed crossing detector (Gate 0), is its bias margin conservative, and can its test tell it from a trivial rule?
* **Evidence**: (i) Linear crossing location is R3.1 (the Corboz–Mila hysteresis protocol, arXiv:1802.00874) plus a margin. (ii) The margin rests on a single-width zero-variance $\hat E_0$ that was off by up to $+2.2\times10^{-3}t$ at width 4, the anti-conservative direction. (iii) The test ladder was centred on the exact ED crossing, so "the centre of the ladder" also scores perfectly. In-sample (job 451852 data, not evidence for a verdict): width 8 LCC decided BBUUAA (truth BBBAAA, inner DEC 0.50, CW 0), width 4 UUUUUA; the plain lower-final-energy rule was right on all 6 targets for all 3 seeds at width 8.
* **Why this kills it**: Its novelty reduces to a margin whose key input is anti-conservative, and its pre-registered test could not separate it from a trivial ladder-centre rule.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: a crossing bracket with a rigorous margin, or a ladder placed blind to $t'_c$ (the requirement the critic set for any further test).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: The noise part of LCC is real: within-seed residuals of the warm ladder are $(1.7$–$2.3)\times10^{-5}t$ against SR-window noise of $(1.9$–$2.7)\times10^{-3}t$. ### R6.1 — Excited-state Ritz with one penalty-trained state, K1 (round-6 ESR) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Train the ground state $\psi$ and one penalty-orthogonal in-irrep excited state $\phi_1$ by sequential penalty VMC (Choo et al., PRL 121, 167204, 2018; P3.1's round-7 machinery), diagonalise $\hat H$ in their span, and decide from the order character of the lowest Ritz vector. The Ritz rotation was claimed to be non-energy information that escapes the $2\times2$ lemma.
* **Lineage**: transfer of P3.1's ESR0 mechanism (round 6, 2026-11-14), triggered by the round-5 Saturation Diagnostic's "non-energy information" lever.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G2 (2026-11-14), confirmed empirically by job 619080.
* **Kill rule**: Gate 2, "does a cheap baseline or the foil reach the same metric?"; the kill check's pre-registered clause, quoted: "KILL-CONFIRMED if, on every instance in $N$, both K1 and ESR0 are wrong or abstain, and the K1 decision equals the lower-diagonal prediction on every instance".
* **Evidence**: Lemma: for a real symmetric $2\times2$ matrix the lowest eigenvector has weight at least $\tfrac12$ on the basis vector with the lower diagonal, whatever the coupling $w$, so the K1 decision is the energy rule between $\psi$ and $\phi_1$. Off-diagonal information is bounded by the diabatic coupling $W\approx5\times10^{-4}t$ against measured $\Delta b=3.6$–$5.4\times10^{-3}t$ on two of three width-4 seeds. Job 619080 (84 CPU-h, width 4, 9 instances): K1 equals the lower-diagonal prediction on 9 of 9 and is right on 4 of 9; every error is a truth-$B$ instance with $\psi$ in $A$. The pre-registered verdict was INCONCLUSIVE because the naive rule never committed wrong ($\lvert N\rvert=0$) and the positive control missed 4 of 9.
* **Why this kills it**: The lemma makes K1 the energy rule between two trained states with a differential bias at least that of the order-seeded pair, so it can only match the foil; the measurement agrees on every instance.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: Ritz with exact excited states where the trained ground state is wrong (the positive control fixes those), and lattices where $W$ is not small compared with $\Delta b$.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: The pre-registered verdict was INCONCLUSIVE, not KILL-CONFIRMED. Answer: both triggering clauses were design artefacts (the naive rule abstained instead of erring; the positive control was a perfect partner only when $\psi$ was wrong), and the lemma held on 9 of 9; the critic accepted the kill.
* **Checked by**: leaf-summary critic (2026-11-16) and the cross-node critic (2026-11-16), which checked the numbers against job 619080.
* **Lesson**: L16 (`library.md`)

* **Lineage**: direct transfer of P3.1's ESR0 (round 6).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G2 (2026-11-14), confirmed by job 619080.

### R7.2 — Mixture-paired difference estimator (reopening candidate C2) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Draw configurations from $p=\tfrac12(\lvert\psi_A\rvert^2+\lvert\psi_B\rvert^2)$, whose importance weights are bounded by 2, and estimate $E_A-E_B$ from the same draws so that shared noise cancels in the difference, attacking ingredient I2 (the noise of the difference exceeds the gap).
* **Lineage**: Operation A on ingredient I2 (2026-11-16); cross-field translation from correlated sampling and common random numbers.
* **Source analogy**: In discrete-event simulation (common random numbers; Law & Kelton, ch. 11) and in QMC (correlated sampling, Filippi & Umrigar, cond-mat/9911326), two systems driven by the same random inputs give outputs with positive covariance, so $\mathrm{Var}(X-Y)$ shrinks. Correspondence: random stream ↔ shared draws. It works when both systems respond similarly to the same inputs (overlapping supports). Gap: order-distinct wave functions have nearly disjoint supports.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G4 (2026-11-16), also failing G0.
* **Kill rule**: Gate 4, "does its gain survive growing system size?"
* **Evidence**: The variance reduction needs the Bhattacharyya overlap $B=\sum_n\lvert\psi_A(n)\psi_B(n)\rvert$, which decays as $e^{-cV}$ for order-distinct states. The T1 diagnostic measured $B\approx0.20$ and a paired-over-independent variance ratio $R_{\mathrm{pair}}\approx0.64$ at 12 sites (a factor 1.6). Correlated sampling is textbook (Umrigar's review; spotlight sampling, arXiv:2507.18930).
* **Why this kills it**: With disjoint supports the paired estimator reduces to independent sampling, so the 1.6 factor is a finite-size bonus.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: paired estimators between states of the same order (for example along a warm ladder).
* **Strongest case against the kill**: Sampling is not the binding constraint at the Hard-Core scale anyway ($n\approx8\sigma^2/\delta^2$ falls as $1/V$). Answer: that makes the route unnecessary rather than wrong; not raised by a critic.
* **Checked by**: leaf-summary critic (2026-11-16).
* **Lesson**: L7 (`library.md`)

### R7.5 — Small-cluster bias calibration (reopening candidate C5) (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Measure the per-site differential bias $\Delta b/V$ of a fixed ansatz on ED-sized clusters where the truth is known, extrapolate it in $V$, and subtract it at the target size, so the decision at large size is bias-corrected, like an information criterion corrects in-sample optimism.
* **Lineage**: Operation B.3, cross-field translation from calibration against known standards and bias-corrected model selection (2026-11-16).
* **Source analogy**: In statistics, Akaike's criterion (IEEE Trans. Autom. Control 19, 716, 1974) subtracts the analytic optimism of an in-sample fit, and instrument calibration subtracts a bias measured on known standards. Correspondence: optimism or instrument bias ↔ $\Delta b$; standards ↔ ED clusters. It works because the bias has a known form or the standards resemble the samples. Gap: $\Delta b$ has no known form, and ED clusters do not host the competitors of interest.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G3 (2026-11-16), also failing G4.
* **Kill rule**: Gate 3, "does it work only by removing the Hard Core?"
* **Evidence**: The competitors that matter (period-8 against period-4 stripes, or stripe against $d$-wave) are not hosted on ED-sized clusters (Hubbard ED stops near 18 sites, arXiv:1808.06327), and boundary pinning changes $\Delta b$ with geometry. * **Why this kills it**: It calibrates the bias of different competitors from those it must correct.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: calibration on larger reference data (DMRG on cylinders) where the same competitors exist.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: DMRG references on width-4 to width-6 cylinders do host stripes and could serve as standards. Answer: then the calibration cost is a DMRG study of each competitor, outside "constant-factor cost"; not raised by a critic.

## Leaf Status Summary
* **State**: DORMANT
* **Updated**: 2026-11-17, documentation pass (Analogy Map, Route Register with 33 cards, reopening ledger); no new result since the reopening review's T1 scope check of 2026-11-16 (`tests/P3.2_reopen_review/aggregate_output.txt`, verdict SCOPED).
* **Conclusion**: On one 12-site Hubbard torus with an exact same-symmetry level crossing, no rule built from trained branch energies or from Ritz rotations of trained states beats the variational bias of the branches: the decision is either easy (enough capacity) or undecidable from energies, and the sound rule is a plain final-energy comparison that abstains within its sampling error. One exact Lanczos step fixes most wrong calls, but sampled at $2\times10^4$ draws per branch it abstains almost everywhere below the Hard-Core gap scale. The leaf is not closed, because the case that matters (a bias pointing against the truth at the Hard-Core gap scale, on trained and saved states) has not been measured.
* **Scope**: $4\times3$ periodic Hubbard torus, $N_\uparrow=N_\downarrow=4$ (doping $1/3$), $U=8t$, $t_y=1.11394t$, $t'$ swept through one same-irrep conical intersection between a period-4 spin stripe and a near-Néel state (not stripe against $d$-wave); space-group-projected NNBF at widths 4 and 8 (676 and 1160 parameters against a projected sector dimension of 1618, so width 8 is in the sector-fitting regime, where the network can nearly fit the sector freely); exact sums for training; sampled decisions with i.i.d. draws only.
* **Compute spent**: 1,564 CPU-h in three Slurm jobs (410917: 67; 451852: 1,412; 619080: 84; master Compute Ledger), plus about 7 CPU-minutes of interactive T1 runs and unledgered ED scans and smoke runs.

**The question.** Neural quantum states (NQS) are neural-network wave functions trained by variational Monte Carlo (VMC). In the doped Hubbard model, competing orders (stripes, $d$-wave, antiferromagnetic states) lie within $\lvert\delta\rvert/V\sim10^{-3}t$ per site of each other ($V$ sites, hopping $t$), below typical variational errors; this is the *Hard-Core scale*. Practice trains two runs, each seeded toward one order, and compares energies. Each run $X$ carries a bias $b_X\ge0$, so the comparison decides by the sign of $\delta+\Delta b$, with $\delta=E_B-E_A$ the true gap and $\Delta b=b_B-b_A$. The leaf asks whether such a biased pair can become a decision rule that is sound, gains power as variance shrinks, costs a constant factor, and does not depend on the seeds.

**The test bed.** Competitors with different quantum numbers are separated trivially by symmetry projection (R1.2), so a two-parameter scan found an exact same-irrep conical intersection at $t'_c=-0.470482$ (Round 3). With $d=t'-t'_c$, the ground state is a spin stripe ($B$) for $d<0$ and near-Néel ($A$) for $d>0$, told apart by the sign of an order character $c$; the in-irrep gap is $1.4\times10^{-3}t$ at $d=\pm0.0002$ and $0.025t$ at $\pm0.005$. Truth is exact diagonalisation (ED). Branches are projected neural-network backflow (NNBF) states trained by stochastic reconfiguration (SR).

**The chain of evidence.**
1. *Transporting easy states fails.* Same-order eigenvector continuation from snapshots far from the crossing was wrong on 5 of 15 width-4 pairs, always toward $A$, while oracle continuation from exact snapshots was always right (job 451852; R3.5): the snapshots' bias passes through.
2. *Capacity decides the easy case.* The lower final energy wins on all 6 targets for all 3 seeds at width 8, but is wrong on 2 of 3 seeds at width 4 (same seeds, steps and warm starts).
3. *The $2\times2$ lemma.* Near the crossing every energy-based rule has its boundary at $\delta+\Delta b=0$, and the lowest eigenvector of a real symmetric $2\times2$ matrix always lies mostly on the lower diagonal, so Ritz rules in trained states return the lower trained energy. Off-diagonal information is bounded by the coupling $W\approx5\times10^{-4}t$, against $\Delta b=3.6$–$5.4\times10^{-3}t$ on two width-4 seeds.
4. *It holds on real states.* In job 619080 the excited-state Ritz decision equalled the lower-diagonal prediction on 9 of 9 instances (right on 4); the naive rule with final-state sampling error $\sigma_{\mathrm{MC}}$ never committed wrong (2 commits, 7 abstentions); an exact symmetric block-Lanczos step was right on 9 of 9 in-sample.
5. *Sampling removes the Lanczos gain below the Hard-Core scale.* On saved snapshot states with biases $0.5$–$1.6\times10^{-2}t$, the exact energy rule is wrong on 6 of 9 and an exact Lanczos step fixes 4; sampled at $2\times10^4$ draws it commits right on 1.3% and wrong on 0.6% of replicates, because the differences it must resolve ($\le1.7\times10^{-3}t$) are below its $3.0\times10^{-3}t$ standard error (R6.5).
6. *At the Hard-Core scale sampling is not the limit.* At $d=\pm0.005$ sampled plain energies commit right on 100% of replicates and never wrong, but the bias there pointed toward the truth, so only the sampling limit is scoped.

**Why the alternatives fail.** Zero-variance extrapolation of independent branches is practice and echoes the seed (R1.1, R3.4); gap-based certificates are powerless where gaps close (R1.3, R3.3, R5.1, R5.2); cross-order overlaps cost $e^{\mathcal O(V)}$ samples (R1.4a, R7.2); joint and shared-backflow ensembles are existing ansätze with heavy-tailed cross terms (R1.4, R2.1, R2.2); field, hysteresis and stability probes see only biased energies (R2.3, R3.1, R7.4); the crossing certificate's margin was anti-conservative (R5.3); Ritz rotations follow the lower diagonal (R6.1–R6.3); Lanczos is not size consistent and imaginary-time projection has the sign problem (R7.1, R7.3); small-cluster calibration lacks the competitors (R7.5).

**What this leaf taught beyond itself** (entries of `library.md`):
