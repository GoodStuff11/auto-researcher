# Subproblem: P3.2 Bias-diagnosing biased-ensemble tests
**Parent Chain**: P3 -> P3.2  **Status**: Completed (Round 4 fix, 2026-11-12; corrected decisive array test-ready, not submitted)
### 1. Mathematical Abstraction & Barriers
* **Formulation**: Let $\hat H$ be the doped Hubbard Hamiltonian of the Invariants on a finite cluster, and let $\{\hat O_X\}_{X\in\{A,B\}}$ be order observables (e.g. the stripe or $d$-wave correlation $C(r)$ at the longest distance) that separate two competing orders. Define the competitor energies $E_X^\star=\min\{\langle\psi\rvert\hat H\lvert\psi\rangle:\ \psi\ \text{has order character}\ X\}$; on a finite cluster these are sharp only when the characters are separated by a quantum number, otherwise they are the eigenvalues of the lowest two eigenstates whose characters differ. The gap is $\delta=E_B^\star-E_A^\star$, with $\lvert\delta\rvert/V$ of order $10^{-3}t$ or smaller ($V$ is the number of sites).
  *Input*: two families of variational states $\{\psi_X^{(p)}\}_{p=0}^{P}$ (accuracy level $p$, e.g. NQS size or number of Lanczos steps), each initialised or constrained toward order $X$, with Monte Carlo estimates of the energy $E_X^{(p)}=\langle\hat H\rangle$, the variance $\sigma^{2\,(p)}_X=\langle\hat H^2\rangle-\langle\hat H\rangle^2$ and the order character $c_X^{(p)}=\langle\hat O\rangle$.
  *Output*: a decision $D\in\{A,B,\text{undecided}\}$ together with a confidence level.
  *Solved means*: (i) **soundness**: $\Pr[D\neq\text{undecided}\ \wedge\ D\neq\mathrm{sgn}\text{-truth}]$ is below a stated level (the error bars are calibrated, i.e. the intervals around the extrapolated intercepts cover $E_X^\star$ at the nominal rate); (ii) **power**: $D$ is not undecided whenever $\lvert\delta\rvert$ exceeds a resolution that shrinks as $\sigma^2\to0$; (iii) the cost is the cost of the two runs times a constant (no massive compute); (iv) the result does not depend on which mean-field reference seeded which branch (Unacceptable Outcome, first bullet).
* **The Node Barrier**: The standard implementation (zero-variance extrapolation of each branch, then compare intercepts) is already the method of choice: Imada group VMC stripe vs. $d$-wave studies (Ido, Ohgoe & Imada, arXiv:1712.05490; Darmawan et al., PRB 98, 205132, 2018, arXiv:1808.06327), the stripe benchmark (Zheng et al., Science 358, 1155, 2017), and the latest NQS paper on intertwined orders (Viteritti et al., arXiv:2604.21978, zero-variance fit of the Pfaffian at each accuracy level). It halts against the Hard Core at four specific points:
  1. *Variance-invisible contamination (fundamental)*. Write a branch as $\psi=\sqrt{1-\epsilon}\,\lvert X^\star\rangle+\sqrt{\epsilon_Y}\,\lvert Y^\star\rangle+\sqrt{\epsilon_r}\,\lvert r\rangle$, with $\lvert Y^\star\rangle$ the competitor and $\lvert r\rangle$ the high-energy residual. Then to leading order $E-E_X^\star\approx\epsilon_Y\delta+\epsilon_r(\bar E_r-E_X^\star)$ while $\sigma^2\approx\epsilon_r\langle(\hat H-E_X^\star)^2\rangle_r+\mathcal O(\epsilon_Y\delta^2)$. The admixture $\epsilon_Y$ moves the energy by up to $\delta$ and leaves no trace in $\sigma^2$, so no extrapolation in $\sigma^2$ can remove it. When $\epsilon_Y>1/2$ the branch has *crossed over*, both intercepts converge to the same eigenvalue, and the initial label is wrong.
  2. *Nonlinearity and unequal extrapolation lengths (practical)*. Linearity $E\approx E^\star+a\sigma^2$ holds only once $\epsilon_r\ll1$. NQS branches typically sit at $\sigma^2/V\sim10^{-2}t^2$, and the two branches have different slopes $a_X$ because their residuals differ. The fit-form systematic (linear versus quadratic intercept) is then comparable to $\delta$.
  3. *Boundary and finite-size pinning (practical)*. On cylinders, open edges pin one order (review arXiv:2608.21291 §3.2, Gu et al. arXiv:2507.02644), so $\delta$ itself changes sign with geometry.
  4. *Differential expressivity (fundamental)*. If the ansatz represents order $A$ worse than order $B$, both branches carry a bias that differs by more than $\delta$. Only the $\sigma^2$-correlated part of that bias is extrapolated away.
* **Anti-Solution Space**: (a) comparing raw variational energies (that is the problem itself); (b) quoting the linear-fit standard error alone as the error bar (it ignores fit-form systematics and contamination); (c) declaring a winner from the order character of a *single* run seeded by one reference (the Unacceptable Outcome); (d) rigorous eigenvalue inclusion bounds at finite energy density (see Route 3); (e) any route that needs $\lvert\langle\psi_A\vert\psi_B\rangle\rvert$ estimated by ratio sampling (exponentially small for macroscopically distinct orders); (f) larger networks or larger sample counts as the fix (massive compute).
### 2. Candidate Routes & Falsification Gates
* **Route 1**: *Calibrated independent-branch extrapolation*. Formalize current practice: per-branch linear $E$-vs-$\sigma^2$ fit, error bar $=\sqrt{\mathrm{SE}_{\mathrm{lin}}^2+(I_{\mathrm{lin}}-I_{\mathrm{quad}})^2}$ (here $\mathrm{SE}_{\mathrm{lin}}$ is the standard error of the linear-fit intercept and $I_{\mathrm{lin}},I_{\mathrm{quad}}$ are the linear and quadratic intercepts), bootstrap over fit windows, decide when the intercept difference exceeds $z$ times the combined error ($z$ is the confidence multiplier).
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): FAIL. This is exactly what Ido, Ohgoe & Imada (arXiv:1712.05490), Darmawan et al. (arXiv:1808.06327) and Viteritti et al. (arXiv:2604.21978) already do, and Sorella's VMC+Lanczos extrapolation (PRB 64, 024512, 2001) underlies it. It has not settled the NQS disagreement (Gu et al. arXiv:2507.02644 versus Roth et al. arXiv:2511.07566) because of barriers 1 and 2. A better error model does not touch the variance-invisible $\epsilon_Y\delta$ term or crossover. Relabelling without a fix.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Consistent as $\sigma^2\to0$.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): FAIL. Branch labels come from the seeding reference. For Minimal-Contrast-Foil branches (Jastrow–Slater with stripe versus uniform BCS references) that never leave their basin, the rule reports the reference.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. No gap or filling assumption.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
* **Route 2**: *Sector-resolved branches*. Bias each branch by a quantum number instead of an initialization (momentum, point-group parity, total spin $S$), project exactly (cost $\propto\lvert G\rvert$ for symmetry group $G$), add $p$ Lanczos steps inside the sector, and detect the level crossing between sector energies.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS for novelty as applied to fermionic stripe decisions, but the machinery is standard. Choo et al., PRL 121, 167204 (2018) handle symmetry-resolved NQS excited states; Hu, Becca, Parola & Sorella, PRB 88, 060402 (2013) apply sector VMC+Lanczos to $J_1$–$J_2$.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Sectors are exact, and branches cannot cross over.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. Sector energies do not depend on the reference.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): FAIL. The competitors of interest (filled $\lambda=8$ versus half-filled $\lambda=4$ stripe, with or without $d$-wave) are symmetry-broken states whose translation- and point-group-symmetrised versions land in the *same* sector on any cluster commensurate with both. The route only works when competitors carry different quantum numbers, which removes the same-sector near-degeneracy half of the Hard Core.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
* **Route 3**: *Rigorous eigenvalue brackets*. Pair each branch's variational upper bound with a Temple lower bound $E_0\ge E-\sigma^2/(\beta-E)$, where $\beta$ is a lower bound on the first excited energy (Temple, Proc. R. Soc. A 119, 276, 1928, doi:10.1098/rspa.1928.0098), or with Weinstein's inclusion $[E-\sigma,E+\sigma]$. Decide when the brackets separate.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS. These bounds are classical but unused for NQS order decisions.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): FAIL. Temple needs $\beta$ with $E<\beta\le E_1$, but $E_1-E_0\to0$ with $V$ in the doped (gapless or nearly degenerate) regime, so the bound degenerates. Weinstein's window has width $2\sigma\propto\sqrt V$ at fixed variance per site, and at finite energy density it contains exponentially many eigenvalues, so it never identifies *which* state the branch approximates.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): not evaluated (killed).
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): FAIL. The route relies on a large gap.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
* **Route 4**: *Jointly optimized orthogonal two-state ensemble with character-labelled Ritz extrapolation*. Replace the two independent branches by one two-state variational object, $\Psi(\mathbf n_1,\mathbf n_2)=\det\begin{pmatrix}\psi_1(\mathbf n_1)&\psi_1(\mathbf n_2)\\\psi_2(\mathbf n_1)&\psi_2(\mathbf n_2)\end{pmatrix}$, as in natural-excited-state VMC (Pfau et al., Science 385, eadn0137, 2024, arXiv:2308.16848; lattice variant: Ma et al., arXiv:2506.08594). Initialise $\psi_1$ toward order $A$ and $\psi_2$ toward order $B$, and minimise the ensemble energy $\mathrm{tr}\,(S^{-1}H)$. Here $S_{ij}=\langle\psi_i\vert\psi_j\rangle$ and $H_{ij}=\langle\psi_i\rvert\hat H\lvert\psi_j\rangle$. By the Gross–Oliveira–Kohn ensemble principle (PRA 37, 2805, 1988) the trace is an upper bound to $E_0+E_1$. Joint sampling from $\lvert\Psi\rvert^2$ gives an estimator of the matrix $S^{-1}H$ whose variance does not blow up when the two states are nearly orthogonal. This is the concrete fix for the exponential cost of independent-branch cross estimators. At every accuracy level, diagonalise $S^{-1}H$ to get Ritz roots $\chi_0,\chi_1$ with energies $\theta_0\le\theta_1$. Measure each root's variance and order character $c(\chi_i)=\langle\hat O\rangle_{\chi_i}$. Extrapolate each root along its own $(\sigma^2,\theta)$ path, label the roots by *measured* character, and decide using the character-labelled intercepts. If both roots carry the same pure character, decide that character: the competitor then lies above the two lowest states.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS. Ensemble or NES excited-state methods exist (Pfau et al. 2024; Ma et al. 2025), and per-branch variance extrapolation exists (Route 1). A WebSearch found no use of a jointly optimized ensemble as a *ground-state order decision rule* for doped Hubbard NQS. The adoption barrier of Route 1 is crossover and seed-label dependence (barrier 1). The mechanism here fixes exactly that: the pair spans a subspace, the labels come from Ritz diagonalisation plus measured character rather than from the seed, and a branch that drifts toward the other order is absorbed by the $2\times2$ diagonalisation instead of corrupting a label.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. $N_e$, $S_z$ and lattice symmetries are untouched. As $\sigma^2\to0$ the Ritz roots become the two lowest eigenstates (Hylleraas–Undheim–MacDonald interlacing). The cost is about $2\times$ a single run plus $2\times2$ algebra per sample. The naive non-orthogonal-CI alternative, which builds $\langle\psi_A\rvert\hat H\lvert\psi_B\rangle$ by ratio sampling from $\lvert\psi_A\rvert^2$, has variance $\propto\lvert\langle\psi_A\vert\psi_B\rangle\rvert^{-2}=e^{\mathcal O(V)}$ and is excluded. The joint-sampling estimator avoids this.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. The decision is invariant under swapping which reference seeded which state, because it depends only on the spanned subspace and measured characters. A foil pair stuck in its references still gives an answer, but that answer comes from the measured energies of both orders inside one variational object, not from the seed. The test's R0 versus R0+ versus R4 ablation quantifies exactly this.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. No gap is assumed for the *decision* (the lowest root's character). The gap above the two-state manifold enters only the error bar, through linearity of the Ritz extrapolation. The route works at any doping and $t'$, and the test runs doped with $t'\neq0$.
  - *Verdict*: SURVIVED
### 3. Top Surviving Route & Sharpest Gap
* **Mechanism**: Route 4. Two biased branches become one jointly optimized, orthogonality-free two-state ensemble (determinant of states). Branch labels are reassigned after Ritz diagonalisation by the measured order character, and each Ritz root is zero-variance-extrapolated along its own path with a fit-form systematic in the error bar. This removes crossover and seed-label dependence, which are the two failure modes that keep independent-branch extrapolation (current practice) from settling NQS stripe disputes. Its cost is a constant factor (about $2\times$).
* **Sharpest Gap**: It is unverified that the Ritz roots of an imperfect two-state subspace have *pure* order character, and that their $\theta$-vs-$\sigma^2$ paths are linear enough for calibrated error bars, near a crossing where $\lvert\delta\rvert$ is far below the variational error. The smoke test already shows that with poor references (initial energy error of about 40%) the joint roots can be strongly mixed ($\lvert\langle\hat R\rangle\rvert$ below $0.06$ at one control value), which makes the rule abstain. Whether the joint step beats simply relabelling independent branches by measured character (ablation R0+) is the open question the test decides. A second, unproven assumption is that the NES $S^{-1}H$ estimator keeps bounded variance for *lattice-fermion* states whose orders differ macroscopically. It is demonstrated for molecules (Pfau et al.) and spins/ions (Ma et al.) only.

### 5. Local Autopsy Log
* **Route 1 (calibrated independent-branch extrapolation)**: Gate 0 and Gate 2 failure. It is current practice (arXiv:1712.05490, arXiv:1808.06327, arXiv:2604.21978) and leaves the variance-invisible competitor admixture $\epsilon_Y\delta$ and the seed-label crossover untouched, so for foil branches it echoes the seeding reference.
* **Route 2 (sector-resolved branches)**: Gate 3 failure. The stripe competitors that matter ($\lambda=8$ versus $\lambda=4$, with or without $d$-wave) share lattice-symmetry sectors after symmetrisation, so sector resolution only handles competitors that already differ by a quantum number.
* **Route 3 (Temple/Weinstein brackets)**: Gate 1 and Gate 3 failure. Temple's bound needs a lower bound on $E_1$ that collapses as the gap closes with $V$. Weinstein's $\pm\sigma$ window, which grows as $\sqrt V$ at fixed variance per site, contains exponentially many eigenvalues at finite energy density and cannot identify the competitor.
* **Sub-variant inside Route 4 (non-orthogonal CI of independently optimised branches)**: Gate 1 failure. Ratio-sampled cross terms $\langle\psi_A\rvert\hat H\lvert\psi_B\rangle/\langle\psi_A\vert\psi_A\rangle$ have variance $\propto\lvert\langle\psi_A\vert\psi_B\rangle\rvert^{-2}=e^{\mathcal O(V)}$ for macroscopically distinct orders, which is an exponential cost. Only the joint-sampling (determinant-of-states) form survives.

  - **Route 2.1 (Mutated from the killed NOCI sub-variant via Operation A.2, and from Route 4 via Operation A.1): State-specific two-reference shared-backflow superposition (2R-SS).** One wave function
$$\psi_\theta(\mathbf n)=\sum_{X\in\{A,B\}}C_X\,\det\!\big[\Phi^{\uparrow}_X+F^{\uparrow}_\theta(\mathbf n)\big]_{\mathrm{occ}}\det\!\big[\Phi^{\downarrow}_X+F^{\downarrow}_\theta(\mathbf n)\big]_{\mathrm{occ}}.$$
    Here $\Phi^\sigma_X$ are the trainable reference orbitals of order $X$ (Néel UHF for $A$, stripe UHF for $B$), $F_\theta$ is **one** backflow MLP shared by both references, $[\cdot]_{\mathrm{occ}}$ selects the occupied rows, and $(C_A,C_B)$ is the exact lowest Ritz vector of the $2\times2$ problem in $\mathrm{span}\{a_A,a_B\}$, where $a_X$ is the $X$ term with $C_X=1$. SR trains $\theta$ and both $\Phi_X$ on the lowest Ritz root $\theta_0$. Decision: the sign of $c(\psi_\theta)$, abstaining if $\lvert c\rvert<\tau$, with $\tau$ the abstention threshold.
    - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS.
        - Prior art: superposing order-distinct non-orthogonal determinants is resonating Hartree–Fock (Fukutome 1988; Tomita, PRB 2004; applied to stripes in the 2D Hubbard model by Tomita & Watanabe, PRL 2009) and symmetry-projected NOCI (Rodríguez-Guzmán, Jiménez-Hoyos & Scuseria, PRB 90, 195110, 2014). Multi-determinant NNBF exists for energy (Loehr & Clark, arXiv:2510.26906; arXiv:2609.26544). There, the extra determinants come from one network via Lanczos steps or symmetry projection, so they inherit one reference.
        - Adoption barrier: ResHF/NOCI lacks dynamic correlation, so the differential correlation energy (larger than $\delta$) decides. Independent NNBF branches have differential expressivity (round-1 barrier 4). Multi-determinant NNBF is used for energy, not for the decision, and is seeded from one reference.
        - Mechanism that fixes it: one backflow dresses both order references, so the dynamic-correlation dressing is common-mode. The variational family is invariant under exchanging the two references, so the answer cannot depend on which reference seeded which branch (round-1 solved-criterion (iv)) by construction.
        - The state is sampled directly from $\lvert\psi_\theta\rvert^2$, so no cross-overlap ratio $\langle\psi_A\vert\psi_B\rangle$ is ever estimated. That removes the exponential-variance failure of the killed NOCI sub-variant.
        - WebSearch (standard mode, four queries) found no shared-backflow superposition of competing-order references, and no use of such a superposition as an order-decision rule.
    - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. $N_\uparrow,N_\downarrow$ are exact. The cost is two determinants per configuration plus one MLP, which is a constant factor. The exact limit is reached whenever either term is exact. The objective is a Rayleigh quotient, so the energy is variational. The analytic gradient matches finite differences to 9 digits (smoke).
    - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. The control is R0+ (two independent NNBF branches, lower energy wins, labelled by measured $c$) at matched wall-clock budget, with 1.5 times the steps per branch. A seed-reporting rule fails on the $t'\ge-0.31$ points, because the stripe reference is lower in mean field there while the truth is $A$. The test can fail: FAIL if 2R-SS does not beat R0+ in wrong-commit rate or in $\lvert c-c(E_0)\rvert$.
    - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. Both competitors share all quantum numbers ($K=0$, $M_x=M_y=+1$, $S=0$; checked by sector-projected ED), the eigenstates are mixed at the crossing, and the instance is doped (1/3) with $t'\neq0$ in 2D (non-stoquastic). The superposition can represent a mixed ground state, which two independent pure branches cannot.
    - *Verdict*: SURVIVED.
  - **Route 2.2 (Operation B.3, cross-field translation from state-averaged CASSCF / state-averaged QMC and property-based diabatisation): State-averaged two-reference shared backflow (2R-SA) with order-operator diabatisation.** Same family as Route 2.1. It is trained on the Gross–Oliveira–Kohn ensemble energy $\mathrm{tr}(S^{-1}H)=\theta_0+\theta_1$ over the 2-dimensional span, as in state-averaged QMC, where states share the Jastrow and orbitals and only CI coefficients are state-specific (Filippi, Zaccheddu & Buda, JCTC 2009; Shepard, Scemama, Moroni & Filippi, arXiv:2504.09259). The Ritz roots are diabatised by diagonalising the order operator inside the span, i.e. Boys-type property-based diabatisation (Subotnik, Yeganeh, Cave & Ratner, JCP 129, 244101, 2008) with the order operator $\hat c$ in place of the dipole. This gives diabatic energies $\varepsilon_A,\varepsilon_B$ and coupling $W$. Decision: the sign of $c$ of the lowest root. $\varepsilon_B-\varepsilon_A$ and $W$ are recorded for the crossing analysis.
    - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS.
        - Prior art: state averaging and Boys diabatisation are standard in quantum chemistry, and NES (Pfau et al.) is the NQS ensemble method.
        - Adoption barrier: in quantum chemistry, sharing parameters "compromises the flexibility" of each state (Shepard et al. 2022, arXiv:2207.12160), so state-specific optimization is preferred for spectra. NES uses separate parameters per state, which brings back differential expressivity.
        - Mechanism that fixes it: for a *decision*, that compromise is exactly what is wanted. The ensemble trains one backflow to serve both orders, so the bias is common-mode. Unlike Route 2.1, the objective cannot discard the higher root, because both roots enter the trace. That closes the collapse mode of Route 2.1, in which one determinant becomes a corrector of the other.
        - The round-1 critic's NES objection does not apply: R4 had two independent networks, while here the network is shared and only references and CI differ.
        - WebSearch found no state-averaged NQS on lattice fermions, and no property-diabatised NQS decision rule.
    - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. HUM interlacing: $\theta_0+\theta_1\ge E_0+E_1$. The cost is a constant factor. The analytic gradient matches finite differences (smoke).
    - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. Same control, budget and FAIL clause as Route 2.1. The SA arm uses the same matched budget.
    - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. Same same-sector instance. Diabatisation is applied to mixed adiabatic roots, which is the case that made round-1 R4 abstain.
    - *Verdict*: SURVIVED.
  - **Route 2.3 (Operation B.2, active probing): two-sided conjugate-field response.** Train each branch under $\hat H-h\hat c$ for $h$ of both signs and decide from where the variational lower envelope $\min_k[\langle\hat H\rangle_k-h\langle\hat c\rangle_k]$ kinks, i.e. the crossing field $h_c$. Killed at Gate 2. The envelope at $h=0$ is decided by the same biased energies: $h_c\approx\delta/\Delta c$ carries the differential bias divided by $\Delta c$, the difference in character between the branches. A basin-trapped ansatz answers either sign of the field with its own order (the P3.1 pinning-field kill), so the two-sided response is fakeable.
  - **Route 2.4 (Operation A.4, continuation in $h$): thermodynamic integration from a field-dominated anchor.** Obtain $E_X(0)=E(\pm h_{\max})+\int\langle\hat c\rangle_X\,dh$ along each diabat. Killed at Gate 1. It replaces energies, whose error is second order in the wave-function error, by integrals of observables, whose error is first order, so the error grows. The anchors are still variational.
  - **Route 2.5 (Operation B.3): Okamoto–Nomura level spectroscopy with twisted boundaries** (Okamoto & Nomura, Phys. Lett. A 169, 433, 1992; Nomura & Kitazawa, cond-mat/0201072). Killed at Gate 3. The method needs a twist or symmetry that assigns the two competitors different quantum numbers. On a periodic 2D cluster, a flux breaks a mirror but does not separate the stripe from the $d$-wave state, so the method reduces to the killed sector-resolved route.
  - **Route 2.6 (Operation B.3): Bayesian evidence comparison of the two branches.** Killed at Gate 1. A variational energy is not a likelihood. Any "evidence" built from $(E,\sigma^2)$ is the calibrated extrapolation of Route 1 under another name.
  - **Route 2.7 (Operation B.1): conical-intersection (Berry-phase) tracking in the $(t',h)$ plane.** Killed at Gate 2. The geometric phase locates the degeneracy seam, not the sign of the diabatic gap at the target point. A biased $2\times2$ model has the same topology, so the phase cannot diagnose bias.
  - **Route 2.8 (Operation B.1, duality frame): decide from the momentum-resolved structure factor of the winning branch.** Killed at Gate 0. Reading $S_s(q)$ of the lower-energy branch is R0+. The momentum-resolved $c$ is adopted as the labelling observable of all arms, not as a route.
### Top Surviving Route & Sharpest Gap (Round 2)
* **Mechanism**: Route 2.1 (2R-SS). Both competing-order references are dressed by **one** shared neural backflow inside a single superposition. The variational optimum then chooses the order mixture with a common-mode correlation dressing, cannot depend on which reference seeded what, and can represent the mixed ground state at a same-sector avoided crossing. Route 2.2 (2R-SA) is the state-averaged twin that cannot drop the second root.
* **Sharpest Gap**:
    * It is unproven that sharing the backflow makes the expressivity bias common-mode. The reference orbitals $\Phi_X$ remain separately trainable, and the network can specialise to one reference.
    * The state-specific objective can make the second determinant a mere corrector of the first. In the 400-step smoke run the second Ritz root drifted to $\theta_1=-5.6t$ against $\theta_0=-8.85t$. If that happens on the real instance, 2R-SS reduces to a single-reference branch with an extra determinant, i.e. to R0+ with one seed, and the reference trap decides again.
    * Route 2.2 removes this collapse mode but pays the state-averaging compromise. Which effect dominates is what the decisive test measures.

* **A structural lemma that drives the batch (2×2 effective model)**. Near the crossing, write the irrep problem as $\hat H_{\mathrm{eff}}=\begin{pmatrix}E_A&W\\W&E_B\end{pmatrix}$, where $E_A,E_B$ are the diabatic energies and $W$ their coupling, with $\delta=E_B-E_A$. A variational method sees diagonal energies $E_X+b_X$, where $b_X\ge0$ is the variational bias of order $X$, and a coupling $W'$. Every rule that orders the two states from variational energies alone therefore has its decision boundary at $\delta+\Delta b=0$, with $\Delta b=b_B-b_A$. This includes energy comparison, the kink of the lower envelope, the curvature peak, the fidelity-susceptibility peak, and the field-envelope kink. Two consequences follow:
    * The Hellmann–Feynman slopes add no information. For a variational optimum, $\mathrm{d}E_{\mathrm{var}}/\mathrm{d}t'=\langle\hat H_{t'}\rangle_{\mathrm{var}}$, the derivative of the same biased curve.
    * The only levers are a smaller differential bias (common-mode or extrapolated), or avoiding the place where the bias is worst: the hard point itself, where projected branches in one irrep can slide into each other's basin.

    * Arms: R0P (cold), R0P-warm, R0P-warm-abs, R0P-warm-sf; SO-EC with its sampled version; ECor, AT and ATor as diagnostics. 800 projected-SR steps per branch.
* **Decision rule**, implemented verbatim in `--aggregate`:
    * The control error $\varepsilon(t)$ is the median over seeds of the smallest $E-E_0$ among the four control branches (cold and warm, $A$ and $B$). A target is hard if $\varepsilon\ge\Delta_{\mathrm{in}}/2$ and $\lvert c(E_0)\rvert\ge0.02$.
    * A width is valid if it has at least 3 hard targets, at least 4 of 6 snapshots certified (Eckart and seed) in the median seed, and both order blocks non-empty in every seed. Otherwise it reports PREMISE-FAIL.
    * If all three exact-force controls have $\mathrm{CW}=0$ on the hard targets, the result is NO-ROOM and SO-EC FAILs.
    * Otherwise SO-EC is judged on the warm-crossover subset:
        * FAIL if the subset has fewer than 3 pairs, if $\mathrm{CW}(\mathrm{EC})>0.30$, or if any control dominates EC (CW no higher and DEC no lower);
        * PASS if $\mathrm{CW}\le0.15$, $\mathrm{DEC}\ge0.6$, EC beats every control (CW lower by at least $0.15$, or CW no higher with DEC higher by at least $0.2$), the sampled-EC flip rate is at most $0.10$, and the sampled-EC CW is at most $0.15$;
        * AMBIGUOUS otherwise, with a 6-seed rerun pre-committed; if it is still AMBIGUOUS, SO-EC is killed.
    * Global: PASS at one or more valid widths and FAIL at none.
    * The regime counts (correct, noise, intrinsic, crossover) for the cold and warm controls are printed at every width, valid or not. They are the main control-side result: whether R0P's wrong call was noise, intrinsic bias, or crossover.
* **Cost.** Per task: 40 trainings (12 at snapshots, 24 at targets, 4 sampled-force) of 800 steps each.
    * At 1.54 s per step (measured in the pilot, width 8, 16 threads) this is about 14 h, plus less than 0.5 h for ED, global checks and sampling.
    * At the worst measured rate (2.2 s per step on 8 threads) it is 19.6 h, inside the 24 h limit.
    * The whole array is about 90 task-hours on 16 CPUs (about 1400 core-hours).
