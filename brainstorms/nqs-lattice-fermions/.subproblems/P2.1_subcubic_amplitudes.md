# Subproblem: P2.1 Sub-cubic antisymmetric amplitudes and fast local updates
**Parent Chain**: P2 -> P2.1  **Status**: Completed (Round 3): Route 3.1 (SK-LR) and Route 3.2 (SK-AGP) survive all gates; decisive test built and smoke-tested, not submitted
### 1. Mathematical Abstraction & Barriers
* **Formulation**: Inputs: a configuration $\mathbf{n}\in\{0,1\}^{2V}$ with $N_e$ fermions on $V$ sites ($2V$ spin-orbitals $p=(\sigma,x)$), the set $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{n})=\{\mathbf{n}':H_{\mathbf{n}'\mathbf{n}}\neq0,\ \mathbf{n}'\neq\mathbf{n}\}$ of connected configurations (for the $t$–$t'$ Hubbard model each particle has $z=8$ neighbours, so $\lvert\mathcal{N}\rvert=\mathcal{O}(zN_e)$), and a backflow determinant $\psi(\mathbf{n})=\det A(\mathbf{n})$, where $A(\mathbf{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{N_e\times N_e}$ has one row per particle, produced by a network from $\mathbf{n}$. Outputs: the ratios $\rho(\mathbf{n}')=\psi(\mathbf{n}')/\psi(\mathbf{n})$ for all $\mathbf{n}'\in\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{n})$, hence the local energy $E_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{n})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}'}H_{\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}'}\rho(\mathbf{n}')$, and the Metropolis ratios. Four cost measures are kept separate: $C_{\mathrm{amp}}$ (one ratio, given data cached at $\mathbf{n}$), $C_E$ (one local energy), $C_{\mathrm{acc}}$ (one accepted Markov move), and $C_{\mathrm{sw}}$ (one sweep of $N_e$ proposals, the cost of one decorrelated sample). "Solved" means: ratios exact to round-off (no truncation bias) with $C_E=o(N_e^3)$ and $C_{\mathrm{sw}}=\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$, i.e. the asymptotic cost of the Minimal Contrast Foil, for a backflow family that is (a) not reducible to Slater–Jastrow, and (b) whose restriction relative to unrestricted neural-network backflow (NNBF) is stated and shown not to cost accuracy at the competing-order scale $10^{-3}t$ per site. Baselines: unrestricted NNBF (Luo & Clark, PRL 122, 226401) has $C_E=\mathcal{O}(zN_e^4)$ and $C_{\mathrm{sw}}=\mathcal{O}(N_e^4)$.
* **The Node Barrier**:
    * **Fundamental F1 (matrix-output bound; kills "without changing the family" for unrestricted backflow).** If $\Delta(\mathbf{n}')=A(\mathbf{n}')-A(\mathbf{n})$ is generically full rank, as in Luo–Clark NNBF whose last layer emits the whole $2V\times N_e$ orbital matrix, then writing $A(\mathbf{n}')$ alone costs $\Omega(N_e^2)$ per $\mathbf{n}'$, so $C_E=\Omega(zN_e^3)$ before any determinant is taken, and the determinant itself costs matrix-multiplication time per $\mathbf{n}'$. Therefore $C_E=o(N_e^3)$ is impossible for the unrestricted family. Every exact sub-cubic route must use a family with $\operatorname{rank}\Delta(\mathbf{n}')\le m\ll N_e$; then the matrix determinant lemma gives $\rho=\det(I_m+V^{\top}A^{-1}U)$ for $\Delta=UV^{\top}$ with $U,V\in\mathbb{R}^{N_e\times m}$. The honest version of the node is: find a rank-structured family that loses no accuracy.
    * **Fundamental F2 (sweep floor).** Every accepted move changes at least one row, so a carried inverse (or projected Green's function) needs an $\Omega(N_e^2)$ update, and a sweep of $\Theta(N_e)$ moves costs $\Omega(N_e^3)$. This holds even for the Foil. Delayed updates (Chen & Roth, arXiv:2602.05255) change constants, not this exponent. Per decorrelated sample, $\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$ is therefore the target; $o(N_e^3)$ per sample is out of reach of MCMC with carried inverses.
    * **Practical P1 (locality costs accuracy).** Finite-range convolutional backflow (SCALE, arXiv:2604.25775, PRX Intelligence 1, 023001) changes $\mathcal{O}(r^2)$ rows per hop and reaches $C_{\mathrm{sw}}=\mathcal{O}(N^3)$, but at $16\times16$, $1/8$ doping it sits about $3$–$4\times10^{-3}$ per site above transformer backflow and the deep ACE ansatz of the same paper. That gap exceeds the competing-order scale of the Hard Core.
    * **Practical P2 (depth undoes locality).** In a deep convolutional net (ACE, 16 blocks) a single hop changes features on every site, so the low-rank structure and the fast update disappear.
    * **Practical P3 (attention forward cost).** For transformer backflow (Gu et al., arXiv:2507.02644: embedding $d=256$, 4 layers, $K=4$ determinants), each $\mathbf{n}'$ needs a forward pass of cost $\Theta(V^2d)$, so $C_E=\Theta(N_e^3d)$ whatever determinant algebra is used. Its readout bound $\operatorname{rank}\Delta\le d$ is also vacuous, since $d\ge N_e=224$.
    * **Existing approaches, where each halts**: Lrux (arXiv:2602.05255) needs a backflow that "admits a low-rank representation" and provides none. SCALE is local and has the accuracy gap of P1. ACE is effectively global, P2. Liu & Clark (arXiv:2311.09450, PRB 110, 115124) show that hidden-fermion determinant states (HFDS) with $r$ hidden fermions equal NNBF with a rank-$r$ additive orbital correction, and that pure low-rank corrections are less expressive than full NNBF. Huang, Ou & Ozolins (arXiv:2609.26544) use low-rank parameter shifts inside a multi-determinant expansion, but every amplitude still needs an $\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$ LU and nothing is carried across Markov steps.
    * **Anti-Solution Space**: (i) finite-range backflow presented as "the same family"; (ii) speedups that hold only at $K=0$ (purely local) or only at infinite range; (iii) truncated-rank or perturbative ratios, which bias $E_{\mathrm{loc}}$ deterministically; (iv) $C_{\mathrm{amp}}$ claims that ignore the network forward pass per $\mathbf{n}'$; (v) claims of $C_{\mathrm{sw}}=o(N_e^3)$; (vi) exponents fitted where interpreter overhead dominates rather than arithmetic; (vii) benchmarks at half filling with $t'=0$, where $z=4$ and nodes are simpler.
### 2. Candidate Routes & Falsification Gates
* **Route 1**: Finite-range (one-layer convolutional) backflow, with a carried inverse and Woodbury updates of rank $m=\mathcal{O}(r^2)$. Point of departure: SCALE.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): FAIL. This is SCALE (arXiv:2604.25775) almost exactly; Lrux (arXiv:2602.05255) supplies the update kernels. Finite-range backflow is not the accurate standard because its $16\times16$ energies trail ACE and transformer backflow by about $3$–$4\times10^{-3}$ per site. The route has no mechanism that closes that gap.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Exact and number-conserving; $C_E=\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$, $C_{\mathrm{sw}}=\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. Published SCALE energies are well below Jastrow–Slater VMC.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. Locality does not need half filling or 1D.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
* **Route 2**: Readout-rank exact ratios for token-wise global backflow. If row $j$ is $a_j=\phi_0(p_j)+t_{x_j}(\mathbf{n})W_{\mathrm{out}}$ with token embedding $t_x\in\mathbb{R}^d$, then $\operatorname{rank}\Delta\le d+1$ for any global network, and with $W_{\mathrm{out}}A^{-1}$ cached, $C_{\mathrm{amp}}=\mathcal{O}(N_ed^2)$ exactly, with no change to the transformer-backflow family.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS. Lrux states the general low-rank condition, but no paper applies the readout-width bound to transformer backflow. It is unused because $d$ is not small compared with $N_e$ in practice.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): FAIL. Each $\mathbf{n}'$ needs a full attention forward pass of cost $\Theta(V^2d)$, because one changed token changes every token after layer 1. So $C_E=\Theta(zN_e\cdot V^2d)=\Theta(N_e^3d)$, which is no better than the cubic barrier. In published runs the rank bound is also vacuous ($d=256\ge N_e=224$, arXiv:2507.02644).
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS (moot).
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS (moot).
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
* **Route 3 (Local + Global-Bottleneck backflow, LGB-BF)**: The row of particle $j$ at spin-orbital $p_j=(\sigma_j,x_j)$ is
$$a_j(\mathbf{n})=\Phi_0[p_j]+h_{x_j}(\mathbf{n})\,\Theta_{\sigma_j}+\big(u_{x_j}(\mathbf{n})\odot c(\mathbf{n})\big)\,\Omega_{\sigma_j}.$$
Here $h_x=\tanh(W_1\,\mathrm{patch}_r(x)+b_1)\in\mathbb{R}^F$ are full-rank local features of range $r$ (one convolution); $c=\tanh\!\big(\sum_x P_x h_x\big)\in\mathbb{R}^K$ are $K$ global channels, each a position-dependent pooling of all local features; $u_x=W_uh_x\in\mathbb{R}^K$ are local gates; $\Phi_0$ are mean-field orbitals; and $\Theta_\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{F\times N_e}$, $\Omega_\sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times N_e}$. A hop fully changes the rows of the set $S$ of particles within range $r$ of the two touched sites ($\lvert S\rvert=\mathcal{O}(r^2)$), and changes every other row only through $\Delta c$, which is a rank-$\le2K$ change. So $A'=A+E_SD+U_gW$ with $m=\lvert S\rvert+2K$. Every inner product needed for the ratio comes from the carried projected Green's function $G=[\Phi_0;\Theta_\uparrow;\Theta_\downarrow;\Omega_\uparrow;\Omega_\downarrow]A^{-1}$, using $a_SA^{-1}=E_S^{\top}$. This gives $C_{\mathrm{amp}}=\mathcal{O}\big(N_e(\lvert S\rvert(F+K)+K^2)\big)$, $C_E=\mathcal{O}\big(zN_e^2(\lvert S\rvert(F+K)+K^2)\big)$, $C_{\mathrm{acc}}=\mathcal{O}\big((2V+2F+2K)N_e m\big)$ and $C_{\mathrm{sw}}=\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$ at fixed density. The improvement is in $C_E$, from $\mathcal{O}(N_e^4)$ to $\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$ at fixed $(r,F,K)$, and in $C_{\mathrm{sw}}$, from $\mathcal{O}(N_e^4)$ to the Foil's $\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$. Restriction, stated plainly: relative to unrestricted NNBF the family is restricted to full-rank finite-range backflow plus a rank-$K$ global part. It strictly contains SCALE ($K=0$), and a rank-$K$ hidden-fermion-type correction in the sense of Liu–Clark. It stays sub-cubic in $C_E$ while $K=o(N_e^{1/2})$.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS. The pieces exist separately: local backflow with Woodbury (arXiv:2604.25775), rank-$r$ global corrections and the HFDS equivalence (arXiv:2311.09450), and low-rank parameter shifts (arXiv:2609.26544). I found no work that combines a full-rank local backflow with an $\mathbf{n}$-dependent rank-$K$ global channel and an exact $C_E=\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$ carried-Green's-function algorithm. Each is not standard for a known reason: local backflow misses long-range correlations (P1), and pure low-rank corrections are weaker than NNBF (Liu–Clark). The route's concrete mechanism targets that barrier: the global channels carry long-range information (stripe phase, antiferromagnetic domain, hole arrangement) on top of a full-rank local backflow, at an additive cost of $\mathcal{O}(N_eK^2)$ per ratio. Whether this closes the gap is the Sharpest Gap below.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Exact (matrix determinant lemma plus Woodbury, no truncation); number-conserving hops only; spin and lattice symmetries are those of the network (translation-equivariant local part). No hardness claim is made: this is a cost result for a fixed family, not a convergence guarantee.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. The Foil already has $C_E=\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$, so cost alone is gameable. The route's claim is backflow expressivity at Foil cost, and its $K=0$ subfamily (SCALE) already beats Jastrow–Slater VMC in published energies. Exactness against brute-force determinants is also part of the metric, which the Foil cannot satisfy for this family.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. The test instance is doped ($\delta=1/8$), has $t'=-0.2$ ($z=8$, non-stoquastic) and is 2D periodic. Nothing uses particle–hole symmetry, 1D, or $U=0$.
  - *Verdict*: SURVIVED
* **Route 4**: Unbiased stochastic ratio estimator for unrestricted NNBF. $E_{\mathrm{loc}}$ is linear in $\rho$, so an unbiased estimator of $\det(I+A^{-1}\Delta)$ would keep $E$ and the gradient covariance unbiased. The estimator would use stochastic Chebyshev log-determinants (Han, Malioutov & Shin, arXiv:1503.06394) with a Russian-roulette exponential.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS. Stochastic log-determinants are standard, but I found no unbiased ratio estimator used for fermionic NQS local energies.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): FAIL. F1 still applies: forming $\Delta(\mathbf{n}')$ for unrestricted NNBF costs $\Omega(N_e^2)$ per $\mathbf{n}'$, so $C_E=\Omega(N_e^3)$. In addition, an unbiased estimate of $\exp(\operatorname{tr}\log(I+X))$ needs a signed random series whose variance grows like $\exp(\mathcal{O}(\lVert\log(I+X)\rVert))$. That blows up near nodes ($\rho\to0$) and at large $\rho$, exactly where $E_{\mathrm{loc}}$ variance is set, and destroys the zero-variance property.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS (moot).
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS (moot).
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
### 3. Top Surviving Route & Sharpest Gap
* **Mechanism**: LGB-BF (Route 3). The backflow change under a hop is forced to be (full-rank on $\mathcal{O}(r^2)$ rows) plus (rank $2K$ on all rows). The projected Green's function $G$ is carried across Markov steps by Woodbury at $\mathcal{O}(VN_em)$ per accepted move. Each of the $\mathcal{O}(zN_e)$ local-energy ratios is an $(\lvert S\rvert+2K)$-dimensional determinant assembled in $\mathcal{O}(N_e(\lvert S\rvert(F+K)+K^2))$, and the incremental network update costs $\mathcal{O}(\lvert C\rvert(F\cdot\mathrm{patch}+FK))$, where $C$ is the set of sites whose patch contains a touched site.
* **Sharpest Gap**: It is unproven that a fixed, $N_e$-independent number $K$ of global channels (any $K=o(N_e^{1/2})$ keeps $C_E$ sub-cubic) closes the about $3$–$4\times10^{-3}$ per site gap between local backflow (SCALE) and global backflow (ACE or transformer) at $16\times16$, $\delta=1/8$, $t'=-0.2$. It is equally unproven that it reproduces the same stripe and pairing correlations $C(r)$. Liu–Clark's finding that pure rank-$r$ corrections underperform NNBF is the main risk, mitigated here only because the local part stays full-rank. The cost and exactness part is settled by construction and is what the 24-hour test checks; the accuracy part needs a follow-up training comparison of $K=0$ versus $K\in\{4,16\}$ versus transformer backflow at $8\times8$ and $16\times16$. That comparison needs JAX on a GPU, which this numpy-only environment does not have, and should take at most a few GPU-days.

### 5. Local Autopsy Log
* **Route 1 (finite-range convolutional backflow with Woodbury)**: Gate 0. It is SCALE (arXiv:2604.25775) plus Lrux kernels. Its known adoption barrier, an energy gap of about $3$–$4\times10^{-3}$ per site to global backflow at $16\times16$, larger than the $10^{-3}t$ competing-order scale, is left untouched.
* **Route 2 (readout-rank ratios for transformer backflow)**: Gate 1. The attention forward pass per connected configuration is $\Theta(V^2d)$, so $C_E=\Theta(N_e^3d)$ regardless of determinant algebra, and the rank bound $d$ is not smaller than $N_e$ in published runs ($d=256$, $N_e=224$).
* **Route 4 (unbiased stochastic determinant ratios for unrestricted NNBF)**: Gate 1. The matrix-output bound ($\Omega(N_e^2)$ per $\mathbf{n}'$ just to form $\Delta$) keeps $C_E=\Omega(N_e^3)$, and the unbiased exponential of a stochastic log-determinant has variance exponential in $\lVert\log(I+X)\rVert$, which diverges near nodes.
### Round 2 — Mutation & Replenishment (2026-11-11)
* **Autopsy of the round-1 survivor (LGB-BF, Route 3)**, boundary conditions for this round:
    * **B1 (Gate 2, test could not fail).** The round-1 metric was the timing slope of a Woodbury implementation on random weights. The foil already has $C_E=\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$, so the metric did not separate the route from the foil, and the only non-trivial claim (accuracy at fixed $K$) was untested. Consequence: the round-2 test measures accuracy, namely $\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}$ and $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ against full NNBF at matched parameter count, by exact sums on the P1.1 clusters. Cost is a side check only.
    * **B2 (Gate 0, family too small).** LGB's global term $u_x(\mathrm{patch})\odot c(\mathbf{n})$, with $c$ a single pooled $\tanh$, is a strict subfamily of the Liu–Clark rank-$r$ correction, whose $r\times N_e$ coefficient block is an arbitrary function of $\mathbf{n}$. Consequence: every round-2 family must contain the Liu–Clark / HFDS family as a special case, and the restriction relative to NNBF must be measured, not argued.
    * **B3 (P1.1 result).** Width fixes amplitudes, not signs. A cheap family is only worth having if it keeps the sign quality $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ of full NNBF. Conversely, a deficit that is purely amplitude can be repaired by a positive factor whose hop response costs $\mathcal{O}(H_a)$, where $H_a$ is its hidden width.
* **Notation for this round.** The configuration is $\mathbf{n}$, with $N_\sigma$ particles of spin $\sigma\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}$ on $V$ sites; $\mathrm{occ}_\sigma(\mathbf{n})$ is the sorted list of occupied sites; $x=2\mathbf{n}-1\in\{\pm1\}^{2V}$ is the network input. For a matrix $X$ with one row per site, $X[\mathrm{occ}_\sigma]$ keeps the occupied rows. The reference orbitals are $\Phi_{0\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times N_\sigma}$. The hidden-fermion orbitals are $\Phi_{h,k\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times r}$, where $r$ is the rank per spin and per term. The configuration-dependent coefficient blocks $C_{k\sigma}(\mathbf{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times N_\sigma}$ are produced by a one-hidden-layer $\tanh$ "sign trunk" of width $H_s$. The positive amplitude factor is $e^{J(\mathbf{n})}$ with $J=w_a^{\mathsf T}\tanh(W_ax+b_a)$, of width $H_a$. The reference matrix is $A_{0\sigma}=\Phi_{0\sigma}[\mathrm{occ}_\sigma]$, and the table is $Y_{k\sigma}=A_{0\sigma}^{-1}\Phi_{h,k\sigma}[\mathrm{occ}_\sigma]\in\mathbb{R}^{N_\sigma\times r}$.
* **Generation Batch 2**:
  - **Route 2.1 (Mutated from 3 via Operation A.1 and A.3): split-capacity hidden-fermion backflow (SC-HFDS).**
    $$\psi(\mathbf{n})=e^{J(\mathbf{n})}\prod_{\sigma}\det\!\big(\Phi_{0\sigma}[\mathrm{occ}_\sigma]+\Phi_{h,\sigma}[\mathrm{occ}_\sigma]\,C_\sigma(\mathbf{n})\big).$$
    The Gate-0 sub-component (LGB's pooled global channel) is replaced by Liu–Clark's unrestricted coefficient block $C_\sigma(\mathbf{n})$, so the family contains the Liu–Clark / HFDS family exactly ($J\equiv0$), which answers B2. The Gate-2 sub-component (the metric) is retargeted to the higher-order witness $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}$, which answers B1. The mechanism aimed at the adoption barrier: Liu–Clark find that HFDS-type low-rank corrections underperform NNBF at matched size. B3 suggests that this deficit may be amplitude, which a separate wide positive network $e^{J}$ repairs at $\mathcal{O}(H_a)$ per hop. The capacity is therefore split: a narrow sign trunk ($H_s=64$) and a wide amplitude network ($H_a$ chosen to match NNBF's parameter count). Hop response: a hop of particle $i$ from site $x$ to site $y$ changes row $i$ plus the rank-$r$ term $\Phi_{h,\sigma}[\mathrm{occ}']\Delta C$, so $\operatorname{rank}\Delta\le r+1$.
    Exact ratio with carried quantities. The carried quantities are $A_0^{-1}$, $G_0=\Phi_0A_0^{-1}$ ($V\times N_\sigma$), $Y$, and $\mathcal{H}=G_0\Phi_h[\mathrm{occ}]$ ($V\times r$). They give $\rho_0=G_0[y,i]$ and $Y'=Y+A_0^{-1}[:,i]\otimes(\Phi_h[y]-\mathcal{H}[y])/\rho_0$. The ratio is
    $$\frac{\psi(\mathbf{n}')}{\psi(\mathbf{n})}=e^{J'-J}\rho_0\frac{\det(I_r+C'_\sigma Y'_\sigma)\det(I_r+C'_{\bar\sigma}Y_{\bar\sigma})}{\det(I_r+C_\sigma Y_\sigma)\det(I_r+C_{\bar\sigma}Y_{\bar\sigma})},$$
    where $\bar\sigma$ is the other spin. The cost per connected configuration is $\mathcal{O}(N_e(H_sr+r^2)+H_a)$, so $C_E=\mathcal{O}(zN_e^2H_sr)$. HFPS (Chen, Roth et al., arXiv:2507.10705) states $\mathcal{O}(N^2\tilde N)$ per new Pfaffian, with $\tilde N$ hidden fermions, and hence $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ per VMC step. The in-place-row convention needs no Jordan–Wigner sign, and the smoke test verifies this pointwise against $(H\psi)(\mathbf{n})/\psi(\mathbf{n})$ on every configuration.
    - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS. HFDS (Robledo Moreno et al., PNAS 119, e2122059119) and its NNBF reading (Liu & Clark, arXiv:2311.09450) exist. HFPS (arXiv:2507.10705) already multiplies a hidden-fermion Pfaffian by a Jastrow taken from 4 of the 32 output channels of the same deep CNN. Why low-rank families are not the default: Liu–Clark report that direct SPO updates (full NNBF) are more expressive at matched size, and HFPS pays $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ per step because the hidden block is rewritten and re-contracted per configuration. What this route adds, as a concrete mechanism aimed at each wall:
        * (i) A carried-table ratio, in which $C$ multiplies a fixed $\Phi_h$ only from the right, giving $\mathcal{O}(N_e)$ per ratio. This removes the $\mathcal{O}(N^2\tilde N)$ per-ratio contraction.
        * (ii) A capacity split justified by a measured sign/amplitude decomposition, with the explicit, falsifiable claim that the Liu–Clark deficit is amplitude and not sign. No published work measures $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ for HFDS-type versus NNBF states.
        * Risk stated plainly: if the decisive test shows the deficit is sign, the route dies, and the result itself (low-rank response costs sign) closes the "sub-cubic without losing accuracy" question for this family class.
    - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Exact (matrix determinant lemma plus Sherman–Morrison, no truncation); number-conserving; $C_E=\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$ at fixed $(H_s,r,H_a)$; sweep $\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$, which meets the F2 floor. No convergence or hardness claim is made.
    - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. The metric is $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ against full NNBF at matched parameter count. On the decisive instances the foil's sign error is about $2\times$ NNBF's (P1.1: $0.154$ vs $0.077$ on $N_\uparrow=N_\downarrow=4$). An explicit control arm, Slater $\times$ neural Jastrow (`sjnet`, $r=0$, same parameter count), must lose by the pre-committed factor ($\gamma\le0.75$), otherwise the low-rank channel is doing no sign work and the route FAILS.
    - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. The test instances are the three doped $4\times3$ PBC $t'=-0.25$ clusters of P1.1, which are non-stoquastic. [Corrected in round 3: the competitor once cited for the $N_\uparrow=N_\downarrow=4$ instance is a triplet at another momentum, so all three instances test the sign half only.]
    - *Verdict*: SURVIVED (decisive test below).
  - **Route 2.2 (Operation B.3, cross-field translation: the multi-determinant table method of continuum QMC): shared-reference low-rank multi-determinant (SK-LR).**
    $$\psi(\mathbf{n})=e^{J(\mathbf{n})}\prod_\sigma\det A_{0\sigma}\;\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_k\prod_\sigma\det\!\big(I_r+C_{k\sigma}(\mathbf{n})Y_{k\sigma}(\mathbf{n})\big),$$
    where $w_k$ are trainable weights. Every term shares the reference $A_{0\sigma}$, so one carried inverse serves all $K$ terms. This is the move that made multi-Slater–Jastrow QMC cost quadratic in particle number and linear in the number of excitations (Clark, Morales, McMinis, Kim & Scuseria, J. Chem. Phys. 135, 244105, 2011, arXiv:1106.2456). Here it is ported to configuration-dependent coefficients. Each term has a rank-$\le r+1$ hop response, so the per-hop cost is $K$ times that of Route 2.1, and $C_E=\mathcal{O}(zKN_e^2H_sr)$.
    Sign-relevant strict containment, provable at fixed table. By Cauchy–Binet, $\det(I_r+CY)=\sum_{\ell\le r}\sum_{\lvert P\rvert=\lvert Q\rvert=\ell}\det C[Q,P]\,\det Y[P,Q]$. So a single HFDS term (Liu–Clark, and Route 2.1) makes $\psi/\psi_{\mathrm{ref}}$ a linear form in the minors of the table $Y$, whose coefficient vector lies in the image of the minor map (a determinantal variety). $K$ terms give its $K$-secant, which is strictly larger for $K\ge2$, $r\ge2$. In addition, the $\uparrow\downarrow$ products are summed, so the coefficient tensor is no longer a single product across spins. The node set $\{\psi=0\}$ is the zero set of this form in the table entries. The family therefore has strictly more nodal freedom than HFDS($r$) on the same table, and SK-LR($K{=}1$, $J{=}0$) is exactly HFDS($r$). This is the "provably richer than Liu–Clark in a sign-relevant way" requirement. It is a statement about the representable node sets at a fixed reference and table, not about learnability, which the test measures.
    - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS.
        * Multi-determinant NNBF exists. Loehr & Clark (arXiv:2510.26906) find that multi-determinant expansions beat width increases, but each determinant is a full NNBF determinant with its own $\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$ amplitude, and their projected-state optimization costs about 3000 GPU-hours, which is the massive-compute wall.
        * The table method exists only for fixed CI coefficients over fixed orbitals.
        * HFPS uses one Pfaffian.
        * I found no ansatz that sums configuration-dependent low-rank terms over a single carried reference. The adoption wall it targets is the $K\times\mathcal{O}(N_e^4)$ cost of multi-determinant NNBF, which this route replaces by $K\times\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$, while keeping the nodal freedom of a sum. P1.1 says that freedom is the binding one, since width does not buy it.
    - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Exact, number-conserving, $C_E=\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$ at fixed $(K,r,H_s,H_a)$, one carried inverse per spin. The only division is by the term sum at the sampled $\mathbf{n}$, which $\lvert\psi\rvert^2$ sampling keeps away from zero; ratios at $\mathbf{n}'$ stay exact even when the term sum there cancels.
    - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. The same metric and the same `sjnet` control as Route 2.1. The additional control `mdnnbf` (unrestricted $K$-determinant NNBF at matched parameters) sets the reference, so "sum of determinants helps anyone" is separated from "the cheap restricted sum keeps up".
    - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. The same instances and the same competitor statement as Route 2.1.
    - *Verdict*: SURVIVED (shares the decisive test).
  - **Route 2.3 (Operation B.1, frame transformation from the particle–hole (Slater) frame to the particle–particle (geminal) frame): low-rank-response AGP.** $\psi=e^J\det\big(F(\mathbf{n})[\mathrm{occ}_\uparrow,\mathrm{occ}_\downarrow]\big)$ with $F(\mathbf{n})=F_0+P\,Q(\mathbf{n})^{\mathsf T}$, where $F_0\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times V}$ is a pair function and $P,Q\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times r}$.   - **Route 2.4 (Operation B.1, momentum / natural-orbital frame): Thouless rotation into an active virtual window.** $\Phi(\mathbf{n})=\Phi_{\mathrm{occ}}+\Phi_{\mathrm{virt}}^{\mathrm{act}}\kappa(\mathbf{n})$, with $\Phi_{\mathrm{virt}}^{\mathrm{act}}$ the $r$ mean-field virtual orbitals nearest the Fermi level (antinodal momenta), and $\kappa(\mathbf{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times N_e}$.

### Top Surviving Route & Sharpest Gap (Round 2)
* **Mechanism (Route 2.2, SK-LR; Route 2.1 is its $K=1$ case and is tested in the same job)**:
    * All determinant terms share one reference determinant.
    * Each term's configuration dependence enters only as a right factor $C_{k\sigma}(\mathbf{n})$ against a fixed hidden-orbital table.
    * The exact ratio for every hop is therefore an $r\times r$ determinant built from the carried $A_0^{-1}$, $G_0$, $Y$ and $\mathcal{H}$ in $\mathcal{O}(N_e)$, and $C_E=\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$.
    * Sign freedom comes from the sum over terms (the $K$-secant of the minor map), not from network width.
    * Amplitude freedom comes from a separate positive network that costs $\mathcal{O}(H_a)$ per hop.
* **Sharpest Gap**:
    * On the decisive clusters $N_\sigma\in\{4,5\}$, so $r=2$ is 40–50% of full rank. A PASS shows that low-rank response loses no sign quality where the restriction is mild. It does not show that a fixed $r$ suffices at $N_\sigma\sim100$, where the restriction is severe. That scaling question cannot be answered with exact $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$, and needs a VMC comparison at $8\times8$ against NNBF using the variance-extrapolated energy as the proxy.
    * A FAIL here is decisive, because the restriction only gets tighter with size.
    * Second gap: SR seed variance at the 10–15% level could blur $\rho_s$. Two seeds per arm, best-energy seed per arm, and the AMBIGUOUS rerun rule are the mitigation.

    - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Exact, number-conserving, $S^z$-conserving; $C_E=\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$ and sweep $\mathcal{O}(N_e^3)$ at fixed $(K,r,H_s,H_a)$. Symmetry projection over a group $G$ multiplies the per-amplitude cost by $\lvert G\rvert$ for every arm alike; translations make $\lvert G\rvert\propto V$, which is why production would project only the point group (stated, not hidden).
    - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. The PASS needs, on every counted instance, the projected-NNBF sign quality, a win over the equal-rank single term (median ratio $\le0.85$ and 7 of 9 seed pairs), and a win over Slater$\times$neural Jastrow by a factor $1/0.6$; a premise-gated instance cannot be passed by a near-exact control.
    - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS for the sign half only. The instances are doped, $t'\ne0$, 2D, open or cylindrical, non-stoquastic; $d_{S\chi}\gg P$ so the projected control is not exact by counting.
    - *Verdict*: SURVIVED (decisive test below).
  - **Route 3.2 (Stage 3.3, Operation B.1 on the killed Route 2.1: frame transformation from the particle–hole (Slater) frame to the particle–particle (geminal) frame): shared-pairing-reference low-rank sum (SK-AGP).**
    $$\psi(\mathbf{n})=e^{J(\mathbf{n})}\det B_0(\mathbf{n})\sum_{k=1}^{K}w_k\det\!\big(I_r+C_k(\mathbf{n})[:,\mathrm{occ}_\downarrow]\,B_0^{-1}\Phi_{h,k}[\mathrm{occ}_\uparrow]\big),\qquad B_0=F_0[\mathrm{occ}_\uparrow,\mathrm{occ}_\downarrow].$$
    Here $F_0\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times V}$ is a trainable singlet pairing function (initialised at a $d$-wave BCS state), $\Phi_{h,k}\in\mathbb{R}^{V\times r}$ are fixed-per-term hidden rows on the $\uparrow$ side, and $C_k(\mathbf{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times V}$ is network output with one column per site, selected by the $\downarrow$ occupations. With $r=0$ it is exactly the Foil (projected BCS times a Jastrow, here a neural one). An $\uparrow$ hop replaces a row of $B_0$, a $\downarrow$ hop a column, an exchange both; each ratio is $\rho_0\det(I_r+C'Y')$ with $\rho_0$ read from the carried tables $W=B_0^{-1}F_0[\mathrm{occ}_\uparrow,:]$ and $W'=F_0[:,\mathrm{occ}_\downarrow]B_0^{-1}$, and $Y'$ from a rank-one or rank-two Sherman–Morrison step in $\mathcal{O}(N_er)$ (formulas in `tj_core.eloc_fast_agp`, checked against $(H\psi)(\mathbf{n})/\psi(\mathbf{n})$ on every smoke configuration to $10^{-13}$).
    - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS, with the overlap stated. One SK-AGP term is a one-sided hidden-fermion Pfaffian (HFPS, arXiv:2507.10705) restricted to singlet pairing; Route 2.3 died for being exactly that. What is new is the same mechanism as Route 3.1 (a sum of configuration-dependent low-rank terms over one carried pairing reference) placed on the Foil's own reference. The adoption barrier it targets is HFPS's $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ per step (hidden block rewritten per configuration) and the Foil's bias; the mechanism is the carried pairing table, which brings $C_E$ to $\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$, plus the sum for sign freedom. Distinctness from Route 3.1 is in the reference frame (pairing versus Slater); the two routes share the sum mechanism, so a FAIL of that mechanism is evidence against both, while a PASS of only one says which frame the low-rank sum needs.
    - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Exact, number-conserving, $S^z=0$; $C_E=\mathcal{O}(N_e^2)$ measured in the cost check; the sweep is not implemented for this family and is not claimed.
    - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. Its own Gate-2 control is the Foil itself ($r=0$), and the equal-rank control is one term of rank 4 on the same pairing reference.
    - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS for the sign half only, as Route 3.1.
    - *Verdict*: SURVIVED (shares the decisive test).
  - **Route 3.3 (Operation A on Route 2.1's carried-table component, aimed at the new projection-cost wall): image-table projected SK-LR.** With a reference whose column span is $G$-invariant, $\Phi_0[g\,\mathrm{occ}]=\Phi_0[\mathrm{occ}]R_g$, so one carried inverse serves all $\lvert G\rvert$ images and only $\lvert G\rvert K$ extra table columns are needed.
    - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): FAIL as a cost route. The per-image network forward $C_k(g\mathbf{n}')$ costs $\mathcal{O}(H_sKrN_e)$ for every image and connection, identical to carrying separate tables, so the local energy is unchanged; only the sweep and memory improve, and with translations $\lvert G\rvert\propto V$ adds a factor $N_e$ either way. A complex plane-wave reference ($R_g$ monomial) is needed even for the sweep gain.
    - *Verdict*: KILLED.
  - **Route 3.4 (Operation A on round-1 LGB: full-rank local rows plus an unrestricted Liu–Clark block, "SCALE + HFDS").** It contains both SCALE and HFDS, which answers the round-1 subfamily objection.
    - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield, for testing): FAIL. On every exact-sum cluster ($4\times4$) the range-1 patch with $t'$ bonds covers nearly every particle, so the local part is global and the restriction cannot be tested; the same reason killed Route 2.6.
    - Gate 0: at risk as a sum of two published components.
    - *Verdict*: KILLED.
