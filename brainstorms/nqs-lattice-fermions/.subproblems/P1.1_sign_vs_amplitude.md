# Subproblem: P1.1 Sign versus amplitude error budget
**Parent Chain**: P1 -> P1.1  **Status**: ANSWERED, scoped to $t$–$J$ at exact-diagonalization size (2026-11-17; see the Leaf Status Summary). History: decisive test 329057 PASS (sign binding) on unprojected NNBF, superseded by P1.1-P (407525, SYMMETRY-DOMINATED)
### 1. Mathematical Abstraction & Barriers
* **Formulation**:
  - *Inputs*: a number-conserving Hubbard Hamiltonian $\hat H$ on $V$ sites with $(N_\uparrow,N_\downarrow)$ fixed, written as a real symmetric matrix $H_{\mathbf n\mathbf n'}$ in the Jordan–Wigner-ordered occupation basis $\mathcal B$ (dimension $D$); its ground state $\psi_0$ with energy $E_0$; a real variational state $\psi_\theta$ (NNBF, HFDS or the foil) with energy $E_\theta$.
  - *Sign structure*: $s_\theta(\mathbf n)=\mathrm{sgn}\,\psi_\theta(\mathbf n)$, $S_\theta=\mathrm{diag}(s_\theta)$, and the sign-gauged matrix $K_\theta=S_\theta H S_\theta$.
  - *Sign-limited energy* (best energy any amplitude can reach with the signs of $\psi_\theta$):
$$E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s_\theta)=\min_{a\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{D}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{a^{\mathsf T}K_\theta a}{a^{\mathsf T}a}.$$
  - *Budget*: with $\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}=E_\theta-E_0$,
$$\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}=E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s_\theta)-E_0\ \ge 0,\qquad \Delta E_{\mathrm{amp}}=E_\theta-E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s_\theta)\ \ge 0,\qquad \Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}+\Delta E_{\mathrm{amp}}=\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}},$$
  and the sign fraction $f_E=\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}/\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}\in[0,1]$. Both pieces are non-negative by construction (the minimum over $a$ includes $a=\lvert\psi_\theta\rvert$ and is bounded below by $E_0$), so the split is additive and order-independent. It is invariant under any diagonal gauge $\mathbf n\mapsto\pm$ applied to both $\psi_0$ and $\psi_\theta$, so it does not depend on the Jordan–Wigner ordering.
  - *Pairing budget*: with $a^\star$ the minimizer, $P_\phi(b)=\langle\phi\rvert\hat\Delta^\dagger_{b_0}\hat\Delta_b\lvert\phi\rangle/\langle\phi\vert\phi\rangle$ for the singlet bond-pair operator $\hat\Delta_b=(\hat c_{j\downarrow}\hat c_{i\uparrow}-\hat c_{j\uparrow}\hat c_{i\downarrow})/\sqrt2$ on nearest-neighbour bond $b=(i,j)$ and reference bond $b_0$, define $e_C^{\mathrm{tot}}=\max_{b:\,d(b,b_0)\ge r_{\min}}\lvert P_{\psi_\theta}(b)-P_{\psi_0}(b)\rvert$, $e_C^{\mathrm{sign}}$ the same with $\psi_\theta\to s_\theta a^\star$, and $f_C=e_C^{\mathrm{sign}}/e_C^{\mathrm{tot}}$.
  - *Rigorous lower bound*: deleting the positive off-diagonal entries of $K_\theta$ gives a stoquastic matrix $K_\theta^{-}$, and since $a^{\mathsf T}(K_\theta-K_\theta^{-})a\ge0$ for $a\ge0$, $E_{\mathrm{stoq}}(s_\theta)=\lambda_{\min}(K^-_\theta)\le E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s_\theta)$. Hence $f_E^{\mathrm{lo}}=\max(0,E_{\mathrm{stoq}}-E_0)/\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}\le f_E$.
  - *Solved means*: for a state-of-the-art fermionic NQS on a doped, $t'\neq0$, 2D cluster at $U/t\in\{4,8\}$, $f_E$ and $f_C$ are known with an optimizer-calibrated error, together with their trend in network width $H$, so that the question "is P1 binding?" has an instance-wise yes/no answer.
* **The Node Barrier**:
  - *Fundamental*: computing $E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s)$ is a minimization of a quadratic form over the non-negative orthant, i.e. a copositivity-type problem, which is co-NP-complete in general (Murty & Kabadi, Math. Program. 39, 117, 1987, DOI:10.1007/BF02592948). It is also only defined relative to the exact $\psi_0$, so the budget needs ED (or an equivalent reference) and cannot be measured at the sizes where P1 matters. At ED sizes the problem is therefore bracketed, not solved: every feasible point (the L-BFGS-B results from four starts, and the self-consistent ten Haaf fixed-node state with sign $s_\theta$) gives a rigorous upper bound, the stoquastic bound gives a rigorous lower bound, and an exact-sign calibration ($s_\theta\to s_0$, start from uniform amplitudes, must return $E_0$) checks for local-minimum trouble.
  - *Practical*: (i) published fermionic NQS benchmarks report energies and V-scores, not sign accuracy (searches below found no sign-resolved budget for HFDS or NNBF on Hubbard); (ii) the spin-model literature uses hybrid swaps $s_0\lvert\psi_\theta\rvert$ and sign-overlap scores, which are not additive and saturate with $V$; (iii) lattice fixed-node (ten Haaf et al., PRB 51, 13039, 1995) is not a sign-only quantity on a lattice, because the sign-flip term needs the guiding amplitudes, so $E_{\mathrm{VMC}}-E_{\mathrm{FN}}$ mixes the two error sources.
  - *Anti-Solution Space*: (a) declaring "signs are the problem" from a sign-overlap score $\sum_{\mathbf n}\lvert\psi_0\rvert^2[s_\theta=s_0]$ (it tends to $0$ for any fixed per-site accuracy as $V$ grows, and carries no energy weight); (b) budgets measured at half filling with $t'=0$, in 1D, or at $U=0$ (Hard Core); (c) budgets for a single, under-trained network (the answer then measures the optimizer, not the ansatz class), which is why the test sweeps width; (d) any decomposition whose two parts can be negative or depend on order.
### 1b. Analogy Map
Written retroactively (2026-11-17) against the routes this leaf ran (R1–R4, Route Register). "Tried (R$n$)" means a route of this leaf used the technique; "untried" entries are candidate ingredients, not results.
* **Barrier A, attribution.** *Field-neutral form*: an approximate solution has two coupled parts, a discrete pattern (here the sign of each configuration) and continuous magnitudes; split its error between them so that both shares are non-negative, add up to the total, and do not depend on the order in which the parts are fixed.
  - *Cooperative game theory and machine-learning feature attribution (Shapley values).* Source problem: share a model's prediction among interacting input features. Correspondence: players are {sign, amplitude}; the value of a coalition is the energy error of the hybrid state built with those parts taken exact; a player's Shapley value is its share of $\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}$. Technique: average each player's marginal contribution over both orders. Why it worked there: attribution is judged only by axioms (efficiency, symmetry), and every coalition is a meaningful input. Translation gap: the hybrid $s_0\lvert\psi_\theta\rvert$ is not a meaningful state, because the trained magnitudes are co-adapted to the trained signs; its energy exceeds $E_\theta$ and a share turns negative. Citation: Lundberg & Lee, NeurIPS 2017 (arXiv:1705.07874). Status: tried (R2), killed.
  - *Sparse regression, refitting at fixed support (relaxed Lasso).* Source problem: separate the error of choosing the wrong support and signs of the coefficients from the shrinkage error of their magnitudes. Correspondence: support and sign pattern ↔ $s_\theta$; refitted coefficients ↔ optimal amplitudes $a^\star$; refit loss ↔ $E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s_\theta)$. Technique: freeze the discrete choice and re-optimise the continuous part, so the remaining loss belongs to the discrete choice alone. Why it worked there: the refit is a convex least-squares problem. Translation gap: here the refit is a Rayleigh quotient over the non-negative orthant, which is non-convex (Barrier B), so it can only be bracketed. Citation: Meinshausen, Comput. Stat. Data Anal. 52, 374 (2007). Status: tried (R1); this is R1's construction.
  - *Continuum fixed-node diffusion Monte Carlo.* Source problem: the lowest energy of any wavefunction with a given nodal surface. Correspondence: nodal surface ↔ $s_\theta$; fixed-node energy ↔ $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$. Technique: impose the nodes as an infinite wall and project. Why it worked there: in continuous space the nodes are a codimension-one surface, the wall decouples the nodal pockets, and the fixed-node energy is exactly the variational minimum at fixed nodes, independent of the guide's amplitudes. Translation gap: on a lattice, sign-violating hops connect configurations directly; ten Haaf's construction moves them to the diagonal weighted by the guide's amplitude ratios, so the lattice fixed-node energy depends on amplitudes and is only an upper bound on $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$. Citation: Reynolds, Ceperley, Alder & Lester, J. Chem. Phys. 77, 5593 (1982); ten Haaf et al., PRB 51, 13039 (1995). Status: tried (R4), killed; repaired (R1), where it is one of the upper-bound starts.
  - *Global sensitivity analysis (Sobol indices).* Source problem: apportion the variance of a model output among inputs that interact. Correspondence: inputs ↔ sign and amplitude; output variance ↔ energy error. Technique: an ANOVA decomposition with an explicit interaction term. Why it worked there: the inputs are independent random variables, which makes the decomposition unique. Translation gap: sign and amplitude are not independent; the interaction term is exactly the co-adaptation that broke R2, and a third "interaction" share does not answer which part binds. Citation: Sobol', Math. Comput. Simul. 55, 271 (2001). Status: untried (it fails the same Gate 0 barrier as R2 at T0).
* **Barrier B, copositivity.** *Field-neutral form*: minimise a quadratic form over the non-negative orthant in dimension $D\sim10^6$, a co-NP-hard problem in general, and certify the answer from both sides.
  - *Bound-constrained quasi-Newton optimisation (non-negative least squares).* Source problem: minimise a smooth objective subject to $a\ge0$. Correspondence: objective ↔ $a^{\mathsf T}K_\theta a/a^{\mathsf T}a$. Technique: projected limited-memory quasi-Newton (L-BFGS-B). Why it worked there: the objective is convex, so any local minimum is global. Translation gap: the Rayleigh quotient on the orthant has local minima on its faces; R1 repairs this with four starts, a spread guard and an exact-sign calibration that must return $E_0$. Citation: Byrd, Lu, Nocedal & Zhu, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 16, 1190 (1995). Status: tried (R1).
  - *Copositive and doubly non-negative conic relaxation.* Source problem: global bounds for non-convex quadratic programs over the orthant. Correspondence: $\min_{a\ge0,\lVert a\rVert=1}a^{\mathsf T}K_\theta a$ ↔ a standard quadratic program; its conic dual value ↔ a lower bound on $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$. Technique: lift $aa^{\mathsf T}\to X$ and relax the completely positive cone to the doubly non-negative cone (positive semidefinite and entrywise non-negative), giving a semidefinite program. Why it worked there: problem sizes of hundreds to thousands of variables. Translation gap: a $D\times D$ semidefinite program is infeasible at $D=6\times10^5$; it would need restriction to the configurations touched by sign-violating bonds. Citation: Burer, Math. Program. 120, 479 (2009); Murty & Kabadi, Math. Program. 39, 117 (1987). Status: untried; it is the candidate for a non-vacuous lower bound (reopening ledger row 7).
  - *Perron–Frobenius theory and non-stoquasticity measures in quantum Monte Carlo.* Source problem: quantify how far a Hamiltonian is from sign-free, and choose a basis that minimises that distance. Correspondence: deleting positive off-diagonals ↔ $K_\theta^-$; non-stoquasticity measure ↔ $E_{\mathrm{sign}}-E_{\mathrm{stoq}}$. Technique: the Perron vector of the sign-free part gives a rigorous lower bound; Hangleiter et al. optimise local basis rotations to shrink the non-stoquastic weight. Why it worked there: frustrated spin ladders with a small, local basis freedom. Translation gap: here the basis is fixed by the trial sign, and the deleted entries carry so much weight that the bound lay $2.7$–$4.1t$ below $E_0$ on every instance (329057). Citation: Hangleiter, Roth, Nagaj & Eisert, Sci. Adv. 6, eabb8341 (2020). Status: tried (R1, lower bound), vacuous.
* **Barrier C, size transfer.** *Field-neutral form*: a diagnostic needs an exact reference that exists only for small instances; estimate it at large instances where only cheap or approximate references exist.
  - *Finite-size scaling (statistical physics).* Source problem: infer the thermodynamic limit from a sequence of small systems. Correspondence: $f_E(V)$ ↔ the scaled observable. Technique: extrapolate in $1/V$ with a scaling ansatz. Why it worked there: universality fixes the functional form. Translation gap: only 12 to 16 sites are within ED, no scaling form for $f_E$ is known, and the sector-fitting regime changes with $V$. Citation: Fisher & Barber, PRL 28, 1516 (1972). Status: untried.
  - *Multifidelity estimation (uncertainty quantification).* Source problem: estimate an expensive quantity from many cheap model evaluations and a few expensive ones. Correspondence: high fidelity ↔ the exact $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$; low fidelity ↔ $E_{\mathrm{stoq}}$ or $E_{\mathrm{FN}}$ from sign-free projector QMC, which scales. Technique: control variates whose weights come from the correlation between fidelities. Why it worked there: the fidelities are strongly correlated and that correlation is estimated on paired evaluations at the target scale. Translation gap: no high-fidelity evaluation exists at the target size, so the correlation would be learned only at ED size, which is the transfer assumption itself; and $E_{\mathrm{stoq}}$ was vacuous here. Citation: Peherstorfer, Willcox & Gunzburger, SIAM Rev. 60, 550 (2018). Status: untried (it is the Sharpest-Gap lemma in another form).
  - *A posteriori error estimation (finite elements).* Source problem: bound the error of an approximate solution without the exact one. Correspondence: residual $\lVert(\hat H-E_\theta)\psi_\theta\rVert$ ↔ element residual. Technique: residual-based estimators. Why it worked there: coercive problems with a stability constant known in advance. Translation gap: the analogous eigenvalue bounds (Temple) need a spectral gap, which closes at the competing orders of the Hard Core (master anti-pattern "bounds that need the gap"), and a residual does not separate sign from amplitude. Citation: Ainsworth & Oden, *A Posteriori Error Estimation in Finite Element Analysis* (Wiley, 2000). Status: untried (fails at T0 on the gap).
### 2. Candidate Routes & Falsification Gates
* **Route 1**: *Sign-constrained optimal-amplitude budget with a copositive bracket.* Bound $E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s_\theta)$ from above by the lowest of bound-constrained L-BFGS-B over the full basis from four starts ($\lvert\psi_\theta\rvert$, $\lvert\psi_0\rvert$, uniform, the ten Haaf fixed-node state) and the fixed-node energy itself, bracket it from below by the stoquastic bound $E_{\mathrm{stoq}}(s_\theta)$, calibrate the optimizer on $s_0$, and evaluate $f_E$, $f_C$ for UHF, the Jastrow–Slater foil, and NNBF at widths $H\in\{8,32,128\}$ on 2D doped $t$–$t'$–$U$ clusters. Point of departure: the spin-model sign/amplitude studies only swap or fix signs; here the sign part is defined as a variational minimum, so the split is additive, non-negative and gauge-invariant.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS. Related work: Westerhout et al. (Nat. Commun. 11, 1593, 2020; arXiv:1907.08186) showed that sign generalization is harder than amplitude generalization for frustrated spins; Szabó & Castelnovo (PRR 2, 033075, 2020; arXiv:2002.04613) separated phase and modulus networks; Bukov, Schmitt & Dupont (SciPost Phys. 10, 147, 2021; arXiv:2011.11214) attributed failure to a rugged landscape over the sign; arXiv:2308.09664 optimized amplitudes at fixed signs for $J_1$–$J_2$. None of these is fermionic, and none gives an additive energy budget or a pairing-correlation budget. Targeted searches for HFDS/NNBF/transformer-backflow Hubbard papers (Luo & Clark PRL 122, 226401; Robledo Moreno et al. PNAS 119, e2122059119; Lange et al. arXiv:2411.10430; Loehr & Clark arXiv:2510.26906; Viteritti et al. arXiv:2604.21978) found energies only, with no sign-resolved error. Adoption barrier of the existing tools: swap metrics are non-additive and order-dependent, and lattice FN is not sign-only. The concrete fix is the variational definition of $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ together with the stoquastic lower bound and the exact-sign calibration.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): PASS. Particle number and $S^z$ are exact (fixed-sector basis); the budget is invariant under diagonal gauge changes; no polynomial-time claim is made: the method is explicitly exponential (ED-sized), and it is a diagnostic that answers a routing question, not a solver. The co-NP-hardness of the inner problem is handled by bracketing ($E_{\mathrm{stoq}}\le E_{\mathrm{sign}}\le E_{\mathrm{sign}}^{\mathrm{up}}\le E_{\mathrm{FN}}$), so $f_E^{\mathrm{up}}$ is a rigorous upper bound and $f_E^{\mathrm{lo}}$ a rigorous lower bound on the sign fraction.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): PASS. The metric is state-specific, and the foil and UHF are measured in the same run; the diagnostic is informative only if it separates them from NNBF, which the smoke test already shows (UHF $f_E=0.033$, foil $0.231$, NNBF $0.141$ to $0.205$ on a $3\times2$ cluster). A foil cannot "hit" a routing answer: if the foil and NNBF had identical budgets, that itself would be a result (the backflow adds nothing to signs). The pre-committed $M$ uses only the widest NNBF.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): PASS. Instances are 2D clusters with $t'=-0.25t$, hole doping $1/6$ and $1/3$, and $U/t\in\{4,8\}$; the doped 2D $t$–$t'$ Hubbard model has no known stoquastic diagonal gauge, so each $K_\theta$ is expected to keep positive off-diagonals (sign-violating bonds) and the inner problem is a genuine sign-constrained one. In the smoke run this shows up as $E_{\mathrm{stoq}}<E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ for every state. Nothing relies on half filling, 1D or $U=0$; the $U=0$ and two-site cases appear only as correctness checks.
  - *Verdict*: SURVIVED
* **Route 2**: *Hybrid sign/amplitude swap (Shapley) budget.* Evaluate $E[s_\theta\lvert\psi_0\rvert]$ (variational signs, exact amplitudes) and $E[s_0\lvert\psi_\theta\rvert]$ (exact signs, variational amplitudes) and assign the energy error by the two-player Shapley average of the swaps.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): FAIL. Swapping or fixing signs is the existing spin-model practice (arXiv:1907.08186; arXiv:2308.09664). It is not standard for fermions because $\lvert\psi_\theta\rvert$ is co-adapted to the wrong signs: $E[s_0\lvert\psi_\theta\rvert]$ can be higher than $E_\theta$, so the "amplitude part" can be negative and the split depends on order. The route has no mechanism that fixes this. (Both swap energies are still printed by the test as a cross-check.)
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): not evaluated (killed at Gate 0).
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): not evaluated.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): not evaluated.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
* **Route 3**: *Sign-mismatch weight as the budget.* Use $w_{\mathrm{sign}}=\sum_{\mathbf n:\,s_\theta s_0=-1}\lvert\psi_0(\mathbf n)\rvert^2$ (or the sign overlap $\sum_{\mathbf n}\lvert\psi_0\rvert^2s_\theta s_0$) as the measure of how much error the signs carry.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): PASS. Sign-accuracy scores are used in spin NQS (arXiv:1907.08186) but not reported for fermionic NQS; an energy-weighted version would be new.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): FAIL. For any ansatz with a fixed per-site sign accuracy, $w_{\mathrm{sign}}$ grows to its saturation value exponentially in $V$ (orthogonality-catastrophe argument: the probability of a fully correct sign pattern over $V$ weakly correlated regions decays as $e^{-cV}$), so the metric stops discriminating states at moderate $V$. It is also not an energy quantity: a sign error on configurations connected only by small $\lvert H_{\mathbf n\mathbf n'}\rvert$ costs almost nothing, so $w_{\mathrm{sign}}$ cannot answer "what fraction of $\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}$".
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): not evaluated.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): not evaluated.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
* **Route 4**: *Scalable budget by lattice fixed-node GFMC with the NQS as guiding function.* Run fixed-node projector QMC from $\psi_\theta$ and read $E_\theta-E_{\mathrm{FN}}$ as the amplitude part and $E_{\mathrm{FN}}-E_{\mathrm{ref}}$ as the sign part, with $E_{\mathrm{ref}}$ from DMRG/AFQMC, at sizes beyond ED.
  - Gate 0 (Novelty & Adoption): FAIL. DMC on top of NNBF is already done (Loehr & Clark arXiv:2510.26906), and the lattice FN energy is not a sign-only functional: the ten Haaf sign-flip potential $\sum_{\mathbf n'}H_{\mathbf n\mathbf n'}\psi_\theta(\mathbf n')/\psi_\theta(\mathbf n)$ over sign-violating pairs depends on the guiding amplitudes, so $E_{\mathrm{FN}}$ is not the best energy at fixed signs (ten Haaf et al., PRB 51, 13039, 1995). That is exactly why FN energies are not read as sign budgets, and this route has no mechanism that removes the amplitude dependence.
  - Gate 1 (Conservation & Asymptotics): not evaluated.
  - Gate 2 (Baseline Gameability): not evaluated.
  - Gate 3 (Hard-Core Shield): not evaluated.
  - *Verdict*: KILLED
### 3. Top Surviving Route & Sharpest Gap
* **Mechanism**: Route 1. On ED-sized doped 2D clusters, every state is evaluated exactly over the full basis, and the energy error is split into a sign-limited part $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}=E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s_\theta)-E_0$ (best non-negative amplitudes under the NQS signs) and an amplitude part $\Delta E_{\mathrm{amp}}=E_\theta-E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s_\theta)$. The same optimal state $s_\theta a^\star$ gives the sign-limited pairing error. The split is bracketed from below by a stoquastic bound and calibrated on the exact signs. Sweeping NNBF width tells which part stops decreasing with capacity, which is the operational meaning of "binding constraint".
* **Sharpest Gap**: unverified assumption that the budget measured on $D\sim6\times10^5$ clusters transfers to the $4\times16$ to $8\times8$ sizes where P1 matters. Two specific risks: (i) at small $V$ a modest NNBF can represent nearly exact signs, so $f_E$ may be small here and grow with $V$ (the smoke run already shows $f_E$ falling with width on a 6-site cluster); (ii) $E_{\mathrm{stoq}}$ is loose (it gave $f_E^{\mathrm{lo}}=0$ in the smoke run), so there is no scalable, sign-only bound yet. A missing lemma that would close both: a bound on $E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s)-E_{\mathrm{stoq}}(s)$ in terms of the weight of sign-violating bonds, which would make $E_{\mathrm{stoq}}$ (computable by sign-free projector QMC at large $V$) a usable proxy.
### 4. 24-Hour Decisive Test & Compute Execution
* **Minimal Instance**: $4\times3$ square lattice, periodic in both directions ($V=12$, includes closed plaquette loops and diagonal $t'$ bonds, hence non-stoquastic and 2D), $t=1$, $t'=-0.25$. Three pre-committed instances: (0) $N_\uparrow=N_\downarrow=5$ (doping $\delta=1/6$), $U=8$, $D=627{,}264$; (1) $N_\uparrow=N_\downarrow=4$ ($\delta=1/3$), $U=8$, $D=245{,}025$; (2) $\delta=1/6$, $U=4$. States: UHF Slater; Jastrow–Slater foil (distance-resolved density Jastrow plus Gutzwiller factor, UHF-initialized orbitals trainable); NNBF (Luo & Clark form, one $\tanh$ hidden layer of width $H\in\{8,32,128\}$ producing configuration-dependent orbital shifts). All optimized by SR with the exact full-sum force and a 2000-sample metric (Woodbury solve), 1500 iterations.
* **Scalar Metric $M$**: everything decisive is evaluated on the NNBF with $H=128$. The sign-limited energy is bracketed by two rigorous bounds:
$$E_{\mathrm{stoq}}(s_\theta)\le E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s_\theta)\le E_{\mathrm{sign}}^{\mathrm{up}}(s_\theta)=\min\Big(\min_{k}E^{\mathrm{L\text{-}BFGS}}_{k},\ \langle H\rangle_{\mathrm{FN}},\ E_{\mathrm{FN}}\Big)\le E_{\mathrm{FN}}(s_\theta).$$
Here $E^{\mathrm{L\text{-}BFGS}}_{k}$ is the bound-constrained L-BFGS-B result from start $k\in\{\lvert\psi_\theta\rvert,\lvert\psi_0\rvert,\text{uniform},\lvert\phi_{\mathrm{FN}}\rvert\}$, and $E_{\mathrm{FN}}$ is the self-consistent ten Haaf lattice fixed-node energy for the sign $s_\theta$ (PRB 51, 13039, 1995): the sign-violating entries $(K_\theta)_{\mathbf n\mathbf n'}>0$ are deleted and moved to the diagonal as $(K_\theta)_{\mathbf n\mathbf n'}\,g_{\mathbf n'}/g_{\mathbf n}$, the Perron vector $\phi_{\mathrm{FN}}\ge0$ of the resulting stoquastic $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is fed back as the guide $g$ (start $g=1$, 10 iterations, floor $10^{-3}\max g$), and the lowest iterate is kept. Since $s_\theta\phi_{\mathrm{FN}}$ has sign $s_\theta$ and $\langle H_{\mathrm{eff}}-H\rangle\ge0$ on it (AM–GM on each deleted pair), $E_{\mathrm{FN}}\ge\langle H\rangle_{\mathrm{FN}}\ge E_{\mathrm{sign}}$. The reported fractions are
$$f_E^{\mathrm{up}}=\frac{E_{\mathrm{sign}}^{\mathrm{up}}-E_0}{E_\theta-E_0},\qquad f_E^{\mathrm{lo}}=\frac{\max(0,E_{\mathrm{stoq}}-E_0)}{E_\theta-E_0},\qquad f_E^{\mathrm{FN}}=\frac{E_{\mathrm{FN}}-E_0}{E_\theta-E_0},$$
together with the multi-start spread $\sigma=\big(\max_k E^{\mathrm{L\text{-}BFGS}}_{k}-\min_k E^{\mathrm{L\text{-}BFGS}}_{k}\big)/\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}$ over the starts that report convergence (all four if fewer than two converged). $M=f_E^{\mathrm{up}}$. The calibration error $\epsilon_{\mathrm{cal}}=E^{\mathrm{L\text{-}BFGS}}_{\text{uniform}}(s_0)-E_0$ is computed once per instance. Reported alongside (not decisive): $f_E^{\mathrm{lo}}$, $f_E^{\mathrm{FN}}$, $f_C$, $w_{\mathrm{sign}}$, swap energies, and $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}$, $\Delta E_{\mathrm{amp}}$ for every width. Hard-Core check (reporting only, no effect on the verdict): the lowest three levels of the fixed-$(N_\uparrow,N_\downarrow)$ sector, $\Delta_1/V=(E_1-E_0)/V$ in units of $t$, and the long-range $d$-wave pair correlation $P_d=\operatorname{mean}_{b:\,d(b,b_0)\ge r_{\min}}\eta_bP(b_0,b)$ ($\eta_b=+1$ on $x$-bonds, $-1$ on $y$-bonds) of the $E_0$ and $E_1$ eigenvectors; the run prints "near-degenerate competitor present" if $\Delta_1/V<0.02\,t$.

## Route Register
### R1 — Route 1: sign-constrained optimal-amplitude budget with a copositive bracket (LIVE)
* **The idea**: Split the energy error of a trained network into a sign part and an amplitude part by a variational question: keeping the network's signs $s_\theta(\mathbf n)=\mathrm{sgn}\,\psi_\theta(\mathbf n)$ fixed, what is the lowest energy $E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s_\theta)$ that any non-negative amplitude vector can reach? Then $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}=E_{\mathrm{sign}}-E_0$ and $\Delta E_{\mathrm{amp}}=E_\theta-E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ ($E_0$ the exact ground energy, $E_\theta$ the network's energy) are both non-negative, add up to the total error $\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}$, and do not depend on ordering or gauge. The inner minimisation is co-NP-hard (a copositivity problem), so it is bracketed: the best of four bound-constrained L-BFGS-B starts and a self-consistent ten Haaf lattice fixed-node state gives a rigorous upper bound $f_E^{\mathrm{up}}$ on the sign fraction $f_E=\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}/\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}$; the lowest eigenvalue of the sign-gauged Hamiltonian with its positive off-diagonals deleted (the stoquastic bound) gives a rigorous lower bound; and an exact-sign calibration must return $E_0$. Sweeping the width $H$ of the neural-network backflow (NNBF) shows which part stops shrinking with capacity, which is the operational meaning of "binding". It is a diagnostic, not a competing ansatz: the Minimal Contrast Foil (Jastrow–Slater VMC) is scored by the same budget in the same run.
* **Lineage**: initial batch (2026-11-10). Patched after the round-1 critic (multi-start with a spread guard, calibration rule, width tie-break, competitor report). Its decisive test (329057) was re-run with symmetry projection as leaf P1.1-P (407525), and its budget code is reused by P1.3 and P2.1.
* **Outcome**: LIVE (T3 reached, job 329057, 2026-11-11). Verdict PASS under the pre-registered rule ($f_E^{\mathrm{up}}=0.607$, 0.772 and 0.652 for NNBF at $H=128$ on the three $4\times3$ tori, rule $\ge0.6$ with spread $<0.05$; spread at most $5\times10^{-13}$, calibration error at most $6\times10^{-13}\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}$). The measurement tool stands; the reading "sign is binding" was superseded by P1.1-P as symmetry contamination of unprojected networks.

### R2 — Route 2: hybrid sign/amplitude swap with a Shapley split (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Measure the sign and amplitude shares of the error by swapping parts with the exact ground state $\psi_0$: build $s_\theta\lvert\psi_0\rvert$ (network signs on exact magnitudes) and $s_0\lvert\psi_\theta\rvert$ (exact signs $s_0$ on network magnitudes), evaluate their energies, and split $\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}$ by the two-player Shapley average of the gain from making each part exact. It was expected to work because it is the established spin-model practice and costs two energy evaluations.
* **Lineage**: initial batch (2026-11-10). Its two swap energies were kept as cross-check outputs of R1.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-10).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0: "the idea is known; is the reason it is not standard practice for this purpose fixed?" The adoption barrier named at the gate: the trained magnitudes are co-adapted to the trained signs, so a swapped hybrid can have a higher energy than the trained state, which makes a share negative and the split order-dependent.
* **Evidence**: Prior use in spin NQS (Westerhout et al., Nat. Commun. 11, 1593, 2020; arXiv:2308.09664); R2 proposed no fix for the barrier. The swap energies later printed by R1's test (329057, seed 1234, read 2026-11-17) confirm the barrier on every state: on all 15 states (UHF, foil, NNBF at three widths, on three tori) both hybrids lie above $E_\theta$. On $5+5$, $U=8t$, width 128: $\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}=0.166t$, but $E[s_0\lvert\psi_\theta\rvert]-E_0=0.352t$ and $E[s_\theta\lvert\psi_0\rvert]-E_0=1.324t$, so the Shapley shares are sign $+0.569t$ and amplitude $-0.403t$. The amplitude share is negative for all 12 foil and NNBF states (from $-0.075t$ to $-0.977t$); only the 3 UHF states give two positive shares.
* **Why this kills it**: A budget whose amplitude share is negative, and whose sign share exceeds the whole error (0.569 against 0.166), is not a fraction of anything; it measures how incompatible the two trained halves are with the exact other half, not how much error each carries. With no mechanism to remove the co-adaptation, the route re-labels existing practice (Unacceptable Outcome 5).
* **What the kill does not rule out**: swap energies as qualitative cross-checks; a swap in which the free half is re-optimised after the swap, which for the sign side is exactly R1.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: the Shapley split is a convention, and it might still rank states in the same order as R1 even with negative shares. Unresolved: the rank agreement was not checked. Not raised by a critic.
* **Checked by**: per-leaf critic, 2026-11-17: upheld; Shapley arithmetic verified; negative shares on 12/12 trained states.
* **Lesson**: L31 (`library.md`)

### R3 — Route 3: sign-mismatch weight as the budget (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Use the ground-state weight on configurations whose network sign is wrong, $w_{\mathrm{sign}}=\sum_{\mathbf n:\,s_\theta s_0=-1}\lvert\psi_0(\mathbf n)\rvert^2$, or the sign overlap, as the measure of how much error the signs carry. It was expected to work because it is cheap, needs only the exact state, and is the sign-accuracy score used for spin NQS; an energy-weighted version for fermions would have been new.
* **Lineage**: initial batch (2026-11-10). $w_{\mathrm{sign}}$ is still reported by R1's test as a non-decisive output.
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G4 (2026-11-10; recoded from G1 by the per-leaf critic, 2026-11-17, because the saturation is a size-scaling argument).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 4: "does its gain survive growing system size?", here the behaviour of the metric with system size $V$.
* **Evidence**: An orthogonality-catastrophe argument: for any ansatz with a fixed per-region sign accuracy, the probability that the whole sign pattern is right over $V$ weakly correlated regions decays as $e^{-cV}$, so $w_{\mathrm{sign}}$ approaches its saturation value exponentially and stops discriminating states. It is also not an energy: a sign error between configurations joined only by small $\lvert H_{\mathbf n\mathbf n'}\rvert$ costs almost nothing. R1's data (329057, read 2026-11-17) show the second point: at width 128, $w_{\mathrm{sign}}$ is 0.197, 0.029 and 0.0063 on the three tori (a 31-fold range), while $f_E^{\mathrm{up}}$ is 0.77, 0.61 and 0.65; and on $5+5$, $U=8t$, widening from 8 to 128 cuts $w_{\mathrm{sign}}$ 3.3 times but $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ only 1.6 times.
* **Why this kills it**: The leaf's question is "what fraction of $\Delta E_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is due to signs"; a quantity that saturates with $V$ and does not track energy cannot give that fraction at any size, and its ordering across instances disagrees with the energy budget.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: $w_{\mathrm{sign}}$ as a descriptive companion number at fixed small $V$; a bond-resolved, energy-weighted sign error (the sum of $\lvert H_{\mathbf n\mathbf n'}\psi_0(\mathbf n)\psi_0(\mathbf n')\rvert$ over wrong-sign bonds), which neither argument kills.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: at ED size ($V\le16$) the saturation has not set in, so the metric could still be informative here. Answer: the energy mismatch shown above holds at $V=12$ already. Not raised by a critic.
* **Checked by**: per-leaf critic, 2026-11-17: upheld, code -> G4 (saturation is a size-scaling argument); at-size evidence is the energy mismatch.
* **Lesson**: L32 (`library.md`)

### R4 — Route 4: lattice fixed-node GFMC with the network as guide, as a scalable budget (KILLED)
* **The idea**: Reach sizes beyond ED by running lattice fixed-node Green's-function Monte Carlo (GFMC) from the trained network, reading $E_\theta-E_{\mathrm{FN}}$ as the amplitude part and $E_{\mathrm{FN}}-E_{\mathrm{ref}}$ as the sign part, with $E_{\mathrm{ref}}$ from DMRG or AFQMC. It was expected to work because continuum fixed-node DMC gives exactly the best energy at fixed nodes and scales polynomially.
* **Lineage**: initial batch (2026-11-10); the ten Haaf fixed-node state was repaired into R1 as one of its upper-bound starts.
* **Source analogy**: continuum fixed-node diffusion Monte Carlo (Reynolds, Ceperley, Alder & Lester, J. Chem. Phys. 77, 5593, 1982): nodal surface ↔ sign pattern, fixed-node energy ↔ $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$; it works there because the nodal wall decouples nodal pockets in continuous space; the translation gap is that lattice hops cross the "nodes" directly (section 1b, Barrier A).
* **Outcome**: KILLED at G0 (2026-11-10).
* **Kill rule**: Gate 0: "the idea is known; is the reason it is not standard practice for this purpose fixed?" The barrier: the lattice fixed-node energy is not a sign-only quantity.
* **Evidence**: DMC on top of NNBF already exists (Loehr & Clark, arXiv:2510.26906). On a lattice the ten Haaf sign-flip potential $\sum_{\mathbf n'}H_{\mathbf n\mathbf n'}\psi_\theta(\mathbf n')/\psi_\theta(\mathbf n)$ over sign-violating pairs depends on the guide's amplitudes (ten Haaf et al., PRB 51, 13039, 1995), so $E_{\mathrm{FN}}$ is only an upper bound on $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$. R1's runs measured the gap: fixed-node sign fraction $f_E^{\mathrm{FN}}$ 0.665, 0.827 and 0.756 against $f_E^{\mathrm{up}}$ 0.607, 0.772 and 0.652 at width 128 (329057), and 0.845 against 0.141 on the 6-site smoke cluster.
* **Why this kills it**: $E_{\mathrm{FN}}-E_{\mathrm{ref}}$ contains an amplitude-dependent term, so the proposed split mixes the two error sources it is meant to separate, and the route has no mechanism to remove that dependence; at sizes beyond ED there is also no way to tell how large the mixing is.
* **What the kill does not rule out**: fixed-node GFMC as an energy improvement (Loehr & Clark), and the fixed-node energy as a rigorous upper bound on $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$, which R1 uses.
* **Strongest case against the kill**: the fixed-node gap was only 0.05 to 0.10 in sign fraction on the tori, so a loose upper bound might still answer a coarse binding question at large size. Unresolved: the smoke cluster shows the gap can be a factor of six, and no bound on it is known. Not raised by a critic.
* **Checked by**: per-leaf critic, 2026-11-17: upheld; amplitude dependence of the lattice FN term is standard (ten Haaf 1995), consistent with f_E^FN ≥ f_E^up in all data.
* **Lesson**: L32 (`library.md`)

**The question.** A neural quantum state (NQS) is a network output $\psi_\theta(\mathbf n)$ over occupation configurations $\mathbf n$; for fermions its signs $s_\theta(\mathbf n)=\mathrm{sgn}\,\psi_\theta(\mathbf n)$ or its magnitudes can be wrong. Branch P1 of this brainstorm (sign layers, P1.2; wrong-sign basins, P1.3) is worth pursuing only if the signs limit accuracy. This leaf measures that for neural-network backflow (NNBF: a Slater determinant whose orbitals are shifted by a one-hidden-layer network that reads the configuration) on doped two-dimensional clusters small enough for exact diagonalization (ED).

**The tool (route R1).** With $E_0$ the exact ground energy, $E_\theta$ the network's, and $E_{\mathrm{sign}}(s_\theta)$ the lowest energy any non-negative amplitude vector reaches when forced to carry the network's signs, the sign part $\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}=E_{\mathrm{sign}}-E_0$ and the amplitude part $\Delta E_{\mathrm{amp}}=E_\theta-E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ are both non-negative, add up to the total error, and do not depend on gauge or ordering. The sign fraction is $f_E=\Delta E_{\mathrm{sign}}/(E_\theta-E_0)$; the same optimal-amplitude state gives $f_C$, the share of the long-range $d$-wave bond-pair correlation error carried by the signs. Computing $E_{\mathrm{sign}}$ exactly is co-NP-hard, so the test reports a rigorous upper bound $f_E^{\mathrm{up}}$ (best of four L-BFGS-B starts and a ten Haaf fixed-node state), checked by a calibration that must recover $E_0$ from the exact signs. Because the bound is an upper bound, a small $f_E^{\mathrm{up}}$ certifies a small sign share without trusting the optimiser.
